# 0031 The default rule and the record of what left: how they are built

## Context

ADR 0019 promised two things that its order of work left to steps 7 and 8: every new account has a rule, turned on, that holds back sign-in and security mail; and every message that leaves writes a receipt, sealed, kept for as long as the account, later chained by hash with its head anchored. The owner decided on 2026-11-16 (ADR 0030, the owner's decision 1) that connections open to every invitee at once, so both must exist the day they do: the rule holding sign-in codes and password resets for every account, by exact conditions and by `about`, failing toward holding as rules do; and a sealed record of every message that leaves (which connection, which parts, by which query or search, what was held, whether a model decided), refusals too. Both must be shaped so that step 7 (the person's own rules) and step 8 (receipts chained and anchored, the activity screen) extend them without migration where possible.

ADR 0028 left a prerequisite for step 7 (docs/plan.md): before any rule that protects relies on a kept answer of the model, rule-mode answers must be rollback resistant, failing closed when a prior decision cannot be recovered; whoever writes the table can delete or put back a kept answer, which forces a new ask, and for a protecting rule a new ask after a silent change of the model could release a message that had been held. The default rule is such a rule, so the prerequisite is met here.

## Decision in short

- **One rule, compiled into the image, the same for every account**: `rules.Default()` (`default-sign-in-and-security`, version 1), three formulas in `internal/rules/default.json`: the subject contains, and the text contains, any of 21 phrases in English and Spanish (verification, security, sign-in, login and one-time codes, passcodes, two-factor, password resets); and one formula a model decides, `message about` a sign-in or verification code or a one-time password, or resetting a password or recovering an account. It applies when any formula is True or Unknown (`query.ForRule`): every message a connection would read that the exact phrases do not catch is held until the model answers below 0.30 for both topics. ("The default rule")
- **Failing toward holding, plainly** (the owner's decision 9): with no model key or a spend limit reached, nothing leaves at all: a connection's searches, reads and attachments answer `model_unavailable` until the model can be asked, decisions already made included, and the consent screen, the API and MCP, the connection's page and `/status` say so. With the provider failing or an answer in doubt, the messages it would decide are held. ("When the model cannot answer")
- **Holds that cannot be undone**: a rule's value that would rest on the model is a decision, begun (`pending`) in a block of the strict anchored records of the mailbox's day (`rule-decisions-<mailbox>-<YYYYMMDD>-<block>`, the day in the message's identifier, a new block opened when none has room, the owner's decision 12) before the model is asked anything for it or a kept answer is used, and settled `held` or `cleared` after, with the model's answers it rests on kept in it; the first settlement is final, and only a committed `cleared` lets a message out. A begun decision is dropped only on positive evidence that no answer of the model was received for it; one whose settlement was lost, or whose answer was received and could not be confirmed kept, is settled `held` (recovered) by whoever finds it two minutes later. Every read of it is strict and made now. A kept answer deleted or put back can then never change a decision once begun, which is ADR 0028's prerequisite met. ("Holds that cannot be undone")
- **The record of what left**: one sealed entry per request of a connection that reads mail or is refused, and per consent, written once (at version 0) before the answer, in the account's partition `receipts#<account>`, keyed by a time-ordered identifier, at most 256 KiB of JSON (a search's page ends before the message that would pass it, and its cursor continues from there), kept for as long as the account, deleted with it. It names the connection and device, the vendor, every formula by role and canonical identifier (the vendor's search and a consent's formulas in full), the rules and their versions, each message made available with its parts and, of those, its senders and subject, each message held and how, and the model's answers a decision rested on, as the decision's own record keeps them, whichever request made it. A request whose receipt cannot be written gives nothing. ("The record of what left")
- **Steps 7 and 8 extend them without migration**: the person's rules are a strict anchored record beside the compiled default, which says whether the default is off, and use the same decisions; receipts are immutable entries keyed by time, which step 8 chains through a fenced cutover, nothing rewritten. Until step 8, whoever writes the table can delete a receipt unseen: the stated limit. ("How steps 7 and 8 extend them", "Deferred")

## The default rule

### What it is

`internal/rules/default.json`, read and made canonical by `rules.Default()` and pinned by its canonical identifiers (`TestTheDefaultRuleIsWhatADR0031Says`):

| Formula | Canonical identifier | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1, exact | `qry_ghi2xjhazzz25rgsy6arrif4ke` | The subject contains any of the phrases |
| 2, exact | `qry_dq33uknaplrglzyuws6gcb2uje` | The text (as a connection receives it) contains any of the phrases |
| 3, a model decides | `qry_lh52gob7oeycyjrkqg2lpn2lje` | `message about "a sign-in, login or verification code, or a one-time password"`, or `message about "resetting a password or recovering an account"` |

The phrases, matched as substrings after ADR 0027's normalisation (case, width and invisible characters ignored): "verification code", "security code", "sign-in code", "sign in code", "login code", "log-in code", "one-time code", "one-time password", "one time password", "passcode", "authentication code", "two-factor", "2-step verification", "password reset", "reset your password", "reset password", "código de verificación", "código de seguridad", "código de acceso", "restablecer tu contraseña", "restablecer la contraseña". English is the product's language; Spanish is the language of the first mailboxes' mail; the model's formula covers the rest (an English topic on Spanish mail scored 0.984 in ADR 0028's spike). The list can grow, as a new version.

A rule is one or more formulas and applies when one of them does (ADR 0027, "Rules: any of their criteria"), each read for a rule: True or Unknown applies. Formulas 1 and 2 are exact and settle most security mail without a model; formula 3 is asked only of what they leave. Its two topics are two atoms, asked in one call with the subject and the whole text (ADR 0028), and kept per message like every answer, so a message asked about once is not asked again for another connection, a preview or the next page.

### Where it applies

To every read of every connection, before the connection's formulas (ADR 0030, "What a read gives"): a message it applies to is held, not given, and the vendor is told nothing, a held message being `not_found` like one that does not exist. It applies to the consent screen's preview with the model (`MatchPage.Held`), and not to the owner's own previews of a formula or to the inbox: the rule is about what leaves.

It is on for every account and cannot be turned off in step 5: the switch ADR 0019 promises comes with step 7's rules screen.

### When the model cannot answer

Formula 3 is Unknown until the model answers, and Unknown applies a rule. So when the provider does not answer, an answer falls between 0.30 and 0.70, the model refuses the statement, or a message's text cannot be read, the messages formulas 1 and 2 do not catch are held: a phrase that does not match leaves the value Unknown, not False.

**The model off: nothing leaves** (the owner's decision 9, confirmed as designed). When the model cannot be asked for the account at all (no key set or the provider refused it, the month's spend, the provider's limit or the account's day share reached: `connections.ModelGate` answers other than `AskAnswered`), the connections service answers every search, read and attachment of the account's connections `503 model_unavailable` before anything is evaluated, and nothing leaves, not even a message whose decision is already a committed `cleared`: the simplest rule, the one that fails closed, and the owner's words. The default rule holding on Unknown would otherwise give a page of only already-cleared mail, which says to a vendor which mail was decided before; the 503 says only that the model is unavailable. Where it is said: the consent screen before Allow ("If Permaily cannot ask its model, this connection gets no mail until it can"), the API's and MCP's error (`model_unavailable`, with words), `GET /v1/connection` and `connection_info` (`delivery: model_unavailable`, never why or how much waits), the connection's page (`delivery`), and `/status` (`connections.model_unavailable: "nothing_leaves"` beside ADR 0028's guard); the alarm tells the operator. A guard that changes during a page holds the page's remaining model-dependent messages, undecided. When the model is back, the next request decides them. Each message the model is asked about costs about $0.00013 (ADR 0028's measure), within the month's 50 USD and the account's 0.50 USD a day an enclave; a connection that reads more than that gets `model_unavailable` until the next day.

## Holds that cannot be undone

### The problem

Answers are kept per message (ADR 0028), and the first kept for a key is the one every enclave uses. Whoever writes the table cannot write one, but can delete one or put back an earlier copy of the entry, which loses the answers added since. The next evaluation then asks again. For a query that is harmless; for the default rule it means that a message held on a kept 0.90 can be asked again, and if the model's behaviour changed under its name in between (which the canary samples and cannot exclude), come back 0.10 and leave. Not keeping rule answers at all would not help: every ask would then be a fresh one, and a held message would be released the same way by the first fresh ask after a change. Committing a hold after it is decided (this design before its review) leaves a window: a crash, or a commit refused at every try, between the decision and its commit, then the kept answer removed, and a later ask releases the message (finding 1). What has to hold is that **a rule's decision, once the model may have been asked for it, is never undone by the storage, and a decision whose outcome was lost fails closed**.

### Decisions, begun before anything is asked

A protecting rule's value on a message that rests on the model (its exact formulas False, its model's formula needed) is a **decision**, `(message, rule, version)`, kept in a block of the strict anchored records of the mailbox's day (ADR 0016, ADR 0026): `rule-decisions-<mailbox>-<YYYYMMDD>-<block>`, the day (UTC) in the message's identifier, so which day holds it cannot be changed by writing to the table, and a block with no record is one never written, which a strict read tells from one lost. **A message has one block** (revised after the third review, R1): the day's blocks are a chain from block 0, every block but the last written `next`, and only the last, the **tail**, ever takes a new decision; when the tail has no room or versions for one, it is written `next` and its successor made with `CreateNew` (a crash between the two leaves a `next` whose block a strict read finds never written, which the next writer makes). Opening the successor is itself a write of the tail at the version read, so every new decision of a day is written to one record at a time, under that record's version: of two requests that both read the day without a message, both write the tail, and the second was read before the first wrote, so it is refused (`store.ErrChanged`), reads again and finds the decision. Room an earlier block regains (its decisions expired or abandoned) is never used for a new one. A decision stays in the block that began it, and every block of the day is read before one is begun, so a decision is found wherever it is; should a message ever be found in two blocks (no transition writes it so), its most protective decision is answered (`held`, then `pending`, then `cleared`) and neither is written. Each block is sealed under a key from the root secret, made with `CreateNew` and changed with `PutRecordAt` at the version read (all of it read again on `store.ErrChanged`, at most five times). Its document and transitions are `inbox.DecisionRecord` and `inbox.DecisionBlocks` (`Begin`, `Settle`), and **the one implementation** of the seam `inbox.Decisions` is `inbox.BlockDecisions`, over an `inbox.DecisionStore` that reads a block strictly and writes it at its mark; all three are committed with this design and its tests. Pull request 4 gives it services/mailboxes' sealed strict records; `disclosetest` gives it memory (`Records`, each block its own record changed only at the version read, every block checked against the item's 400 KiB) and unsealed strict records (`Anchored`, `Enclaves`), and has the contract every store passes, with the interleaved schedules of two enclaves.

1. **Begin.** Before the model is asked anything for a message's rule, or a kept answer is used for it, the discloser calls `Begin` for the page's messages that need it (one strict read, made now, of each block of each day the page touches, and one write per block that changes). Each is answered: `begun` (this call wrote it `pending`: the discloser may ask), `pending` (another request began it), `held` or `cleared` (final, with the evidence it rests on, or `recovered`: used as it is, the model not asked), `full` (the day has no room: held, undecided), or `expired` (its message has expired by this enclave's clock: nothing is stored, nothing leaves, and it is not counted as `full`). A block that cannot be read or written holds every message of its day, undecided.
2. **Ask and settle** (revised after the second review, N1 and N4, and the third, R2). For the messages it began, the discloser asks through the same `inbox.Asker` as queries (kept answers shared), then settles each as `inbox.SettleAfterAsk` says, in one write per block: `held` when the rule applies on what the model said (True; or Unknown, which applies a rule, with any answer of the rule's atoms received: an answer between 0.30 and 0.70, a kept refusal, which `inbox.Evidence.Refused` tells from a failure, or any answer beside an atom left without one: **an answer received is never forgotten**, R2), `cleared` when it does not (False on the model's kept answers), each **with the evidence it rests on** (`inbox.Settlement.Evidence`, the `Asked.Evidence` of the rule's atoms, kept in the block with the decision, at most `DecisionEvidenceMax` answers and `DecisionEvidenceBytesMax` bytes; a settlement past them is refused and the decision stays `pending`, never kept without its evidence). With no answer received, but one the Asker received and could not confirm kept (`inbox.Asked.Unsettled`: `Keep` failed, wrote and then failed to confirm, as the second review reproduced, or kept another answer first), the decision is left `pending`, the message held, undecided, for this request, and it is recovered `held` after the grace (N1). It is `abandoned`, which drops the pending decision so a later request decides it, **only on positive evidence that the model answered no part of it**: no atom of the rule has evidence or is unsettled, because the guard stopped the ask before it started or every call failed without an answer, or the Asker failed (it fails only before asking anything, by its contract). The cost, stated: a message for which the model answered one atom and not the other is held for that rule's version, even when the answer it gave was low. `Settle` answers each final state as it is after the write; only a message answered `cleared` may leave, and one answered `held` where `cleared` was asked (another request settled it first) is held. **A settlement changes only its own attempt** (revised after the fourth review, F1): `Begin` answers each `begun` decision with its attempt (`inbox.Attempt`: the block and the version of it its begin wrote, which no other write of that block shares), kept with the `pending`, and the discloser gives it back in the settlement; `abandoned` and `cleared` change only a `pending` of that attempt, and one that finds another's (a later request's, begun after this one's was dropped) changes nothing and is answered `pending`. A settlement whose later block is refused keeps what its earlier blocks committed and settles only the rest again, read afresh, so neither its retry nor a settlement given again after a write of unknown outcome can drop or clear another request's `pending`.
3. **Recovery.** A `pending` decision older than `inbox.PendingGrace` (two minutes, longer than one message's ask and its settlement's tries) lost its settlement: a crash, every write refused, or an answer received and not kept. Whoever finds it settles it `held`, marked `recovered` and with no evidence (`inbox.Decision.Recovered`, `HeldRecovered`): it rests on no answer that can be recovered from a table anyone could have changed, and says so instead of naming one. Its late settlement, if one ever comes, loses: it fails closed. A younger `pending` is held for this request, undecided, and left for its owner.
4. **What is never committed.** A hold by an exact formula (formulas 1 and 2 True, or Unknown on a text that cannot be read): it is read from the sealed meta and entry, which cannot be changed, and holds without the model. And nothing is begun while the model cannot be asked: the request is refused first (decision 9).

**Reads are strict and made now** (finding 2): `Begin` never answers an absence or a `pending` from an earlier read; a final decision may be cached, with its evidence, since it never changes. So a message whose hold another enclave has committed is never released on a cached absence and an older kept answer put back.

**Evidence that survives** (N4). A final decision keeps, in its block, the model's answers it rests on (atom, probability or refusal, model, prompt version, when, the canary's days; never the content's hash), and every later `Begin` answers them with it. So a decision settled by a consent preview, by a request that crashed before its receipt, or through the other enclave, is recorded in a later vendor's receipt with what it rested on, whatever happened since to the kept answers; nothing is asked again to fill it in, and a recovered one says `recovered`.

**Bounds** (finding 11; revised after the second review, N4, the owner's decision 12, and the third review, R3). A block holds at most `inbox.DecisionsMax` (500) decisions and `inbox.DecisionsBytesMax` (300 KiB of JSON before sealing, binary-sealed, within the 400 KiB item), every `pending` counted at its largest settlement (its own size plus `DecisionEvidenceBytesMax`, 2 KiB), so a settlement always fits; and at most `inbox.DecisionsVersionsMax` (9,000) versions, below the strict read's 10,000, with one kept for the settlement of every `pending` and one for writing the block `next`, so a begun decision can always be settled and the tail can always open its successor (a `held` stored for a message with nothing begun keeps the same reserve). Each begin or settle of a page is one version of each block it changes; pruning expired decisions happens only in a write that is made anyway (a begin's own included), never as a write of its own. **A tail out of decisions, bytes or versions opens its successor**, whatever it holds (R3): a message past its own expiry is answered `expired` and set apart first, so a refusal for room or history is never taken for a message with nothing left to decide, and an empty block whose versions were spent by begun and abandoned attempts opens the next as any full one does. **A tail is not retired for what its live `pending` keep** (revised after the fourth review, F2): when it is out of bytes only because its `pending` younger than `PendingGrace` are counted at their largest settlement, it waits, and a decision it cannot take now is `full` for this request (held, undecided) and begun in the same tail by a later request once those settle; it opens its successor only when it is out of decisions or versions, or out of bytes even with its live `pending` counted at their present size (an overdue one still at its largest, since its settlement may never come), so a lost settlement holds the tail two minutes at most. A day has at most `inbox.DecisionBlocksMax` (64) blocks. A settled decision of the default rule with its two answers is about 580 bytes, so 500 fit a block's bytes and a tail filled by pages of 50 is retired at its 500 decisions: a day holds at least 28,800 (`decisions_per_day` on `/status`; 32,000 with sequential pages, H30); even with every decision pending at its largest at once, a block takes about 140 and a day about 9,000. That is far above any real mailbox (a person's mail is tens of messages a day): the owner's decision 12, which asked for no practical daily cap. **Overflow fails closed and is visible**: past the day's last block, a new decision is `full` and its message held, undecided, named so in its receipt (`undecided`), counted on `/status` (`decisions_full`; `expired` is not counted there) and logged by that kind. **A filled day recovers** as its last block's decisions expire with their messages: each is dropped by the next write of its block an hour after its message (`DecisionKeptAfter`), and a begin that finds no room in the tail first prunes it and then begins, so the day takes new decisions again as soon as some of those messages are gone; the room earlier blocks regain is not used (R1). Versions never come back, since a commit is history: a block whose versions are spent takes no decision again, and the day goes on in its successor. The worst case is a day's 64 blocks spent by about 288,000 begun and abandoned attempts (two versions each, a provider failing every page of a mailbox for days): the day is then `full`, held and undecided, until its messages are gone; nothing undecided is ever released meanwhile. The bound is computed from the blocks each time, never remembered by a writer that failed.

**How long.** A decision is kept an hour past its message's expiry (`inbox.DecisionKeptAfter`, far longer than two enclaves' clocks differ, so an enclave whose clock is behind never finds a message still there and its decision gone; `Begin` answers `expired` for a message past its own) and dropped from later versions after it; the mailbox's removal (disconnecting it, deleting the account) removes its records with `RemoveRecord`, by walking its days from the mailbox's creation to its removal and each day's blocks by their `next`, not an index.

**Why a day, in blocks.** A page reads newest first, so it touches one or two days, and nearly always one block of each: one or two strict reads and up to four writes a page. A month would put a busy mailbox past the bounds within days; a record per message would cost a strict record per message; an hour, which the owner's decision 12 offered, would cost a strict read per hour a page spans, up to fifty for a quiet mailbox. Blocks keep the common day to one record and give a busy day as many as it needs, up to 64.

**What it guarantees.** Whatever whoever writes the table does to kept answers, and however two enclaves interleave, a message whose rule's decision was begun is released only on a committed `cleared`, and one decided `held` stays held for as long as the message exists: deleting or putting back the answer changes nothing once begun; putting back an earlier record of decisions is refused, and hiding one (a fork) holds every model-dependent message of that day; a second request's decision for the same message is refused by the tail's version (R1); a retry reads afresh, finds what the first try wrote and never gives again what it committed, and a settlement drops or clears only the attempt that began it (F1); a received answer is never abandoned (R2); and a full or spent block hands its day to the next rather than releasing anything (R3). What it does not cover: a message never asked before is decided by its first answer, which is the model's and nobody else's; and a decision begun and abandoned (on positive evidence that the model answered no part of it) is decided again later, which is not a decision undone, since none was made. ADR 0028's prerequisite asked for a commitment that tells a decision never made from one lost, failing closed: this is it.

**Step 7** uses it for every rule that protects, the person's own included; nothing in it is the default rule's. The consent screen's preview with the model uses the same discloser, so a decision shown there is the one a vendor meets, with the same evidence.

## The record of what left

### What a receipt is

An entry of the account's partition `receipts#<account>`, keyed by its identifier (`rcp_` and a time-ordered identifier, the time first, so the keys' order is the time's), its value sealed (`envelope.SealText`, `permaily receipts v1`, a key from the root secret) and bound to its partition and key, written with `PutEntry` at version 0 (never updated), with no expiry. Its document (`receipts.Receipt`, version 1) is at most `receipts.MaxDocumentBytes` (256 KiB of JSON; sealed as text it is about 342 KiB, within the 400 KiB item with its other attributes): `Write` refuses a larger one rather than cut it (finding 10). One message's part of a receipt is bounded by what a stored message may hold (a From field of 16 KiB with 100 addresses, a subject of 2 KiB, ADR 0026), which fits, with 50 held messages and their model evidence, at the worst JSON escaping (`TestTheLargestMessageFitsOneReceipt`); so a search's receipt is built message by message, counting the whole document as it will be written (every field, the escaping, the formulas in full, held and cleared evidence alike), and its page ends before the message that would pass the bound, the vendor's cursor continuing from the last message recorded: nothing is skipped, nothing recorded is cut, and no evidence is dropped. The 256 KiB is the designer's choice (the second review, ruling 4); a single message always fits, so no separate representation is needed.

| Kind | When | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `search` | A page of a search answered | The connection, device, client, vendor, resource; every formula by role (`consented`, `mailboxes`, `since`, `search`) and canonical identifier, the search's canonical form in full; the rules and versions; each message made available (mailbox, identifier, when it arrived, the parts given, and of those its senders and subject, and the rules decided `cleared` for it with when); each message held (mailbox, identifier, rule and version, how: `exact`, `decided`, `recovered` or `undecided`); the model's answers each decision rested on, as its record keeps them (`inbox.Decision.Evidence`: atom, probability or refusal, model, prompt version, the canary's last check and last change, ADR 0028; never the content's hash), whichever request made it |
| `read` | One message read whole | The same for the one message; never its text |
| `attachment` | One attachment given | The message, the attachment's index, and its decision's evidence as for a read |
| `refused` | A read or a search that gave none: a held message (with how and its evidence, an attachment's included), one outside the scope (or not there), an attachment without the part or too large, a formula refused or looking at a part not given, the model unavailable; or an answer recorded and then not released (`too_late`, naming the earlier receipt) | The reason; the message's identifier when one was asked |
| `consent` | The person allowed or refused a request, added a device, or renewed a connection | What the screen showed: the sentences, parts, mail since, end, mailboxes, every consented formula in full, the model's notice's version, what it did to the client's connection (new, a device added, replaced) and what it replaced; the decision |
| `ended` | A connection, or one device of it, ended | Why, and what replaced it |

**What a receipt proves.** It is written before the answer starts, and records what was prepared, authorized and made available to the vendor's request; it does not prove the vendor's side received it (a client that disconnects, or a cut transfer, still has its receipt), and the activity screen of step 8 says "made available", never "received". An answer recorded and then not released (ADR 0030, "Release") gets a second, `too_late`, receipt; the first stays: a receipt may record more than left, never less. Only requests attributed to a connection by a valid token are recorded (a request refused before that is counted on `/status` and logged by kind, never written to an account guessed from what it sent); once a grant is attributed, every refusal of a request that asked for mail is recorded, and the vendor is told nothing that distinguishes them.

What left is recorded; what did not leave is recorded by identifier only. A receipt keeps a message's senders and subject only when those parts left with it, so the person can still see, once the message is deleted, what a vendor got; never its text or an attachment's content, which would make the record a second copy of the mail. A request refused before a connection is known (no token, a token not valid) has no account to write to: it is counted on `/status` and logged by kind.

### When a receipt is written

Before the answer, and the answer is given only once it is stored: a request whose receipt cannot be written is answered 503 and gives nothing (`receipts.Writer`). One receipt per request, holding every message of it (at most 50 for a search): one write per request, never one per message. Two enclaves write receipts of one account at once without coordinating: each is a new entry, and the time-ordered identifier with 10 random bytes never collides.

### What it guarantees, and until when

Whoever writes the table cannot read a receipt, change one, or make one. Until step 8 they can delete one, or the whole partition, unseen; putting back a deleted one changes nothing (it is the same receipt). The receipts outlive the mail: deleting a message or disconnecting a mailbox leaves them; removing the account deletes the partition (`DeletePartition`), as its other entries are, and accounts' removal sweep deletes it once more `receipts.RemoveAgainAfter` (ten minutes) later, for a receipt whose write began before the removal and ended after it (no request lasts that long), so no orphan partition is left (choice 32).

## How steps 7 and 8 extend them

- **Step 7, rules.** The person's rules are a strict anchored record of the account (`rules-<account>`, made at the first rule), each a `rules.Rule` with an identifier of its own and its version, beside the compiled default; that record also says whether the default is off, so an account without one has the default on, which is every account of step 5: nothing to migrate. Rules for some connections only are a field of each rule. Removing details is a new effect and a new view of the text (ADR 0028's `TextView`). `Decisions` serves every rule that protects. A new version of `default.json` is a new `DefaultVersion`, whose decisions are made afresh; receipts name the version that applied.
- **Step 8, receipts.** Step 5's receipts are immutable entries, keyed by time; step 8 does not get them for free as a chain. Keys' order across two enclaves is not append order (an earlier key can commit after a later one was read), so step 8 must define a fenced cutover: a point after which every receipt is appended through the head's anchored record, and a manifest of what existed before it (the entries listed after every writer of the old kind has stopped, hashed in key order), never a hash of sorted pages while writes continue. Receipts deleted before step 8 cannot be recovered by chaining later: the accepted gap until then. The activity screen and the export read the same entries; the receipt's fields are what the sample's activity screen (`web/src/pages/activity`) shows.

## Deferred

Turning the default off, the person's own rules, rules for some connections, removing details (step 7); chaining, anchoring the head, the activity screen, the export with CSV guarded against formula injection (step 8). The default rule's phrases in more languages, as the product gains them.

## Options considered

- **No model in the default rule** (phrases only): no spend and no holding when the model is off; but a code in a message that says "Here is your number: 482913" leaves. The owner chose exact conditions and `about`.
- **The phrases as a model's `mentions`**: one atom fewer, but every message would need the model even for the plainest "Your verification code".
- **Not keeping rule answers** (asking afresh every time): as argued in "The problem", a fresh ask after a model's change releases a held message just the same; the commitment of holds is what prevents it.
- **Rule answers in an anchored record per answer**: an S3 commit for every message asked; a decision per message, begun and settled in a record of its day, costs at most two writes a page per day touched.
- **Committing a hold after it is decided** (this design before its review): a crash or a refused commit between the decision and the commit, then the kept answer removed, let a later ask release the message (finding 1); begun first, the decision is never lost, only settled `held`.
- **A block derived from the message's identifier** (its hash, or its time in the day), suggested to the third review's ruling: one owner per message too, but each block's share of a day is fixed, so a burst, or an uneven hash, fills one block while others are empty, below the owner's 28,800, and a page of fifty messages may read fifty strict records; the tail fence gives one owner with the reads and capacity of the chain (R1).
- **An index a decision claims before it is written**: one owner, at the cost of a strict record per message or per page, written before every ask.
- **Reusing room earlier blocks regain** (this design before its third review): two requests could allocate one message to two blocks whose histories are each valid, and a cleared in one hide a held in the other (R1).
- **Caching a read of the record of holds for five seconds** (before the review): an absence cached by one enclave let it release a message another had just held (finding 2).
- **A record of holds per mailbox and month, 5,000 at most** (before the review): 5,000 holds are about 1.17 MB sealed, past the 400 KiB item, and its versions were not bounded (finding 11); a day's record within counted bytes, decisions and versions is.
- **Giving already-cleared mail while the model is off**: the owner decided nothing leaves (decision 9), and a page of only cleared mail would tell a vendor which mail was decided before.
- **Chunked receipts with a manifest**: more writes per request and a second kind of entry for step 8 to chain; ending a page at the byte bound is simpler and skips nothing (finding 10).
- **A receipt per message**: simpler to show, but 51 writes for a page of 50; one per request holds the same.
- **Receipts without senders and subjects**: less kept of the mail, but after a deletion the person could no longer see what a vendor got, which is what receipts are for (ADR 0019: they outlive the mail).
- **The receipt written after the answer**: faster, but mail could leave without one when the write fails.
- **Chaining now**: needs the head's anchored record updated by every receipt from two enclaves, which is step 8's design; written once and keyed by time, the receipts of step 5 can be chained later as they are.

The cost, stated: room an earlier block regains is not reused, so a filled day recovers through its last block only; the capacity is unchanged. The review's schedule rested on a request reading the day as expiry made room, so a decision is also kept an hour past its message (`DecisionKeptAfter`) and a message past its own expiry is never begun (`expired`); and, in depth, a message ever found in two blocks answers its most protective decision and neither is written. The fake no longer changes a day under one lock or restores it whole: `disclosetest.Records` keeps each block as its own record, written only at the version read, as strict records are; one algorithm, `inbox.BlockDecisions`, runs over it and over strict anchored records alike, and `Contract` runs the review's interleaving on both (a store that pauses before its first write while the other enclave acts). `TestReview3R1CompetingAllocationsCannotHideAHold` (the review's schedule on real strict records: B's write refused, M `held` and in one block), `Contract`'s H25 and H28 cases on `Records` and strict records; H25, H28.
- **R2. A partial in-doubt answer was abandoned and could later be cleared.** Accepted, as proposed: abandonment now accounts for every atom of the rule, not only the unanswered ones. Unknown with any answer received (in doubt, a refusal, or any answer beside one missing) is `held` with that evidence; with none received and one unsettled it stays `pending`, recovered `held` after the grace; only when the model answered no part of it is it `abandoned`. Of the review's two options (commit Held with its evidence, or keep Pending for recovery), Held wherever an answer was received: final at once and with its evidence, which a receipt then names; Pending only where nothing received can be named. The cost: a message for which the model answered one atom and not the other is held for that rule's version, even on a low answer. `TestReview3R2APartialAnswerIsNeverAbandoned` (in doubt and absent, refusal and absent, low and absent, kept and unsettled, only unsettled, nothing), `TestReview3R2PartialDoubtIsHeldThroughTheRealAsker` and `TestReview3R2PartialDoubtIsHeldOnStrictRecords` (the review's schedule through the real Asker, `KeptStore`, `Eval` and both stores: `held` with the 0.50; the kept entry erased; the other enclave's 0.05 and 0.05 losing); `TestReview2N1OnlyPositiveEvidenceAbandons` now expects a kept answer beside an unsettled one held with it; H4, H26.

## Changed after the fourth design review (2026-11-16)

The fourth design review of ADR 0030's pull request at `afcc460` found R1 to R3 fixed and two new defects in the shared implementation of the records of holds (F1, P1; F2, P2), and kept partial what rests on them (N1, the first review's findings 1 and 16, dispositions 27 and 31, the implementation of the owner's decision 12, and parallel-build condition 4). The orchestrator accepted both, as the review proposes. Each reproduction is a numbered test that failed at `afcc460`, passes now, and fails again when its fix is undone; each change is made in place above. By item:

- **F1. A settlement's retry replayed an abandonment against another request's `pending`.** Accepted. `BlockDecisions.Settle` kept nothing of a try whose later block was refused and gave the whole settlement again, so an abandonment committed in block 0 was replayed against the `pending` another request had begun since in the tail: the protection of the answer that request had received was dropped, and a later low answer committed `cleared`. Two changes, each enough for the review's schedule, both kept. What a try's blocks committed is kept as it was answered, and only the rest is settled again, read afresh (`DecisionBlocks` says which block's write commits each answer). And every destructive settlement is bound to the attempt that began it: `Begin` answers each `begun` decision with an `inbox.Attempt` (its block and the version of that block its begin wrote, unique because no two committed writes of one block are made at one version, so it needs no counter and no randomness), stored with the `pending` and dropped when it settles; `abandoned` and `cleared` change only a `pending` of the attempt `Settlement.Attempt` names, and one that finds another attempt's changes nothing and is answered `pending`. `held` names none: holding another's `pending` only protects, as a recovery does. A settlement given again after a write whose outcome is unknown is as safe as the retry. The discloser (pull request 4) gives back the attempt of each decision it began; one it forgets fails closed (the `pending` stays and is recovered `held`). `Contract`'s H29 case runs the review's split page, built through `Begin` and `Settle` only, on `Records` and on two strict `Anchored` stores, its conflict the store's own (no lock over a day, no conflict made up): A's answers kept, B's `pending` kept, A's whole settlement given again and a `cleared` of A's attempt changing nothing, B's 0.90 `held` and never cleared; with `-race -count=20`. H29.
- **F2. A tail retired while its room was held by `pending` reservations lowered the capacity below 28,800.** Accepted, keeping R1's fence. Of the review's options, the simplest that keeps every guarantee: a page that does not fit waits for the tail's settlements. The tail is retired only when it is out of decisions or versions, or out of bytes even with its live `pending` (younger than `PendingGrace`) counted at their present size; while only their reservations keep a decision out, it is `full` for this request (held, undecided, named and counted as any `full`) and a later request begins it in the same tail once they settle. An overdue `pending` still counts at its largest, so a lost settlement keeps the tail waiting two minutes at most; nothing is allocated anywhere but the tail (R1), R2 is untouched, and a tail out of versions still opens its successor at once (R3). The cost, stated: near a block's end, a message may be held, undecided, for one request while the page before it settles. With the default rule's two answers (about 580 bytes settled, 500 of them within the block's 300 KiB), a tail is now retired at its 500 decisions: the review's probe, now `TestReview4F2SequentialPagesFillADay` on the whole of `DecisionBlocks`, fills a day's 64 blocks with 32,000 settled decisions (25,696 at `afcc460`), every retired block at 500, and `DecisionsPerDay` stays the promised minimum, 28,800. `TestReview4F2ATailWaitsOnlyForItsLivePending`: held while its live `pending` hold its room, more taken once they settle, the successor opened once they are overdue, and a tail out of its count retired whatever it holds; `TestReview4F2AHeldWithNothingBegunWaitsToo`: a `held` for a message with nothing begun waits for the tail the same way. `Begin` now counts a block's bytes once and then entry by entry, and reads the day once a page instead of once a message, so the full day's test takes seconds. H30.
