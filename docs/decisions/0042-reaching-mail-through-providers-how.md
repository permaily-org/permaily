## Context

Today a mailbox receives mail only by forwarding (ADR 0018, how in ADR 0026): the person tells Gmail or another provider to forward to a random address at `inbox.permaily.com`, and the MX inside the enclave seals and stores what arrives. That has three costs the owner wants gone in F3: forwarding brings nothing from before it started; Outlook.com and many work tenants forbid or hide automatic forwarding to outside addresses; and forwarding is a set-up of several steps at the provider that many people do not finish.

The owner decided on 2026-11-27 that a mailbox may instead read the person's mail from the provider: Gmail through its API, Outlook through Microsoft Graph, and a closed list of IMAP providers; the person chooses forwarding or a provider for each mailbox; what is read is stored as a sealed copy exactly as forwarded mail is, so rules, receipts, matches and decisions do not change. This record says how, exactly enough that the record, the sync engine, the three provider adapters, the routes and the web app can be built at the same time against seams committed first.

It is the opposite direction to ADR 0030. There Permaily is an authorization server and vendors are its clients; here Permaily is an OAuth client of Google and Microsoft, holding a refresh token that reads a person's mailbox. That token, and an IMAP app password, are the most powerful secrets the enclave will ever hold for a person: whoever has one reads the whole mailbox at the provider, also what Permaily never stored. The design keeps them inside the enclave, sealed under the mailbox's own key, gone with it.

Synchronising a mailbox is also the first thing in Permaily that deletes mail on someone else's say-so. The design's main rule follows from that: **no stored position, cursor or list ever authorises a deletion**; each deletion is authorised by evidence about that one message, read from the provider in the same step that deletes it. Everything a sync stores is then only a way to do less work, and can be lost or put back without harm beyond work done again. That is what lets this design use no new strict record and no new fence.

## The owner's decisions

Taken on 2026-11-27 in a round of questions; final.

1. Gmail (API), Outlook (Microsoft Graph) and IMAP, built and released in one step together.
2. A mailbox is either forwarding or provider; the person chooses.
3. A sealed copy in the enclave's storage, as forwarding stores it (rules, receipts, matches and decisions unchanged); the provider is read only when syncing.
4. On connecting: new mail from then on, plus 0, 30 or 90 days back, which the person picks (30 by default).
5. New mail by polling only, every five minutes (Gmail's history, Graph's delta, IMAP). No Pub/Sub, no Graph subscriptions, no IDLE.
6. Google in testing mode first (at most 100 test users, added by hand); Google's verification and the CASA assessment only when real users wait.
7. IMAP only to a closed list of providers in the image, port 993, implicit TLS; the egress stays a verifiable allowlist. Arbitrary servers were explained (SSRF, self-signed TLS, hostile servers, abuse from our address) and declined.
8. Outlook: personal and work accounts (some organisations' administrators must approve the application).
9. Folders: every folder except spam and trash; not sent mail.
10. Deletions mirrored: a message deleted at the provider is deleted from Permaily with its kept decisions.
11. Read-only permissions (`gmail.readonly`, `Mail.Read`): Permaily never changes, moves or sends anything in the mailbox.
12. IMAP authentication by an app password, sealed in the enclave.

## Decision in short

- **A mailbox has a kind**: `forwarding` (every mailbox so far), `gmail`, `outlook` or `imap`, fixed when it is made and signed in its record (ADR 0026), with three more fixed fields (`connected_at`, `history_days`, and the provider account's identity, sealed). A provider mailbox has no address at `inbox.permaily.com`; it counts against the five mailboxes and the ten creations in thirty days like any other.
- **One safe effect boundary per protocol**, each an existing commit ("Effect boundaries"): a provider mailbox is published by ADR 0026's `CreateNew` of its record; it ends at ADR 0026's terminal commit; a message is stored by ADR 0026's claim; a deletion is authorised by fresh provider evidence for that message; the grant changes only at the version read of its one entry; the feature opens only with a release whose image says so.
- **One activation barrier for the whole feature** ("Launch: one barrier"): every release from the first that carries provider code is dormant (`providers.open: false`, compiled): no provider mailbox can be made, nothing is polled. One later release, the opening release, sets it true for the three kinds together, and is cut only once the owner's legal determination is recorded here and the KMS keys of every release before the first dormant one are removed.
- **The legal gate has one choke point** ("The owner's legal gate"): before the opening release no provider mailbox exists in production at all; after it (and in any dormant release a rollback lands on), the mailboxes service's access table refuses, by the signed kind of the mailbox, every opening of it for the model or for a vendor that the compiled `providers.uses` closes. Every path to the model or to a vendor opens a mailbox first.
- **Connecting is an OAuth trip the web app starts and the enclave finishes**, on the pattern of the sign-in trip (ADR 0017): authorization code with PKCE (S256), a state and a nonce, bound to the initiating session, a `__Host-` cookie with `Path=/`; the enclave exchanges the code, checks the granted scopes against a fail-closed allowlist, reads the stable identity, rechecks the session's standing immediately before the record's commit, and makes the mailbox through ADR 0026's creation; the grant is written after.
- **Client identifiers are in the image**, a Google Cloud project and a Microsoft application separate from signing in's; **client secrets come from the operator** over the attested connection (`scripts/provider.sh`, ADR 0029).
- **The grant is one sealed entry** in the mailbox's partition, under the mailbox's key, changed only at the version read: connecting again, a rotated refresh token and a grant that stopped working are all writes of it. Disconnecting removes the key (ADR 0026), so the grant opens to nothing. **Permaily does not call Google's revocation**: the person is told where to remove Permaily's access at their provider, as for Microsoft and IMAP.
- **Cursors are sealed entries, not strict records**: Gmail's history position, Graph's delta links and IMAP's `UIDNEXT` per folder. A cursor lost or put back means messages are read again, and the claim stores each once.
- **A provider message is stored through ADR 0026's claim**, named by the provider's identifier (Gmail's message id, Graph's immutable id, IMAP's folder, `UIDVALIDITY` and UID); the claim lives as long as the copy. A person's delete keeps the claim, so the copy is not imported again; a mirrored deletion deletes the copy, then writes the claim `gone`, which an import takes over at once, so a message restored at the provider is copied again (H2). **Every effect on one message is ordered by its claim**: the claim is written (made, taken over, or moved to `checking`) before the provider is read for that message, and the effect commits by a write at that version, so an observation made before another effect's write never commits (G2).
- **Deletions need fresh positive evidence about that message**: Gmail's current labels or a 404 for its id; Graph's current parent folder for its immutable id when that folder is itself an excluded well-known folder, or a 404 (no folder's ancestry is evidence, I2); an IMAP `UID SEARCH` for its UID completed under an unchanged `UIDVALIDITY`, a greater `UIDVALIDITY`, or `[NONEXISTENT]` for its exact folder. Evidence is read in the step that deletes, after the claim's `checking` write. Moves keep a message where the provider's identifier survives them (Gmail, Graph); in IMAP a move is a deletion and a new copy, and the message's kept decisions are lost.
- **One admission rule for history**: whatever the provider lists of the past is admitted only when its provider date is within `history_days` before `connected_at` (less ten minutes' margin) and within the retention from now; new mail from the live stream is admitted always. Retention counts from when Permaily copied the message, as for forwarding.
- **A reset is a resync**: a new baseline, then an add-only enumeration beside the live stream, then a reconciliation that checks every claim of the scope by its own evidence, resumable and independent of what the enumeration listed (G4). It runs when the provider loses the position, and when a local cursor is lost.
- **The poller** reuses ADR 0039's due index, lease and repair; the lease only saves work, since nothing a poll writes needs a fence.
- **Egress is a policy compiled into the image and enforced inside the enclave**: exact scheme, host, port, method and path template, checked before connecting and before a credential is attached, on every request including continuations; IMAP only to the listed hosts on 993 with the listed name verified by TLS. `/status` publishes the policy and `permaily-verify` compares it exactly.

## Effect boundaries

Each protocol commits its effects at one record and version; everything before that commit is invisible or inert, and everything after it is idempotent or authorised again by its own evidence.

| Protocol | Commits at | Before the commit | After the commit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provisioning | The mailbox record `mailbox-<id>`, made with `CreateNew` (strict; ADR 0026's creation, step 5) | The trip's checks, the code's exchange, the scope and identity checks, the reservation in the account's list (strict, ADR 0026's steps 1 to 3), and the session's standing read again; nothing of the provider written | ADR 0026's settings and index entry, then the grant entry and the schedule entry, inside `IngestDeadline` from a strict read of the record. A crash after the commit leaves a mailbox that shows "Connect again" ("Provisioning") |
| The grant (connecting again, a rotated token, a grant that stopped working) | The grant entry `grant` in the mailbox's partition, at the version read | A strict read of the mailbox record (active, the same provider identity) | The new token used; the notice of a break queued ("Notices by mail") |
| A sync step and its cursor | Nothing strict. The cursor entries (`sync`, `fold#<folder>`) at the version read, moved only past units whose every effect is terminal (G3); a unit's start written before its first effect (H3) | The step's strict reads of the record and of the account's list (G11) and its read of the grant; the provider's reads; the imports and deletions below, each one its own boundary | The next step starts from the cursor. A cursor stale or put back costs messages read again; a cursor lost starts a reconciliation (G4) |
| Import | ADR 0026's claim `seen#<occurrence>` marked stored (`Writer.Mark`, at the version of the claim's `importing` write) | The claim made or taken over (`importing`), then the provider's read of that message (its current labels or folder, and its bytes), then the raw object, the search entry and the item, in ADR 0026's order; the item is where the person can see it, so after it the writer reads the message's deletion mark again (G1) | Nothing |
| A mirrored deletion | The claim written `deleting` at the version of its own `checking` write (G2) | The claim written `checking` at the version read; then the provider's evidence about that message, under that step's strict reads | `Reader.Delete` (ADR 0028's mark first, then the copy and its kept decisions), then the claim written `gone` at its `deleting` version (H2); whoever finds a `deleting` claim older than `ClaimStale` finishes both; from the commit on, webhook admission and evaluation read the claim and treat the copy as gone (K3) |
| A person's delete | ADR 0028's mark (unchanged) | The message's claim written `stored` at the version read when it is still `importing` (G1) | The copy and its kept decisions deleted; the claim kept, so the copy is not imported again while it lives; the mark kept until the sweep's pass after `SweepGrace` has deleted the copy's parts once more (H1) |
| Disconnecting | ADR 0026's terminal commit of the record (strict) | ADR 0026's fresh proof | Steps in flight end within `IngestDeadline` of their read; the sweep after `SweepGrace` deletes the partition, the grant with it |
| The legal gate and the launch | The image (PCR0): `providers.open` and `providers.uses`, compiled | Dormant releases: no provider mailbox can be made | The opening release: the three kinds offered together; the uses it compiles enforced at every opening of a mailbox |

Two enclaves may run the same step of the same mailbox at once (a lease lost or skewed): both claim the same messages and one stores each; both read evidence and the deletions are the same; both write the cursor at the version read and one loses. Nothing in the table needs a run generation, a grant epoch, a journal or an identity row, because none of those effects is authorised by stored state that an earlier or concurrent run could have left.

**The claim orders one message's effects (G1, G2).** A provider claim's sealed value carries a `state`: `importing` (ADR 0026's claim not yet marked), `stored` (ADR 0026's mark), `excluded` (read and not stored: found in an excluded folder, or past a bound, with its reason), `checking` (a deletion's evidence is being read), `deleting` (the deletion is committed) and `gone` (the deletion finished, or the provider answered that the message does not exist; nothing held), and `checked_at`, the time of the last evidence a write of it followed, and two proof slots (J3): `checked_in`, the nonce of the unit whose write made the claim terminal, and `reconciled_in`, the nonce of the reconciliation whose write did. Each pass's own slot is cleared by its opening write and set by its terminal one; the other pass's slot is carried unchanged by a deletion check's `checking` write and by that check's return to `stored` with the same message identifier, both slots are carried unchanged by the hourly away check's `checking` write and by its return of the same copy, under the same message identifier, to `stored` (K5), and every other write clears them, so a slot holds a nonce only while the claim is the terminal state, with the copy, that its pass proved ("Units and progress", H3, I1, I7, J3). Forwarded claims keep only `importing` and `stored`. Every transition is `PutEntry` at the version read (ADR 0026's `putSeen`); nothing else is added to `internal/store`. **One incarnation per key (H2).** `PutEntry` compares only the version, and versions start again when an entry is deleted or expires, so a provider claim is never deleted while its copy can live: a finished deletion writes it `gone` at its `deleting` version, and an import takes a `gone` or `excluded` claim over at the version read, so its versions only grow from its first write to its expiry. No transition is written to a claim read less than `IngestDeadline` plus `ClockSkew` before its expiry (its copy expired a day before; nothing is left to decide), so no delayed write can land on a later incarnation made after that expiry. `Writer.Mark`'s fallback (on a conflict, read the claim again and mark over it when it names the same message) never applies to a provider claim (I6): a provider mark that meets a conflict fails and writes nothing, its effect is not terminal, and its unit is taken again; the actor holding the claim's current version decides on its own evidence and resolves what the stale writer staged or published (a takeover of `importing` keeps the message's identifier, so those parts are under its own keys: kept and marked when its evidence includes the message, deleted by its `deleting` commit otherwise). An import that takes a `gone` or `excluded` claim over draws a fresh message identifier and `received_at`, as a new claim does (only a takeover of `importing` keeps the identifier, `Writer.Claim` today), so no deletion mark or sweep pass left by an earlier copy names the new one (I6). A takeover of an `importing` claim does not know whether its first writer published the item, so a takeover whose evidence excludes the message, or finds it gone, commits `deleting` at its own version instead of `excluded`, and the deletion's finish removes whatever the first writer staged or published. The invariant, for every pair of effects on one occurrence: **an effect commits only by a write at the version it wrote before it read the provider about that message**. So of two effects that each read the provider, the one whose write came second either read the provider after the first one's write, and saw what the first saw or later, or fails its commit and discards its observation. Any change at the provider after an effect's read is a later event (a Gmail history record, a Graph delta change, an IMAP expunge found by a later search), which the cursor does not pass while that claim is `importing`, `checking` or `deleting` and younger than `ClaimStale` (G3). What a step finds per state: `stored` and young `importing`, `checking`, `deleting` as the table above says; an older `importing` is taken over (ADR 0026), an older `checking` is taken over and its evidence read again, an older `deleting` is finished; `excluded` and `gone` are taken over at once by an import, and are nothing held for a deletion.

### Records and layout

What ADR 0026's table gains. "Sealed" and "signed" mean what they mean there; "strict" means a strict anchored record (ADR 0016).

- **The message's claim in its metadata (G1).** `StoredMeta` gains `Claim`, the key of the `seen#` entry that fixed the message, written by `Writer.Store` for every ingress from pull request 1 on; a person's delete reads it from the item's metadata, which `markGone` already reads. A forwarded message stored before has none, and keeps today's behaviour.

- **The scope of a claim's name** is `gmail`, `graph`, or `imap\0<folder name>\0<uidvalidity>`; so the claims of one IMAP folder under one `UIDVALIDITY` share a prefix, which the presence check lists ("Per provider"). The HMACs are under the key ADR 0026 derives for `seen#` names, from the mailbox's key: the table's reader sees how many claims a scope has and when they were written, never an identifier or a folder name.
- **Why the grant is in the mailbox's partition, under its key.** Disconnecting removes the key from the record (ADR 0026, "What disconnecting does to the key"); from that commit on no enclave opens the grant, and the sweep deletes it with the partition. A grant copied from another mailbox does not open (its seal is bound to its mailbox's identifier); one whose `subject` is not the record's is not used (T14).
- **Why nothing here is strict, apart from the record.** ADR 0016's strict records are the right tool for state whose replay would do harm. Here a replayed grant is an older token of the same provider account with the same scopes, or a break already mended (denial of service, which whoever writes the table can do anyway); a replayed cursor reads messages again; a replayed claim either keeps a message from being copied again or lets it be copied once more ("The threat model"). None authorises a deletion. A strict record written by every poll would also cost what the second review measured: every strict read lists the record's whole history and fetches at most 500 objects not read before, so a record written every five minutes outgrows a cold read within two days (E4).
- **The sweep's prefixes** (`partitionPrefixes`, ADR 0026's sweep) gain `grant`, `sync`, `fold#` and `away#`; provider claims are `seen#` entries already swept. A schedule entry whose mailbox has ended is deleted by the poller or by the repair when it reads the record ("The poller"), and in any case by ADR 0026's sweep of that mailbox's partition, which knows its identifier and deletes `providers#sched#<shard>`/`<id>` with it (G13); its due minutes expire 72 hours after their minute. A test lists every key a provider mailbox's life writes and checks that the sweep or the poller deletes it (T40).
- **Writes of the mailbox record.** Unchanged from ADR 0026 (made, `receiving_since` once, pauses, disconnect, removal). Connecting again, a rotated token and cursors never write the record.

### Launch: one barrier

Production runs one enclave at a time (docs/operations.md: `scripts/handoff.sh` hands the root secret from the running release to the next on the same instance, and the old enclave is terminated); ADR 0024's second enclave is not deployed. Two releases therefore meet the same data only through a rollback, which `scripts/deploy.sh` makes possible while the older release's KMS key exists (docs/operations.md, "Roll back"). A release from before this record decodes a mailbox's record and an account's list into typed structures and writes a fresh encoding (`internal/services/mailboxes/service.go`, `mailboxDoc`, `listDoc`), dropping fields it does not know, and has no idea that some mail must not reach the model or a vendor. So:

1. **The floor.** Pull request 1 fixes the record's provider fields, the provider claims and the partition's new entries, teaches the sweep and the disconnect every provider kind, and adds the access table's column ("The owner's legal gate"). The first release that carries it is the floor, F. From F on, every release reads, keeps and rewrites provider records and lists whole; no later pull request changes those fields (a change would need its own record).
2. **Dormant releases.** F and every release after it until the opening one compile `providers.open: false`: the connect routes answer `providers_closed`, `Import` refuses a provider kind, the poller and the operator's provider route exist but act on nothing, the web app offers no provider. The code ships and runs in production with nothing to do.
3. **The opening release, O.** One small pull request sets `providers.open: true` for the three kinds together (decision 13), compiles `providers.uses` from the owner's determination, and adds that determination to this record as a dated section. It compiles `providers.determination`, the date and SHA-256 of that section's text; a build with `open: true` and no determination fails its tests (T144), and `permaily-verify` pins both (T128). It is cut, and deployed, only when all of these hold:
   - the owner's determination is recorded ("The owner's legal gate");
   - every release before F has been removed from `release_keys` and the change applied, so its KMS key is scheduled for deletion (docs/operations.md, "Delete the key of an old release": the key is unusable from the apply, deleted seven days later; `deploy/tofu/releases.tf` sets `deletion_window_in_days = 7` and the key policy allows `kms:ScheduleKeyDeletion`) and no release before F can open the root secret again;
   - **no runtime that held the root secret before that apply is still running** (G10, H9): the owner reads, through AWS's own API and not through the parent instance, for each pre-F key by its exact key ID, the successful CloudTrail `ScheduleKeyDeletion` event and `DescribeKey`'s `KeyState` `PendingDeletion` (or `NotFoundException` for a key already deleted); the retirement time is the latest of those events; then that every instance able to run the enclave in the account was launched, or stopped and started, after it (EC2's `LaunchTime`, and for a stop and start the `StartInstances` event); an enclave does not survive its instance's stop, so no enclave admitted before the retirement holds the root secret. A key retired before CloudTrail's 90-day event history has as evidence its `NotFoundException` and the dated line docs/operations.md recorded when it was retired (as on 2026-11-17 for v0.10.0 and v0.11.0); an instance launched within those 90 days is after it. This is the operator's assertion under the owner's trust, recorded with the key IDs and the timestamps in docs/operations.md's opening checklist; no attestation proves it (choice 34);
   - the release running when O is handed over is F or later, so a rollback from O lands on a release that keeps provider records and closes their uses;
   - O's staging checks passed (T62, T68, T81, T107 to T110, T144, T163).

   **Joint readiness at run time** (G10, decision 13). O's image offers and connects a provider kind only while `ready`: the Google mail and the Microsoft mail client secrets are both installed and match the image's client identifiers, and the closed IMAP list is not empty. Until then O behaves as dormant (`providers_closed`, nothing offered, nothing polled) and `/status` says `open: true, ready: false`. The same check runs at every connect and at every offer, not only the first: if a secret is later removed, no new mailbox of any kind can be connected while it is missing, and the removed kind's mailboxes are `provider_off` while the other kinds' keep being polled (choice 35). No stored flag records a first opening, so there is nothing to roll back or replay.
4. **Rollback from O** to a dormant release R (F ≤ R < O) is safe by construction: R keeps every record and entry, polls nothing, connects nothing, and its access table refuses the model and vendor uses of every provider mailbox (T68, T118). The person still reads, downloads and deletes their copies, and can disconnect.

What the barrier rests on, stated: that a pre-F enclave cannot open the root secret is verifiable (its key's state is in KMS, which the owner reads; the running image's own key policy is on `/status`). That no pre-F enclave is still running from before its key was disabled is the operator's fact, checked against AWS's control plane as step 3 says and not against anything the parent reports: the handoff terminates the old enclave on the one instance, and with ADR 0024's two instances both would have to run F or later. `/status` says `providers.open` and the compiled uses, which `permaily-verify` checks against its pinned determination (T53, T128).

### Provisioning

A provider mailbox is made by ADR 0026's creation, unchanged except that it has no address:

1. ADR 0026's steps 1 to 3: the account's list gains the reserved identifier, within the five and the ten; its standing read strictly.
2. No address and no pointer (ADR 0026's step 4 is skipped for provider kinds).
3. For Gmail, `users.getProfile` read with the exchange's access token; its `historyId` is the baseline, and a failure makes nothing (`provider_failed`) (G9, H6). Then the session's standing read again ("The trip", step 6), immediately before the commit, and `connected_at` taken after both.
4. **The record made with `CreateNew`**, `active`, holding the wrapped key, the kind, `connected_at`, `history_days`, the sealed `subject` and, for Gmail, the sealed `gmail_baseline`. This is the commit that publishes the mailbox.
5. ADR 0026's settings and index entry, and the notice "a mailbox was added", as today.
6. The grant entry at version 0, then the schedule entry and its due minute, under a context of `IngestDeadline` from a strict read of the record and of the account's list (`listed`, G11) made after step 4; a list that is closed or does not name the mailbox writes nothing.

A crash or a failure before step 4 leaves a reservation that ADR 0026's settler fences after `ReservationAge`, and nothing else: no grant, cursor or schedule is written before the record exists. A crash after step 4 and before step 6 leaves an active mailbox with no grant, which the person sees as "Connect again" and which counts against the five until they connect it again or remove it (T64). A missing schedule entry, or a missing index entry, is remade by the repair or by the person's next look at their mailboxes ("The poller", G13). A grant written late after a disconnect's commit is inside `SweepGrace`, which the sweep's last pass covers, as for a late delivery (T21). Account removal during provisioning is ADR 0026's M20 (T70).

### One provider account, one mailbox per account

Connecting a provider account already connected to a live mailbox of the same Permaily account answers `provider_account_in_use`: the check reads the `subject` of the account's other provider mailboxes (at most four records, read and unsealed while the list is settled). It is best effort: two connections of the same provider account racing in one Permaily account may both succeed, and the person then has two mailboxes with the same mail, either removable. Two Permaily accounts may connect the same provider account; each completed the provider's own consent, so neither gains access the provider did not grant (choice 3). Nothing deployment-wide records a provider account, so the table's reader learns nothing of who is connected where.

### Kinds and the five

- **Making**: `POST /api/v1/mailboxes` for forwarding as today; a provider mailbox only through the connect flow below, and only when providers are open.
- **The limits** are ADR 0026's: five mailboxes that count, ten creations in thirty days, whatever their kind. Connecting again is not a creation and needs no free slot; it is bounded at ten in thirty days a mailbox, counted in the grant (`MaxReconnectsPerPeriod`), past which it answers `too_many_reconnects` (Google's testing mode needs about four).

### States

The record's `state` keeps ADR 0026's machine (`active`, `disconnecting`, `cancelled`, gone; lost and forked unreadable). What the person sees of a provider mailbox is read from the record, the grant, the sync state and the schedule:

| Shown | When |
|---|---|
| `importing` | The sync state's `phase` is `importing`: the live stream is followed, and the history window is being read too |
| `syncing` | `phase` is `syncing`: polled every five minutes |
| `backoff` | The schedule's `backoff_until` is later than now: the provider answered 429 or 5xx, or did not answer |
| `resyncing` | `phase` is `resyncing`: the provider lost the position ("Resets") |
| `needs_reauth` | No grant, or the grant's `state` is `broken`; not polled; keeps what it holds |
| `provider_off` | The operator removed the provider's secret, or the provider left the list; not polled, no notice |
| `paused` | The record's `paused_at`, as ADR 0026; not polled |
| `disconnecting`, `unreadable` | The record, as ADR 0026 |

| From | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| (made) | `importing`, or `syncing` with 0 days | The first poll captures the baselines ("Bootstrap") |
| `importing` | `syncing` | The backfill read its window, or reached a bound |
| any polled state | `resyncing` | Gmail 404 on the history position, Graph `syncStateNotFound`, `resyncRequired` or 410, IMAP `UIDVALIDITY` changed |
| `resyncing` | the phase before | The resync's enumeration and its checks completed |
| any polled state | `needs_reauth` | `invalid_grant` to a refresh; a 401 that survives one refresh; a refresh answered outside the scope allowlist; IMAP `AUTHENTICATIONFAILED` in two consecutive polls; `LOGINDISABLED`: the grant written `broken` at the version read |
| `needs_reauth` | the phase before | Connecting again with the same provider account writes the grant `ok` |
| any | `provider_off` and back | The provider's secret removed or set again, or the provider removed from the list by a release |

**What the person sees** (each sentence to review with the designer): "Importing: 1,240 messages so far from the last 30 days"; "Up to date, checked 3 minutes ago"; "Checking again in 15 minutes: Gmail asked us to slow down"; "Catching up after Gmail lost our place"; "Permaily can't read this mailbox any more. Connect it again"; "Gmail isn't available right now" (`provider_off`). After an import that reached a bound: "Copied 20,000 messages from the last 90 days; some older ones weren't copied."

**Accounts closing, blocked, not agreed, removed**: as ADR 0026 says for forwarding, polling goes on while forwarded mail would go on arriving, and stops at the commit that ends the mailbox. An operator's pause stops polling, as it stops `RCPT TO`. A step already running when a pause, a disconnect or a secret's removal is committed ends within `IngestDeadline` of its own read, as a delivery in flight does; it may finish the reads and imports it started, and starts nothing.

## Connecting with Google and Microsoft

### The applications

- **Separate from signing in**, and for Google in a separate Cloud project (`permaily-mail`): verification of a restricted scope and its yearly CASA assessment never touch sign-in, and removing a mail secret leaves sign-in alone.
- **Client identifiers in the image**: `deploy/enclave.json` gains `providers: {open, uses, google: {client_id}, microsoft: {client_id}}` (and under `staging`, with `test_subjects`). Choice 1.
- **Client secrets from the operator**, never in the image: `PUT /v1/admin/providers/{google-mail|microsoft-mail}` with the admin token, sent by `scripts/provider.sh set google-mail <client-id>` only to the attested key (ADR 0029), refused when the identifier is not the image's; sealed under the root secret in the providers' applications' record (ADR 0017). Secrets may be set while providers are dormant, so the opening release has them at once. Until both are set nothing is offered ("Launch: one barrier", joint readiness, G10); a secret removed later turns its kind off: its mailboxes are `provider_off`, and no kind is offered for new connections until it is set again.
- **Staging before the opening release.** A staging image may compile `staging.providers.open: true` with every use closed and a list of test subjects (`staging.providers.test_subjects`: the owner's synthetic Google accounts' `sub`, Microsoft `tid:oid`, IMAP host and username); a connection whose identity is not listed is refused after the exchange and before any mail API request (T117). Google's testing mode adds its own list of test users, which holds only those accounts.

### The trip

1. `POST /api/v1/mailboxes/connect/start {provider: gmail|outlook, history_days, label}` (or `{provider, mailbox}` to connect again) by a signed-in session of an open, agreed account, with a free slot for a new mailbox (none needed to connect again). The enclave draws a state (32 bytes), a PKCE verifier (S256) and a nonce, and keeps them sealed in `pending#mailconnect#<SHA-256 of state>` for ten minutes with the account, the session's identifier and its sign-out generation, the provider, the purpose (`connect`, or `reconnect` with the mailbox), `history_days` and the label; sets `__Host-permaily-mailconnect` exactly as the sign-in trip sets its cookie (`internal/httpapi/accounts.go`): `Path=/`, no `Domain`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, ten minutes, a random value whose hash the entry holds; answers the provider's address. One trip at a time per browser: a second start replaces the cookie, and the first trip's callback fails (T113).
2. The provider's address:
   - Google: `https://accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth` with `response_type=code`, `client_id`, `redirect_uri`, `scope=openid email https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly`, `access_type=offline`, `prompt=consent`, `include_granted_scopes=false`, `state`, `nonce`, `code_challenge`, `code_challenge_method=S256`.
   - Microsoft: `https://login.microsoftonline.com/common/oauth2/v2.0/authorize` with `response_type=code`, `response_mode=query`, `client_id`, `redirect_uri`, `scope=openid email offline_access https://graph.microsoft.com/Mail.Read`, `prompt=select_account`, `state`, `nonce`, `code_challenge`, `code_challenge_method=S256`.
3. `GET /api/v1/mailboxes/connect/callback?code&state` (or `error`) on the app host, one more cross-site arrival in `internal/httpapi/crosssite.go`. Malformed (missing or repeated parameters, a state not 43 base64url characters): refused before any read. The cookie is checked against the entry's hash first; then the entry is consumed by `DeleteEntryAt` at the version read, so one callback consumes it. No callback sets or clears the cookie (H13): it expires at its ten minutes, and a later start replaces it; a matched callback's consumed entry makes the cookie inert for that trip, so a response to an earlier trip, however late, cannot touch a newer trip's cookie. A callback whose cookie is missing or belongs to another trip leaves that trip alone, consumes nothing, and the unmatched entry expires unconsumed at its ten minutes (G17, G22).
4. The code exchanged at the token endpoint with the secret and the verifier, through the provider egress ("Egress"), reading at most 16 KiB.
5. **Checks**, each a refusal with a fixed reason, against a typed token response (`internal/providers/oauth`): the ID token's signature (keys fetched through the provider egress), issuer, audience, nonce and `iat` (not in the future beyond `ClockSkew`, and not older than the trip's ten minutes: a bound `internal/idp`'s check does not have today and the export adds, G17), with `internal/idp`'s checks exported for mail without its fallback from `oid` to `sub`; a refresh token present; the stable identity (Google `sub`; Microsoft `tid` and `oid`, both required); the granted scopes ("Scopes"); on staging, the test subjects.
6. **Standing, immediately before the commit**: the sealed session still open with the same sign-out generation (accounts' `sessionOpen`, the check the sign-in trip's link makes); the account open, its terms in force agreed; for a new mailbox, then ADR 0026's creation, whose strict read of the list checks the slot again ("Provisioning"); to connect again, the mailbox the account's, active, and the provider identity the record's (`provider_account_mismatch` otherwise). A sign-out, a terms change or a full account that lands before this read makes nothing (`signed_out`, `provider_failed`, `too_many_mailboxes`); one that lands after it does not undo what it made, as a forwarding mailbox's creation racing a sign-out is not undone: the mailbox is the account's and can be disconnected (T114, choice 32).
7. The grant written ("Provisioning", step 6; to connect again, the grant entry at the version read, `ok`). The browser goes to `/mailboxes/<id>`; a failure to `/mailboxes#connect_error=<reason>` with one of the fixed reasons (`cancelled`, `admin_approval_needed` for AADSTS65001 and AADSTS90094, `scope_not_granted`, `provider_account_in_use`, `provider_account_mismatch`, `too_many_mailboxes`, `too_many_reconnects`, `signed_out`, `providers_closed`, `provider_failed`), never a word of the provider's.

A trip saved and replayed after it was consumed meets its entry consumed, which is the replay guard, and a code the provider has already redeemed; the cookie may still be in the browser until its ten minutes, and matches no entry. It makes nothing (T7, T72, T167).

### Scopes

Requested scopes are compiled in. Granted scopes are parsed from a typed response, canonicalised, then compared with a fail-closed allowlist at the first exchange and at every refresh:

| Provider | Canonicalisation | Must hold | May hold | Anything else |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google | `email` and `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/userinfo.email` are one; `openid` as is | `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly` | `openid`, `email` | Refused, nothing stored |
| Microsoft (the access token's resource scopes, as the response reports them) | `Mail.Read` and `https://graph.microsoft.com/Mail.Read` are one; `openid`, `email`, `profile`, `offline_access` dropped from the comparison | `Mail.Read` | `User.Read` | Refused, nothing stored |

- **An omitted `scope`** means what that request asked for (RFC 6749, section 5.1): at the first exchange the requested set; at a Microsoft refresh, which always sends an explicit `scope`, that refresh's requested set; at a Google refresh, which sends none, the grant's stored set. An empty one is refused (T115, G17).
- **A refresh** asks Microsoft for `https://graph.microsoft.com/Mail.Read offline_access` (bound to the Graph resource) at the tenant's own endpoint, `/<tid>/oauth2/v2.0/token` with the record's sealed `tid`, never `common`, so the token it returns addresses the tenant the person consented in; an answer whose ID token (when present) names another `tid` or `oid` writes the grant `broken`. A Graph 404 is evidence only under such a token (G6, G17). A refresh answered outside the allowlist writes the grant `broken` and stores nothing of it; one with no new refresh token keeps the stored one.
- **The display address** (`email`, Microsoft's `preferred_username` or `email`) is optional and untrusted: shown to the person only, never matched; without it the mailbox shows "Outlook account" or "Gmail account".
- What a 403 or a conditional-access challenge from Graph means is established by the spike; until then a 403 is `backoff` with outcome `forbidden`, and three in a row write the grant `broken`.

### Tokens

- **Refresh** when the access token held in memory is missing or within five minutes of its end, re-reading the grant first. A rotated refresh token replaces the stored one at the version read before the access token is used; a write that fails (another enclave wrote first, or the person connected again) drops this access token and the step ends. Access tokens are never written anywhere.
- **Connecting again** writes the grant at the version read with the new refresh token and `state: ok`. A worker still holding the old access token finishes its step reading the same provider account's mail with the same scopes; its own write of the grant then fails at the version read, so it can neither mark the new grant broken nor put the old token back (T77).
- **Lifetimes**, as documented (confirmed by the spike): Google's refresh token lives until revoked, unused for six months, or the password changes; **in testing mode, seven days**; Google keeps at most 100 refresh tokens per account and client. Microsoft's lives 90 days, renewed by each use; it ends at a password reset, a revocation, or a conditional-access policy.

### Disconnecting

ADR 0026's disconnect, with a fresh proof, unchanged: from the terminal commit no enclave opens the grant (the key is gone from the record), every step in flight ends within `IngestDeadline` of its read, closing its IMAP session and dropping its access token, and the sweep deletes the partition after `SweepGrace` (T5, T74). Permaily does not call any provider's revocation (choice 21): the refresh token or app password becomes unreadable at the commit and is deleted by the sweep, and Google keeps listing Permaily as having access until the person removes it or the token ends. The disconnect's answer, the mailbox list and the notice "a mailbox was disconnected" say so, in fixed words per kind: "Permaily deleted its access. To remove Permaily from your Google account too, open myaccount.google.com/permissions" (Microsoft: account.live.com/consent/Manage or myapps.microsoft.com; IMAP: the provider's app-password page).

Six failure domains: Yahoo (with AOL), Apple, Fastmail, GMX, Zoho US (two hosts) and Zoho EU. Hosts of one failure domain share its connection limits ("Quotas and backoff"). Not listed: Gmail and Outlook over IMAP, Proton, and any host the person types. A provider leaves the list only by a release; its mailboxes are then `provider_off`.

### The connection

- **TLS** through the provider egress ("Egress"): `CONNECT <listed host>:993`, then TLS 1.2 or later inside the enclave before any IMAP byte, the listed name as SNI and as the name verified against the image's roots (WebPKI only: no pinning, no exception, no `STARTTLS`, no other port).
- **Authentication**: `AUTHENTICATE PLAIN` (or `LOGIN` without `AUTH=PLAIN`), after TLS. `AUTHENTICATIONFAILED` in two consecutive polls, or `LOGINDISABLED`: the grant `broken`, with "turn IMAP access on at <provider>" for the latter.
- **Read-only on the wire**: a command writer allows only `CAPABILITY`, `AUTHENTICATE`, `LOGIN`, `ENABLE CONDSTORE`, `LIST`, `EXAMINE`, `UID FETCH` (`BODY.PEEK[]`, `UID`, `INTERNALDATE`, `RFC822.SIZE`, `FLAGS`, `MODSEQ`), `UID SEARCH`, `NOOP` and `LOGOUT`, and refuses any other command before it is sent (T33).
- **One session per mailbox at a time**, held only within a step and closed at its deadline.
- **The app password and username** are sealed in the grant; typed in the connect screen (`POST /api/v1/mailboxes/connect/imap {host, username, app_password, history_days, label}`, with the trip's standing check, step 6), tried once before anything is made, never shown or answered back. **Connecting again** is `POST /api/v1/mailboxes/{id}/reconnect/imap {app_password}`: the same host and username, the grant written at the version read, the cursors kept.

### The client and its bounds

- **Library**: `github.com/emersion/go-imap/v2`, at a version pinned by pull request 6 with evidence (the version, and transcript tests) that `EXAMINE` is sent for its read-only select, `CONDSTORE` can be enabled, and a body's literal is read as a stream. `QRESYNC` and `OBJECTID` are not used.
- **The wrapped connection** bounds what any answer can make the enclave hold, before allocating: a line 64 KiB; a body's literal checked against its announced length before it is read, at most 25 MiB plus one byte, streamed into the import's reservation ("Memory"); any other literal 64 KiB; everything but body literals at most 1 MiB a command; nesting 16; 10,000 tokens a line; 1,000 unsolicited responses a command; each command 30 seconds. Over any bound the session closes and the step ends with the outcome `bounds`; a range whose answer passed 1 MiB is asked again in halves (T35, T123).
- **Fuzz targets** (`scripts/fuzz.sh`, a group `imap`): the wrapped reader with go-imap's decoder over arbitrary server bytes, the `LIST`, `FETCH` and `ESEARCH` answers, folder names in modified UTF-7.
- **A played server** for tests: go-imap v2's in-memory server behind TLS with a test CA, scripted to misbehave.

## Egress

The egress for providers is a policy compiled into the image and enforced inside the enclave; the host is untrusted and its allowlist is defence in depth only.

| Destination | Port | Methods | Paths | For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `oauth2.googleapis.com` | 443 | POST | `/token` | Google's tokens |
| `www.googleapis.com` | 443 | GET | `/oauth2/v3/certs` | Google's ID-token keys |
| `gmail.googleapis.com` | 443 | GET | `/gmail/v1/users/me/profile`, `/gmail/v1/users/me/history`, `/gmail/v1/users/me/messages`, `/gmail/v1/users/me/messages/{id}` | The Gmail API |
| `login.microsoftonline.com` | 443 | POST; GET | `/common/oauth2/v2.0/token`, `/{tenant}/oauth2/v2.0/token`; `/common/discovery/v2.0/keys` | Microsoft's tokens and keys; `{tenant}` only the record's `tid` (G17) |
| `graph.microsoft.com` | 443 | GET | `/v1.0/me/mailFolders`, `/v1.0/me/mailFolders/{id}`, `/v1.0/me/mailFolders/{id}/childFolders`, `/v1.0/me/mailFolders/{id}/messages/delta`, `/v1.0/me/mailFolders('{id}')/messages/delta`, `/v1.0/me/messages/{id}`, `/v1.0/me/messages/{id}/$value` | Microsoft Graph; `{id}` of `mailFolders/{id}` may be a well-known name ("Folders"); the second delta form is the one Graph's own continuations use (G6) |
| Each listed IMAP host | 993 | IMAP | none | IMAP over implicit TLS |

- **Where it is enforced.** `internal/providers/egress` holds the table (`egress.Policy`) and two dialers: an HTTP transport that, for every request, before it connects and before any `Authorization` header is attached, checks scheme `https`, the exact host, port 443, the method and the path template (one path segment per `{id}`, percent-encoded, no `..`), and drops the request otherwise (`egress_refused`, counted in the log); and an IMAP dialer that connects only to a listed host on 993. Both send `CONNECT` over the allowlisted proxy (vsock 8000), read its reply bounded at 4 KiB, send nothing before the TLS handshake ends, and do TLS inside the enclave with the image's roots and the policy's host as SNI and verified name. HTTP/2 (ALPN `h2`) for every destination the spike shows offers it, HTTP/1.1 for one it shows does not, compiled per destination; no redirect followed, no cookie, `Accept-Encoding: identity`, headers at most 16 KiB, each call 30 seconds, bodies bounded (a history, delta or folder page 4 MiB, a Gmail raw message 34 MiB of base64url, a Graph `$value` 25 MiB plus one byte). The ID-token verifier fetches its keys through this transport too.
- **Continuations.** Graph's `@odata.nextLink` and `@odata.deltaLink` are opaque: each, for folder listings as for delta, is parsed and bound before it is stored or followed (G6): the same host, `/v1.0/me` (the signed-in mailbox, never `users/…`), the same operation, either documented form of the path, the folder id decoded (percent-decoding, then the quotes of the `('…')` form) and equal to the folder whose cursor it is, and only `$skiptoken`, `$deltatoken` and the `$select` the request sent as its query. One that is not is refused, and that folder or listing starts again from its beginning (T140).
- **One origin per request** (G18). Requests are built only from typed provider operations, never from a URL given by a caller: the checked URL's host is the `Host` header (HTTP/1.1) or `:authority` (HTTP/2), the SNI, the verified name, the `CONNECT` authority and the connection pool's key, all one canonical origin; userinfo, a `Host` or `:authority` set apart from the URL, proxy and authorization headers set by a caller, redirects, encoded slashes or dots in a path, and reuse of a connection for another origin are refused (T151).
- **The host's list.** `cmd/permaily-host`'s `defaultEgress` gains the hosts too, and a test fails when `defaultEgress`, host and port, differs from the union of a fixed list of the destinations it held before this record (written out in the test, not derived from either list) and the destinations of `egress.Policy` (T45, T168, G18, H14); nothing relies on it: a test runs every adapter through a permissive proxy, an off-origin continuation, and servers with valid certificates for other names, and sees nothing leave (T75, T124).
- **DNS** (choice 23, an amendment of the design brief, which asked for the providers' names to be resolved over DNS over HTTPS). The host resolves the name of a `CONNECT`, untrusted, as for every allowlisted destination; DNS over HTTPS stays for DKIM. What is guaranteed is the WebPKI identity of the logical destination, never its address or location: a host that routes a correctly checked `CONNECT` elsewhere reaches a server that cannot prove the name, and the handshake fails before any credential or mail byte is sent. The host can deny service, and can route to any server that holds a valid certificate for the same name. The amendment covers the provider egress only; the DKIM resolver's own traffic is in "The privacy page and terms" (G19).

## Sync

### The poller

`internal/services/providersync`, run by every enclave, on ADR 0039's pattern as implemented in `internal/services/webhooks`:

- **Due index**: each pass reads the due minute of now from its start and, while the scan is behind, each minute back to `scanBack` (ten minutes), then continues its forward scan, at most `scanEntriesMax` entries a pass, paginating within a minute; a due entry that does not fit the ready queue, or whose lease another holder keeps, stays where it is. The next due minute is written before the old one is deleted. **One rule for what falls behind (H10, I8)**: once an hour each enclave's pass is a deep pass, which walks the due minutes of the last 72 hours oldest first from its own position, kept in the enclave's memory apart from the short scan's (a minute and the key after the last entry examined, as ADR 0039's `scanCursor` in `internal/services/webhooks`): at most `scanEntriesMax` entries and 4,320 minute queries a deep pass (most of them empty), the position moving past every entry examined, whether the pass took it, found its lease held or found the queue full, and wrapping to the oldest retained minute after the newest. So a prefix of held entries cannot hide a later one, an entry passed over is visited again at the next wrap, and with B retained due entries a full walk takes at most ⌈B / `scanEntriesMax`⌉ deep passes (one, for the fleet this record targets: a few hundred mailboxes, at most two due entries each); an enclave started again begins its walk at the oldest minute. A due entry that expired at its 72 hours is remade by the repair, which writes a due minute of now for a schedule whose `next_due` has passed and whose due minute is not there.
- **Admission**: due mailboxes go into ready queues, one per provider kind and within it one per account, taken round robin; at most 1,024 ready. Per enclave: 8 polls at once, 1 mailbox of an account at a time, 4 IMAP sessions, 2 to one IMAP failure domain.
- **The lease**: the schedule entry is read; a poll is skipped while another holder's `lease.until` plus `ClockSkew` has not passed; it is taken by a write at the version read with this enclave as holder and `until` = now + `LeaseFor` (six minutes); and **released** by the poll's last write, which also sets `next_due` (T126). The lease saves duplicate work and quota; it fences nothing, and nothing needs it to (T22).
- **Steps**: a poll is a sequence of steps, at most `PollBudget` (four minutes) in all. Each step reads the mailbox record strictly (stopping when it is not `active`, is paused, or its kind's provider is off), then the account's list strictly with forwarding's own check (`listed` in `internal/services/mailboxes/deliver.go`: the list open and naming the mailbox; a blocked account and one in its closing days keep an open list, G11), and only then opens the grant, and does all its work, remote calls and writes, under a context of `IngestDeadline` (one minute) from before those reads, so ADR 0026's `SweepGrace` covers a step across a disconnect exactly as it covers a delivery (T21). A step starts only while the lease leaves `IngestDeadline` plus `ClockSkew`.
- **Interval**: due again at the poll's start plus five minutes (`PollEvery`), plus a jitter up to 30 seconds from the mailbox's identifier; `backoff_until` when later.
- **The repair**, hourly: walks ADR 0026's mailboxes' index, reads each provider mailbox's record and its account's list strictly (`listed`, G11), remakes a missing schedule entry or due minute only for a mailbox that passes both, deletes the schedule entry of a mailbox that has ended, and queues a `needs_reauth` notice not yet marked queued ("Notices by mail"). The person's list of their mailboxes, which reads every record its account's list names, remakes a provider mailbox's missing index entry (ADR 0026's signed index entry, as provisioning writes it) and its missing schedule entry (G13), so a mailbox whose index entry was never written becomes reachable by the repair from then on. An ended mailbox's schedule entry is also deleted by ADR 0026's sweep of its partition ("Records and layout"), so neither a lost index entry nor a seven-day backoff keeps it.
- **Bounds, stated separately (G14).** Healthy: each mailbox polled within `PollEvery` plus one pass, while the fleet's demand is under the poller's capacity, which T108 measures (a quiet poll is one or two provider calls; 400 mailboxes at five minutes need about 80 polls a minute, inside eight slots only if the average poll is under six seconds, which T108 checks). Failover: within `LeaseFor` plus `ClockSkew` plus one pass. Overload: no bound is claimed; nothing is lost, because a due minute is deleted only after the poll that wrote the next one, the hourly deep pass walks every minute of its 72-hour life oldest first, from where its last capped pass stopped (I8), and the repair remakes one past it (H10); the hourly log counts `overdue` mailboxes. Within one IMAP mailbox a poll visits at most 40 folders, from `sync.next_folder`, in rotation, so a 200-folder mailbox's folders are each visited within five polls and its presence checks within about 90 minutes (choice 30, amended).

### Bootstrap

`connected_at` is the record's creation time, the boundary from which new mail is promised: taken after Gmail's baseline was read and before Graph's and IMAP's (H6). The first poll captures Graph's and IMAP's baselines and starts the backfill:

- **Gmail**: the live stream's first position is the `historyId` read in the trip before `connected_at` and sealed in the record (`gmail_baseline`, "Provisioning", steps 3 and 4), so every message added after `connected_at` is after it, and no lost write can take it away (G9, H6). A message added before that read is mail from before connecting, history, admitted by its date. The backfill lists `users.messages.list` with `q=after:<connected_at − history_days − 1 day>` (no spam or trash) and admits each message by its exact date, except one whose `historyId` (as `format=raw` and `format=metadata` return it) is above the baseline: that message was added or changed after connecting, and the live stream decides it, so a backdated new message is never taken as history (spike item 3 confirms the field).
- **Graph**: each included folder's first delta round, unfiltered (no `$filter`, so no 5,000-message cap), is read page by page as backfill; its final `deltaLink` is the folder's live position. A message that arrives during the round appears in it or in the next delta; one in the next delta is live, one in the round is admitted by its date. When a history bound is reached during a round, the round still runs to its `deltaLink` (the live position must not be lost), admitting only messages dated at or after `connected_at − ConnectMargin` and counting the rest as skipped (G9).
- **IMAP**: each included folder's `UIDVALIDITY` and `UIDNEXT` (and `HIGHESTMODSEQ` with `CONDSTORE`) from `EXAMINE`, the first time that folder is seen. UIDs at or above the baseline are the live stream, whatever their `INTERNALDATE`; below it, the backfill, by date. The two ranges are disjoint, so the live stream and a listing never contend for one message.

**The admission rule**, the same for the backfill, a resync and a folder discovered later: a message listed from the past is admitted only when its provider date (Gmail `internalDate`, Graph `receivedDateTime`, IMAP `INTERNALDATE`) is at or after `connected_at − history_days − ConnectMargin` (ten minutes) and at or after now less the mailbox's retention; a message with no date, or a date more than `ClockSkew` in the future, is not admitted from a listing. New mail from the live stream is admitted always. So with 0 days a message that arrives in any folder between `connected_at` and that folder's baseline is admitted by its date, and mail from up to ten minutes before connecting may be too (T80, T122). **What dates cannot prove (G9, choice 36).** For Graph and IMAP, a message added between connecting and that folder's first baseline (normally the first poll, seconds after the commit) or during Graph's first round, whose provider date is before the history window (migrated or appended mail), is not admitted; the same holds, for every provider, for such a message added while a reset's position was lost. The person's page says so in fixed words when a reset ran ("Mail added while we were catching up and dated before your history window may not have been copied").

### Per provider

- **Gmail, live.** `users.history.list?startHistoryId=<cursor>&historyTypes=messageAdded&historyTypes=messageDeleted&historyTypes=labelAdded&historyTypes=labelRemoved&maxResults=500`, page by page. Records are processed in order; the cursor moves to each record's `id` once every effect of that record is terminal ("Units and progress"), written at the end of each step and at least every 100 records, and to the response's `historyId` after the last page. A cut resumes at the first record not done, so a page larger than a step never stalls (T91). For a message added, or whose labels changed, that Permaily does not hold: when its history entry's labels are excluded ("Folders") nothing is fetched; otherwise the claim is made (`importing`) and then `users.messages.get?format=raw` is read, whose current `labelIds` decide (included: stored, origin `live` only for `messageAdded`; excluded: the bytes dropped and the claim written `excluded`). For a message Permaily holds: deleted, or labels changed so that the entry's labels are excluded, it is a candidate, and `users.messages.get?format=metadata&metadataHeaders=` read after the claim's `checking` write gives its current labels, the evidence ("Deletions"); labels changed with the entry's labels still included need nothing, since a later exclusion is a later record.
- **Graph, live.** Folders from `GET /me/mailFolders?includeHiddenFolders=false` and `childFolders`, paged through bound continuations, at most 200, depth 8; per included folder, `delta` from its cursor with `Prefer: odata.maxpagesize=100` and `$select=id,receivedDateTime,parentFolderId`. Every Graph request that names or returns a message id carries `Prefer: IdType="ImmutableId"`: delta, `GET /me/messages/{id}` and its `$value` (G6). A step with effects first writes its candidates' claims (`importing` for additions not held, `checking` for removals); then lists the folders (the ancestry of this step, read after every claim write) and reads the excluded well-known folders by their lookups. **A removal** then reads the message's current `parentFolderId` once, and commits `deleting` only when that read answers `ErrorItemNotFound` or names one of the excluded well-known folders itself, which cannot be moved, so one read at one instant proves it (spike item 5); a parent anywhere else (included, `unknown`, or a folder under an excluded one) is no evidence, and the claim goes back `stored` (I2); unless that parent is a folder this step's listing includes and this step's `sync` read showed `live` (its first round completed before the message's read, K2), the step first writes the claim's `away#` entry, naming the copy with a fresh generation (K1), so the copy is checked again ("Graph's folder changes", J2), and the effect is terminal only once both writes succeeded. **An addition** from the live stream (a delta change after its folder's first round) first reads its `parentFolderId` after the listing and before `$value`: a parent the listing excludes is `excluded` with no body read, one it does not place is not terminal, and only an included one is fetched (J7, choice 50); every addition, live or listed by a round, reads its `$value`, then its `parentFolderId` after the body and before `Store`: stored when this step's listing includes that folder; `excluded`, the body dropped, when the listing excludes it; a parent the listing does not place (`unknown`) discards the observation, not terminal. **No folder's ancestry is evidence for a deletion** (I2): ancestry decides only which folders are read and whether an addition is stored, and both are repaired add-only. So a folder moved before the listing is seen; one moved after it is a later event the next discovery finds ("Graph's folder changes"), and a message's own move is a later delta change. An `@removed` for a message held is a candidate only.
- **Graph's folder changes** (G6, I2). `sync.folders` keeps, for each folder listed, its HMAC, whether it is included, `cursor` (it was once read) and `live` (its first round completed, so its `deltaLink` reports every later removal from it, K2). **A folder newly included or reparented** gets an add-only enumeration: a fresh unfiltered delta round of that folder, under the admission rule, whose imports take over the `excluded` and `gone` claims that a stale listing or an earlier deletion left (H4). **A folder that becomes excluded or `unknown`** imports nothing and starts nothing: its place is not evidence, so its messages' copies stay until each message is deleted for good or moved into an excluded well-known folder itself, or until their retention ends (choice 48); its entry and `fold#` stay while it is listed. **Such copies are still checked (J2).** A folder with `cursor` keeps its delta read for removals only (its additions are not imported), so a message deleted for good there, or moved on, is a removal candidate decided by its own evidence as above. A message whose removal's evidence placed it outside the folders included and `live`, in a folder that had a `cursor`, in one that never had one, or in an included one whose first round has not completed (K2), has its `away#` entry; at most hourly a mailbox (`sync.away.started_at`), a pass lists those entries in key order, 100 a page from `sync.away.after`, and puts each through the deletion order ("Deletions"), its own `checking` write first and the entry read after it: `ErrorItemNotFound`, or the message itself in an excluded well-known folder, deletes the copy and then the entry it read; a parent in a folder included and `live` in the step's `sync` read sends the claim back `stored` and then deletes the entry it read; a parent included but not yet `live`, or anywhere else, writes the entry again with a fresh generation and then sends the claim back `stored` (K1, K2). Every deletion of an entry is `DeleteEntryAt` at the version and value read, so a newer entry (a restored copy moved away again, or one refreshed in place) survives a stale cleanup (K1). **The pass's share (K4).** While a pass is due and unfinished, each step gives it at least half of the Graph requests the step spends after its fixed calls, before live work takes the rest, and either takes what the other leaves; a step with no live work gives it all. A pass whose position is lost starts again from the first entry, at one more check an entry. So a message kept away and then deleted for good is mirrored by its folder's delta within a poll, or by the next pass that reaches its entry: a poll spends at most 170 Graph requests (the bucket's 10, then 40 a minute over four minutes), of which four steps' fixed calls take about 48 (F, "Units and progress"), so a due pass makes at least 60 checks a poll under a steady live backlog and about 120 on a quiet mailbox, and passes a mailbox of up to 2,400 such copies within about four and a half hours under that backlog (an hour before it starts, then 40 polls) or about three when quiet, longer in proportion when discovery makes F larger (K4); and in any case by its copy's retention's end (choice 48, amended). **A folder with `cursor` that a complete discovery no longer lists** (deleted for good, or emptied from deleted items) starts a reconciliation of the mailbox's Graph claims ("Resets"), whose evidence for its messages is `ErrorItemNotFound`; once that reconciliation completes, its `fold#` and then its entry are deleted (a cut between the two leaves a `cursor` with no `fold#`, which reconciles once more, harmlessly). A folder moved back out of deleted items finds its messages' copies kept (T140).
- **IMAP, live.** `LIST` (with `SPECIAL-USE` when offered); per included folder `EXAMINE`; a changed `UIDVALIDITY` is a reset of that folder ("Resets"). New messages: `UID FETCH <cursor UIDNEXT>:<current UIDNEXT − 1>` only when the interval is not empty, in slices of at most 50 UIDs (first their sizes and dates, then each body after its claim), responses outside the slice refused; the folder's `uidnext` moves past the last UID whose effect is terminal (G3). **Presence**, at most hourly a folder (`presence_checked_at`): the folder's claims listed by their prefix (scope = folder and `UIDVALIDITY`), then `UID SEARCH UID <their UIDs>` in commands of at most 10,000 UIDs; each command completed with its tagged `OK`, under the `UIDVALIDITY` the claims were made under, is evidence for the UIDs it asked and did not return; a command that failed or was cut is evidence for nothing (T37). UIDs are never reused under one `UIDVALIDITY` (RFC 9051), so a UID absent from a completed search was expunged from that folder, whatever happened in other sessions meanwhile.
- **Cursor writes.** At most two a step, each at the version read: the unit's start, when the step begins a unit, before any effect of it (H3); and at the end of each step that changed anything, each changed `fold#` entry and the `sync` entry. A step starts no effect once less than ten seconds of its `IngestDeadline` remain, so the end write has its time. A step that read nothing new writes nothing but the schedule.
- **Units and progress** (G3, H3). A unit is what the cursor moves past at once: a Gmail history record, a Graph delta page, an IMAP UID slice. An effect is terminal when its claim is `stored`, `excluded` or `gone`; when nothing was to be done; when the provider answered definitively that the message does not exist (Gmail's or Graph's `ErrorItemNotFound` 404 at the import's fetch, a UID absent from the slice's `UID FETCH`: the claim written `gone`); or when a bound skipped the message (by a known size, with no claim, decided again the same way if the unit is read again; by bytes read, its claim written `excluded` with the reason; counted). `ErrClaimed`, a claim found `importing`, `checking` or `deleting` and younger than `ClaimStale`, a failed `Mark`, a transient answer (429, 5xx, a timeout, a reset) and a call cut by the step's deadline are not, and the cursor stays before that unit. The next step takes the unit again; a claim left by a dead worker is taken over once older than `ClaimStale` (two minutes), so a unit waits at most that plus a poll. **Progress inside a unit lives in the claims, bound to the unit as observed.** Before its first effect a step writes the unit's start (`sync.unit`, `fold#.unit`): its provider identity (the Gmail record's id; the HMAC of the Graph continuation the page was read from; the IMAP folder, `UIDVALIDITY` and UID range), the `digest` (an HMAC of the items and changes the page as read lists), a fresh random `nonce`, and the time. Only a claim write that leaves the claim terminal (`stored`, `excluded`, `gone`) carries `checked_in = nonce`; a write that opens an effect (`importing`, `checking`, `deleting`) clears it (I1); a reconciliation's check of the same claim uses `reconciled_in` and carries `checked_in` through its `checking` write and its return to `stored`, so neither pass erases the other's proof of a terminal claim neither changed (J3). The hourly away check (K5) has no slot: its `checking` write and its return of the same copy, under the same message identifier, to `stored` carry both slots unchanged, and any other outcome of it clears both, so an away check that changes nothing erases neither proof, and one that deletes or meets a new copy erases both. A retry reads the unit again: when its identity and digest are the ones stored, an item whose claim is terminal **and** holds the unit's nonce is not asked of the provider again, because that terminal write followed evidence read after the start write, which came after the read of every event the digest names, and anything else is a later unit; a claim in any other state is decided by its state whatever it holds ("Effect boundaries": young, the unit waits; stale, taken over and its evidence read again; `deleting`, finished), so a cut after a claim's opening write and before its evidence never counts as done. When identity or digest differs (a page replayed with a later change), the proof starts again under a new nonce. **A bound found by bytes** (a body past 25 MiB, a structure past the preflight) is met after the import's claim, so that claim is written `excluded` with its reason (`too_large`, `too_complex`) and the nonce, and a retry skips it like any terminal claim, never fetching that body again (I1); a skip by a known size reads no body and is decided again the same way at no provider cost. No clock is compared, so neither skew nor a short first attempt keeps an item from counting. So every attempt after the start write keeps what it finished, and a unit of any size completes, whatever reconciliation runs beside it. Counted explicitly (J3, K6): over k steps a unit of A additions and R removals or checks makes at most c·A + R + k·(F + c) provider calls, where c is what one addition asks (Gmail's `messages.get`: 1; Graph: 3 for a live addition, its parent before and after `$value`, and 2 for a round's; IMAP's fetch: 1), a removal or a check asks one, F is a step's fixed calls (a Gmail history page: 1; a Graph delta page, the nine well-known lookups and discovery's D requests, one per page of the root listing and one per page of `childFolders` of each listed folder that has subfolders, at most 200 folders to depth 8, so 10 + D, 12 for a mailbox whose folders fit one root page and one parent; an IMAP `EXAMINE` and the slice's size fetch: 2), and k·c counts the item a cut can leave between its opening write and its terminal one, whose reads its replay asks again whole; a reconciliation's walk the same, its claim page counted in F (T156, T170, T179, T189); a unit whose page changes on every read restarts its proof each time, and costs again only the checks of messages held, since an addition already `stored` asks nothing of the provider. A unit that cannot be read at all within its byte bound even at the smallest request the provider allows (a Gmail history page at `maxResults=1`; a Graph page, whose continuation cannot be made smaller) is a reset of that scope ("Resets"), shown as "Catching up" (T123, T136).

### Folders

One precedence table per provider; exclusion wins over inclusion:

| Provider | Excluded (wins) | Included | Not read at all |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gmail (labels on the message now) | `SPAM`, `TRASH`, `DRAFT`, `CHAT`; `SENT` without `INBOX` | Anything else: `INBOX`, `IMPORTANT`, `STARRED`, `CATEGORY_*`, user labels, no label (archived) | |
| Graph (by opaque id and ancestry) | The folders that `GET /me/mailFolders/{name}` returns for `junkemail`, `deleteditems`, `sentitems`, `drafts`, `outbox`, `conversationhistory`, `syncissues`, `recoverableitemsdeletions`, `scheduled`, and every descendant | `inbox`, `archive`, `clutter`, every folder the person made outside the excluded subtrees | Search folders |
| IMAP (`SPECIAL-USE`, RFC 6154) | `\Junk`, `\Trash`, `\Sent`, `\Drafts`, and their children | `INBOX`, `\Archive`, every other selectable folder | `\All`, `\Flagged` (virtual), `\NoSelect`, `\NonExistent`. Without `SPECIAL-USE`: the pinned names per host, compared after decoding |

- A Gmail message with `SENT` and `INBOX` (sent to oneself) is read (choice 16). Drafts and the providers' system folders are excluded because decision 9 excludes sent mail and drafts are unsent mail (choice 26, to confirm with the owner).
- **Graph roles** come only from the well-known lookups, by opaque id, never from a display name, so a renamed or localised system folder is classified correctly; the ancestry is the `parentFolderId` of a listing read in the same step, after that step's claim writes (G6). A folder whose ancestry is not complete (a parent not listed) is `unknown`: not imported, and a message found in it is not evidence of anything (T119). A folder's ancestry is never evidence for a deletion: only a message's own place in an excluded well-known folder, or its absence, is (I2, choice 48).
- **Discovery that is incomplete** (over 200 folders or depth 8, a listing that failed, a continuation refused) is `partial_folders`, shown: folders not listed are not imported, and nothing is deleted because its folder was not listed.
- **IMAP folders that disappear** (G5): a folder missing from a complete `LIST` is marked `missing_since`, and missing again from a complete `LIST` at least an hour later makes its claims candidates; neither `LIST` nor the stored time is evidence. In the step that deletes, the exact folder name and `UIDVALIDITY` each claim holds (sealed in the claim, not taken from a `fold#` entry) are put to the server: `EXAMINE <name>` answered with a tagged `OK` and a `UIDVALIDITY` greater than the claim's proves every UID of the claim's epoch gone (RFC 9051 requires a greater value when earlier UIDs do not persist); an equal one sends the claims to the ordinary presence search; a smaller one is no evidence and is logged as `uidvalidity_decreased`; `EXAMINE` answered `NO [NONEXISTENT]` (RFC 9051's response code) proves the folder, and so every UID of that name's epoch, gone, since a folder made again under the same name must take a greater `UIDVALIDITY`; any other `NO`, a `BAD`, a `BYE`, a timeout or a missing code is no evidence, and the copies stay. A host whose spike shows it does not send `[NONEXISTENT]` keeps a vanished folder's copies until their retention ends (choice 37). A renamed folder is a deleted one and a new one (choice 20).

### Storing

- **One path for every kind.** `mailboxes.Service.Import(ctx, box, occurrence, msg)` runs ADR 0026's sequence from its step 2.1 under the step's context, with a provider ingress: no SMTP quota is charged and no forwarding confirmation is looked for; DKIM checked and parsed as delivery does, the envelope sender empty, `StoredMeta.Source` (`gmail`, `outlook`, `imap`) and `StoredMeta.Origin` (`live`, `history`) set.
- **The claim** is ADR 0026's, named by the occurrence ("Records and layout"), with two changes to `inbox.Writer`: a provider claim lives until the copy's expiry plus a day (the claim takes its lifetime from the caller), so a message held is never copied twice; and **a claim taken over** (older than `ClaimStale`) first reads the deletion mark (ADR 0028) of the identifier it fixed, and when the mark is there writes nothing of the message and marks the claim stored, so a copy the person deleted while its first writer was stopped is not written again (T121). The second change applies to forwarded mail too, where the same window exists today. **Two more changes close that window for good (G1)**, because the deletion mark is swept `SweepGrace` (three minutes) after the deletion began, before a late takeover may come: a person's delete (`Reader.Delete`, both routes) first reads the message's claim through `StoredMeta.Claim` and, when it is still `importing` and names this message, writes it `stored` at the version read, retried as `markGone` retries, and deletes nothing until that write succeeded or found the claim `stored` or naming another message; and `Writer.Store`, after `PutItem`, reads the message's deletion mark again and, when it is there, deletes what it wrote through `Reader.Delete` and marks the claim stored. The writer's read after `PutItem` is the fast path only: a writer stopped after its `PutItem` and before that read, or answered ambiguously and gone, never makes it (H1). **The durable path is the sweep's pass over the marks** (`sweepGone` in `internal/services/mailboxes/sweep.go`), which today deletes a marked message's `dec#` once more after `SweepGrace` and then the mark: from pull request 1 it deletes, by the mailbox and message identifiers the mark's key names and with no key of the mailbox, the raw object and the item (`DeleteMessage`, `ErrNotFound` counting as done), `txt#`, `confirm#` and `dec#` once more, and deletes the mark only once all of those succeeded; a failure leaves the mark for the next sweep. The invariant: the mark is written before the item is deleted; every writer that may still write the message took its claim before the delete's `stored` write and ends within its `IngestDeadline` plus `ClockSkew`, inside `SweepGrace`; so every late part lands before the sweep's pass, which deletes it, and the mark goes only after a pass that found nothing it could not delete; from then on the claim is `stored`, and no writer writes the message again. What this does not give: a late item can be listed from its `PutItem` until that pass (`SweepGrace` and the sweep's period, a few minutes), choice 41. The same holds for forwarded mail from the floor on; a release before the floor sweeps the mark without the second deletion, as today, and its window is the one that exists today. An ambiguous answer to the delete's claim write or to `PutItem` fails that request before anything is deleted, and the retry reads the claim again (T129, T130, T153).
- **Identifiers and dates.** Every message's identifier and `received_at` are from when Permaily copied it, as for forwarded mail, so ADR 0026's walk of the decision days from the mailbox's creation holds unchanged and ADR 0039's cursors see each copy once; the inbox lists history in the order it was copied, each message showing its own date (choice 28). Retention counts from the copy (choice 5).
- **Origin**: `live` when the message came from the live stream as new mail (Gmail `messageAdded` after the baseline, an IMAP UID at or above the folder's baseline, a Graph delta change after the folder's first round) or its provider date is at or after `connected_at`; `history` otherwise. Webhooks select only `live` (choice 7), so new mail found by the backfill or a resync during the first minutes still notifies. A listing and the live stream do not contend for one message where the provider can tell them apart (Gmail's `historyId` against the baseline, IMAP's disjoint UID ranges, "Bootstrap"); where it cannot (Graph's first round), the date decides, as choice 36 states (G9).
- **`ErrClaimed`** (another worker's younger claim) is not terminal: the step stops before that unit, and the next step finds it stored or takes the stale claim over ("Units and progress", G3).
- **Too large**: a known size over 25 MiB is not fetched; bytes read past 25 MiB are dropped, and that message's claim written `excluded` with the reason (I1); either is counted in the sync state ("2 messages were too large to copy") and the cursor moves past it (choice 8).
- **Too complex** (H7): before DKIM and the parser, a preflight scans the body buffer in place, allocating nothing: the top header block at most `MaxHeaderBytes` (256 KiB) and `MaxHeaderLines` (2,000 lines, K7) and each field at most `MaxHeaderFieldBytes` (16 KiB), and, following the multipart boundaries, at most `MaxMIMEParts` entities, each header block within `MaxHeaderBytes`; and in the body no run of CR, LF, SP and HTAB longer than `MaxBlankRun` (64 KiB), the most a DKIM body canonicalizer holds back before it writes (I4, choice 49). A message that exceeds any of them is not stored, its claim written `excluded` with the reason, counted as `too_complex` ("1 message couldn't be copied"), and the cursor moves past it (choice 43), so the existing multipart parser, which checks a part's header after `NextPart` built it (`internal/inbox/text.go`), and DKIM, which copies the whole header block before applying its five-signature limit, only ever see bounded headers.

### Memory

`providersync` holds one pool of its own, `ProviderBytes` (320 MiB, H7), beside SMTP's budget, which it does not touch. **The pool counts every allocation its stages make (G12, H7)**: a reservation is taken before anything of its class is read, sized to the class's measured worst case, and covers both the buffers handed out from it (fixed-capacity buffers that refuse to grow past it) and what the code the stage reuses allocates on its own (DKIM, the parser, `envelope.Seal`, the store's and the SDK's encoding), whose inputs the preflight and the codecs bound before they run; T146 and T161 measure each at its maximum input, each class on its own as well as all together (I4). ### Deletions

- **Evidence, per message, in the step that deletes, read after the claim's `checking` write.** Gmail: the message's current labels excluded, or a 404 for its id. Graph: its current `parentFolderId` equal to an excluded well-known folder itself (its id read by its lookup in that step; such a folder cannot be moved, so no ancestry is read, I2), or a 404 whose error code is `ErrorItemNotFound` for its immutable id, asked with `Prefer: IdType="ImmutableId"` under a token from the tenant's own endpoint (G6, G17); any other 404 code (a malformed id, a mailbox not enabled for REST, an online archive), a 403, a parent `unknown`, or a parent under an excluded folder rather than one of them is no evidence, and the spike records each (item 5); a parent in a folder included and `live` is a move: nothing happens; a parent elsewhere, or in an included folder whose first round has not completed, keeps the copy, named by its `away#` entry, which the hourly pass checks again (J2, K2). IMAP: a completed presence search that did not return its UID, a greater `UIDVALIDITY` for its folder, or `[NONEXISTENT]` for its exact folder ("Folders", G5). A missing report, a truncated listing, an incomplete resync, a cursor, a stored list, a folder listing or a stored time is never evidence.
- **The order** (G2): the claim written `checking` at the version read; the evidence; the claim written `deleting` at the `checking` version (the commit), or, when the evidence does not prove the message gone, written back `stored` with `checked_at` at that version; then `Reader.Delete` (ADR 0028: the mark first, then the raw object, the item, `txt#`, `confirm#` and `dec#`); then the claim written `gone` at its `deleting` version, never deleted, so its versions keep growing (H2); then any `away#` entry deleted by `DeleteEntryAt` at the version and value read after the `checking` write, only when it names this copy (K1). The invariant: the evidence was read after the claim's `checking` write, and the deletion commits at that write's version, so any import, restore or other check of that message either wrote the claim first (and the commit fails, the observation is discarded) or read the claim after the `checking` write and waits ("Effect boundaries"). A restore at the provider is therefore seen either by the evidence (nothing is deleted) or after the commit, as an event that waits while the claim is `deleting` and then takes the `gone` claim over and imports the message as a new copy. IMAP's evidence cannot be reversed (a UID is never reused, an epoch never returns), so its deletions may skip `checking` and commit `deleting` at the version read before the evidence. A step stopped after the commit leaves a `deleting` claim, which whoever finds it older than `ClaimStale` finishes: the rest of the copy deleted (`Reader.Delete` asked again finishes a delete stopped between the raw object and the item), then the claim written `gone`; so no copy is left readable without its raw object, and an event that waited then imports the message anew if the provider holds it (T122, T133). A step stopped before the commit leaves `checking`, which the next actor takes over and decides on its own evidence.
- **Moves.** Gmail's id and Graph's immutable id survive moves between included folders, which change nothing. In IMAP a move is an expunge from one folder and a new UID in another: the copy is deleted and a new one made, and the old copy's kept decisions are lost; two equal copies in two folders are two messages (choice 20, T36, T120). No content is ever compared to carry a message's identity.
- **Kept decisions** go with the copy, as for a person's delete: `Reader.Delete` deletes `dec#`. A strict hold of ADR 0031 for that message stays in its day's block, holding nothing, until its block's next write an hour after the copy's original expiry, exactly as after a person's delete; its earlier versions stay in the block's immutable history under the anchor's retention, sealed under a key derived from the root secret (choice 29, T89).
- **Receipts** keep naming the message, as they do for a message the person deleted: they record what left (choice 27).

### Resets

A reset (Gmail 404 on the history position; Graph `syncStateNotFound`, `resyncRequired` or 410 for a folder; IMAP `UIDVALIDITY` changed for a folder; a unit no smaller request can read, "Units and progress") is a resync of that scope. **A local checkpoint lost is a reset too (G4)**: a `sync` entry missing while the mailbox holds any provider claim (one `ListEntries` of `seen#p`, limit 1, at the step that finds it missing), or a folder whose entry in `sync.folders` says `cursor` and which has no `fold#` entry, starts the same resync; the step lists the mailbox's `fold#` entries (at most 200, two pages) and compares them with every `sync.folders` entry that expects one, whether or not discovery returns that folder now (H8). A folder listed with no `cursor` (an excluded folder, or one not yet baselined) is not a loss, and a folder neither lists is new, and only baselined. **A folder's first `fold#` and its expectation (I7).** The first `fold#` is written before the `sync` write that sets the folder's `cursor`, and no claim is made from that folder until a `sync` write carrying its `cursor` has succeeded; a step that finds a `fold#` whose folder's entry lacks `cursor` sets it in its next `sync` write before any effect of that folder, or deletes that `fold#` when a complete discovery no longer lists the folder. So every claim made from a folder's position was written after the folder's expectation was durable, and a `fold#` lost with any claim behind it is always expected; one lost before its expectation had no claim behind it. The scope of the reconciliation (step 3) is then the whole provider's claims: the mailbox's Gmail or Graph claims, or every IMAP claim of the mailbox, whatever folder and epoch.

1. A new baseline first (Gmail `getProfile`; a fresh unfiltered delta round for the folder; the new `UIDVALIDITY` and `UIDNEXT`), written with `phase: resyncing`; the live stream continues from it at once.
2. An add-only enumeration of the scope, beside the live stream: Gmail `messages.list` with `q=after:<now − retention − 1 day>`, its page token kept in the sync state so a cut resumes (a token that no longer works restarts the enumeration); Graph the new delta round; IMAP `UID SEARCH ALL`. Each listed message not held is admitted by the admission rule.
3. **A reconciliation** (G4), which does not depend on what the enumeration listed: `sync.reconcile` is written with its start time and a fresh `nonce` before its first check; every claim of the scope is then read in key order, a page of at most 100 at a time from `reconcile.after`, and each is checked by its own evidence through the deletion order ("Deletions"), as the quota allows, and deleted only on that evidence; each check's terminal write carries that nonce in `reconciled_in` (J3), and a claim found terminal with it is skipped (I7), so a reconciliation whose position was lost, or whose page write lost its version, repeats no provider call for a claim it already checked, whatever the clocks; a claim the live stream changed since (an import, a deletion or a restore, whose writes clear `reconciled_in`) is checked once more, and one a unit only checked again and found unchanged keeps it (J3). IMAP claims are grouped by the folder name and `UIDVALIDITY` sealed in each, so an epoch or folder no `fold#` names any more is still reached: one `EXAMINE` and the searches of "Folders" per group, where a greater `UIDVALIDITY` or `[NONEXISTENT]` is evidence for the whole group. It may run beside the enumeration; the order of the two does not matter, because neither reads the other's state.
4. When the enumeration has completed (the last page, the `deltaLink`, the tagged `OK`) and the reconciliation has passed its last claim, one write of `sync` clears `reconcile` and returns the phase to what it was; until then the person sees "Catching up", which is the reconciliation-incomplete state. A reset during a resync starts again from a new baseline and a new reconciliation. **Two recoveries, two costs (H8).** A reconciliation whose `reconcile` (its start and nonce) survives (a cut step, a page write that lost its version) resumes and asks the provider nothing again for a claim checked since it began. A `sync` entry lost meanwhile loses that start too: the new reconciliation's start is later than every check made before the loss, so it is a new full recheck and asks the provider again for each claim of the scope, once per loss (choice 44); only `checked_in` of a unit still in the cursors spares any of it.

Resyncs are rare (a Gmail position lasts at least a week, and a five-minute poll keeps it fresh), which is why their cost is not bounded further: a Gmail resync of a 20,000-message mailbox costs up to 400,000 units of evidence, about three hours of one mailbox's budget, spent in the background.

### History import

- **Order**: as the provider lists it; IMAP folders are taken a page at a time in turn, so one large folder does not consume the bounds. No order across folders or within Gmail's and Graph's listings is claimed.
- **Bounds a mailbox**: 20,000 messages admitted, 10 GiB, 24 hours of import time (choice 10), counted as examined, stored and skipped in the sync state. Reaching one ends the admission of history; the person sees how many were copied and that some were not ("Copied 20,000 messages from the last 90 days; some older ones weren't copied"), never a claim of which. Live sync is unaffected: the bounds never count or stop mail admitted as live, and a Graph first round still runs to its `deltaLink` ("Bootstrap", G9).
- **The backfill is add-only**: it never deletes.

|---|---|

| 5xx, a timeout, a reset, IMAP `BYE` or `UNAVAILABLE` | The same |

What these budgets do not hold, stated: two Permaily mailboxes of the same provider account (in two Permaily accounts) each have their own budget, and the provider answers 429 when they pass its limit; a schedule entry lost during backoff costs at most one early request, which the provider answers again (T127).

**Cold starts and calls outside a poll (G16).** Each per-enclave budget is a token bucket that starts empty when its enclave starts, fills at its rate and holds at most fifteen seconds of it (H12): a Gmail mailbox 40 units a second, at most 600; the Gmail imports and resyncs of a mailbox, charged to it and also to a bucket of 30 units a second holding at most 450; the Gmail project 8,000 units a second, at most 120,000; a Graph mailbox 40 requests a minute, at most 10. A bucket of rate r and capacity c spends at most c + r·W in any window W, so through one enclave a Gmail mailbox spends at most 3,000 units in any minute (2,400 sustained), the project at most 600,000, a Graph mailbox at most 410 requests in any ten minutes; two enclaves, however long either was idle and however they restart, stay under 6,000, 1,200,000 and 10,000. An enclave started again, or a replacement, spends nothing it did not earn since it started. Every outbound call, each retry and each continuation included, is charged before it is sent to the bucket of what it reaches: connecting's calls (Gmail's `getProfile` in the trip, Microsoft's token exchange) to the project bucket, an IMAP trial login to its failure domain's two sessions, a refresh to its mailbox. Concurrent repairs of one missing schedule cost at most one early poll per enclave, each charged to its bucket. Graph's per-tenant limits are not modelled: the provider's 429 rules them (choice 24, restated).

So does a contributor that no longer opens at all, for every kind, forwarding included (H5): its mailbox gone (`OpenForConnection`'s `ErrNotFound`, which today's `Recheck` skips), its message gone, or its mailbox no longer covered by the connection. A source that cannot be opened cannot have its policy checked, so the frozen body is never sent once any of its sources is unavailable; the worker's same path makes a new notice from what still opens (choice 46). **At admission (I3).** `Recheck` before `Admit` stays the preliminary check; the same all-contributor check runs again inside `Admit`, after its read of the connection's authority and basis and before it registers the permit (`Admit` gains the match): each message of `Match.Messages` opened through `OpenForConnection` and read. A contributor confirmed unavailable makes `Admit` answer the match changed, which the worker handles as `SkipChanged`, the existing rewind. The permit's `WriteBy` still counts from that subscription read (`webhookWriteBy(sub.ReadAt, …)`, `oauth.ConnectionFresh`, five seconds), which comes before the contributor check, so the check is inside the permit's freshness bound, and a check that does not finish within it registers no permit and defers the notice (J6, below). Invariant: a frozen body is sent only when every source it was derived from opened after the read that admits the attempt; a change after that read is inside an attempt already admitted (ADR 0039's boundary, unchanged; choice 46). **A deleted source is unavailable from its mark (J1).** A person's delete commits at its deletion mark ("Effect boundaries"), before the item goes, and a late item can be written under a surviving mark (choice 41), so reading the item is not enough: the check reads, for each contributor, after its mailbox opens and before its item, the message's mark (`GetEntry` of `GoneKey` in `GonePartition`, through a new `inbox.Reader.Gone`), and a mark there is a confirmed change, as the message's `ErrNotFound` is; a failed mark read is `ErrSourceUnavailable`. The order matters: the mark is written before the item is deleted, and the sweep deletes the item before the mark, after which nothing writes the item again ("Storing"), so a deletion committed before the admitting read is seen either as its mark or, once swept, as the item's absence. The evaluation that makes a new match (the arrival scan and the late fallback) reads the mark of each message before it enters `Match.Messages` and passes a marked one over as not found, so the rewind does not make the same notice again from the marked item, and the other sources still notify. **A mirrored deletion is unavailable from its commit (K3).** A provider deletion commits at its claim's `deleting` write, before `Reader.Delete` writes the mark, so for a provider message the check reads, after its mark and its item, the claim its `StoredMeta.Claim` names (a key under `seen#p`), through a new `inbox.Reader.Mirrored`: the contributor is a confirmed change when that claim is `deleting`, `gone` or `excluded`, names another message identifier (a later copy took a `gone` claim over), or is not found; a failed claim read is `ErrSourceUnavailable`, as a failed mark read is. The mark is still read first and the transient contract is unchanged. The order is sound because a claim only moves forward for one message identifier: once it is `deleting` for that identifier, every later read shows `deleting`, `gone`, or a takeover under a fresh identifier, so a deletion committed before the admitting read is seen as its claim, its mark or the item's absence, whichever the check reaches. The evaluation reads the same claim before a provider message enters `Match.Messages`, and passes it over as it passes a marked one. This covers a live removal's, the hourly away pass's and a reconciliation's deletions alike, since all commit at that write. A forwarded message's `StoredMeta.Claim` never names a `seen#p` key (forwarded claims are `seen#` and a hex digest), and one stored before has none, so forwarded contributors read no claim and behave as today. Invariant: a frozen body is sent only when no contributor had a deletion committed before the admitting read, whether that commit is a person's mark or a mirrored deletion's `deleting` write. **Within the permit's time (J6).** `Admit`'s check runs under a context that ends one second before the permit's `WriteBy`, so a check that passes leaves the send at least that second; it opens each mailbox of the match once (at most five: a connection opens only its account's mailboxes, `OpenForConnection`, and an account has at most `mailboxes.MaxMailboxes`, K9) and reuses that open for its contributors, since the open follows the admitting read, and reads marks, items and provider claims at most four at a time, about 65 store reads for a full match of 16 (`webhook.MatchMessagesMax`). A check that does not finish inside that context, or any `ErrSourceUnavailable` in `Recheck` or `Admit`, registers no permit and sends nothing: the worker writes the frozen notice's `NextAt` a minute later, never past its `CreatedAt` plus `GiveUpAfter`, and keeps its identifier and body, instead of leaving it due now; a candidate not yet frozen has no identity to keep and is rewound as a changed match, and the worker writes into the connection's state `HoldUntil`, a minute later (K9): until then `evaluate` does not run, so the arrivals wait behind the rewound progress, and the wakes evaluation asks for (its own and the rewind's `dueNow`) move to that minute; a turn that runs earlier anyway (a wake set before, a fresh due hint, a restart) evaluates nothing and checks no source, while a scheduled run and an in-flight notice's `NextAt` keep their own times (choice 52). Invariant: no match is evaluated again, and no source checked for it, before the minute its last failed check set, whatever wakes the connection. A `Start` refused after a check that passed keeps today's immediate retry (ADR 0039's N2). **Confirmed changes only (I5).** A contributor is confirmed unavailable only when its mailbox answers `ErrNotFound` or `source_closed`, the connection no longer covers it, or its message answers `ErrNotFound`. Any other error opening or reading a contributor, in `Recheck` or in `Admit`, is `ErrSourceUnavailable`: the worker sends nothing, keeps the frozen identifier and body, and tries again a minute later, within `GiveUpAfter`, by the deferral below (J6); today every `Recheck` error goes to `forget`, which pull request 1 changes for this one. Invariant: a frozen notice is replaced only when one of its sources is known to have changed, so a lost acknowledgement followed by a transient read error sends the same identifier and bytes again (ADR 0039's K37 and K38). Pull request 1 checks that `Match.Messages` names every message the body was derived from, and makes it so if not. Mixed connections: a closed source's messages are left out as withheld and the connection's progress moves past them, so its forwarded notices go on. Candidate selection, in both the arrival scan (`arrivalSpan`) and the late fallback, skips a message whose `StoredMeta.Origin` is `history` before any evaluation (T142).

The routes that may ask the model move to `AccessModel` (`internal/httpapi/mailboxes.go`'s search with a condition on meaning and its matches route; `internal/httpapi/rules_preview.go`'s criteria and removal previews); the webhook worker's recheck already opens through `OpenForConnection` before each send, so work queued while a use was open is not sent once it is closed (T83). Forwarding mailboxes (a record with no `kind`, or `forwarding`) are unaffected. A local preview refused is not a disclosure and makes no receipt; a connection's read that skips a provider mailbox records it as withheld, as for a mailbox the connection does not cover. One test runs every route on the mux and every MCP tool over a provider mailbox in an image with both uses closed, with a model and a vendor egress that fail on any call; a route added without a case in that test fails it (T116).

1. **Uses.** Which user-facing features may use provider mail: showing the person their own sealed copy; rules the person writes; the default rule; connections to vendors the person approves; webhooks; previews. Which are "user-facing features prominent in the requesting application's user interface" under Limited Use, and how the app presents each. The answer becomes `providers.uses` per kind.

4. **Onward transfers to vendors.** Whether disclosing approved parts of Gmail messages to a vendor the person connects is a transfer Limited Use allows, and what the vendor must be told or bound to.
5. **Retention.** The sealed copy's retention for provider mail (choice 5 assumes forwarding's 7 to 365 days from the copy), claims, receipts that outlive the copy, and sealed backups.

9. **DKIM lookups** (G19). ## The threat model

- **What the host sees**: that the enclave connects to `gmail.googleapis.com`, `graph.microsoft.com` or an IMAP host, when, for how long and how many bytes each way, and the names it resolves. It cannot open the TLS. It can delay, drop or misroute connections (polls back off; mail waits at the provider); it cannot make the enclave send a credential anywhere a server without a valid certificate for the policy's name could read it.
- **What the storage's reader sees**: the mailbox record's readable fields (its owning account, as today; its kind, `connected_at`, `history_days`); the number of claims per scope (per IMAP folder and `UIDVALIDITY`), their names (HMACs) and write times; how many Graph copies are kept away (`away#`, J2); the schedule's keys and timing; the sizes and timing of every write, and so how often a mailbox changes and when it is deleted. Never the provider account (sealed in the record), an address, content, a token or a folder name. No entry joins two mailboxes of the same provider account.
- **What the storage's writer can do**: delete or put back entries. A grant: the mailbox reads nothing ("Connect again") or reads with an older token of the same account and scopes. A cursor or the sync state: work done again, or a resync. A claim: a message copied a second time, a copy the person deleted copied again from the provider while it is still there (choice 11), or a message restored at the provider not copied again until the claim ends. A schedule entry: polls missed or doubled. An `away#` entry: a kept-away copy not checked again until its retention ends, or one checked needlessly (J2). It cannot make the enclave delete a copy without the provider's evidence, roll back the mailbox record (strict), widen the egress or open a use (both compiled): a claim's `deleting` value is written only after evidence that its copy is gone and names that one copy, so putting back an old one only finishes a deletion already proved (G2); a `checking` value put back is decided again on fresh evidence.
- **Refresh tokens and app passwords never leave the enclave**: sealed under the mailbox's key, sent only to the provider's token endpoint or the listed IMAP host over the checked egress, never logged (the scrubber drops their shapes, T46), never in an answer. After a disconnect they stay valid at the provider until the person removes Permaily or they end, unreadable to anyone.
- **The operator** registered the applications and knows their client secrets; with a secret alone nobody reads a mailbox. The operator can turn a provider off (denial of service, shown). The operator cannot add scopes (compiled requests, a fail-closed allowlist), cannot open providers or their uses (compiled), and is trusted, as stated in "Launch: one barrier", to have stopped every release before the floor.
- **A compromised Google or Microsoft account of the person**: whoever holds it reads the mail at the provider already; through Permaily they can put mail in (by receiving it) and delete Permaily's copy (by deleting it at the provider), which mirrored deletions make possible by design (decision 10). Receipts of what already left stay.
- **A hostile or compromised IMAP server** on the list: bounded and preflighted reads, a fuzzed parser, a command allowlist on the wire, TLS verified; it can send hostile mail, as any sender can, and claim any message expunged, which deletes only Permaily's copy.
- **Google's testing mode**: at most 100 test users added by hand (so Google, and whoever runs that console, know them); refresh tokens that end after seven days.
- **Microsoft's work tenants**: an administrator may need to approve; a tenant's policies may end tokens at any time; publisher verification is needed for many tenants.
- **Abuse from Permaily's address**: polls bounded per failure domain and per enclave; IMAP a closed list; a person cannot make the enclave connect anywhere they choose.
- **Public telemetry**: `/status` publishes the configuration only, no counts (choice 25); counts per kind and outcome go to the enclave's log once an hour, which the host reads anyway as connection timing.
- docs/threat-model.md has a short section pointing here; pull request 9 fills it in.

## What /status and permaily-verify show

`/status` gains `providers` (`attestation.ProvidersStatus`): `open`, `determination` and `uses` per kind (compiled); `ready` (joint readiness, "Launch: one barrier"); `kinds` and, for each, whether its secret is set; `scopes` (the requested sets and the granted allowlists, exactly as compiled); `read_only: true`; `push: false`; `revokes: false`; `poll_seconds: 300`; `history_days: [0, 30, 90]`; `client_ids`; `egress` (the complete `egress.Policy`: each destination with port, methods, path templates and HTTP version, and the IMAP hosts with port 993 and `tls: "implicit"`); `token_storage: "sealed_per_mailbox"`; on a staging image, the number of test subjects. Never a count, an address, an account, a mailbox identifier or a provider's error text.

`permaily-verify` prints the section and fails when the published `egress` differs in any way from its pinned policy (a missing, extra or duplicate destination, a port, a method, a path or a version), a requested or allowed scope is not in its pinned read-only set, `read_only` is false, `push` is true, `poll_seconds` is under 300, or `open`, `determination` and `uses` differ from its pinned determination (`open: false` for every release before the opening one) (T53, T128). `ready` is reported, not pinned: it is run-time state.

## Choices made for the owner (to confirm)

Numbers 1 to 27 keep their meaning from the previous revision where they still apply; "changed" and "withdrawn" say what the simplification did.

1. **Client identifiers in the image**, secrets through the operator's route. *Alternative*: both through the route, as sign-in.
2. **Separate Google Cloud project and Microsoft application for mail**, not the sign-in ones.
3. **Changed. A provider account connects to one mailbox per Permaily account**, best effort; two Permaily accounts may connect the same one. *Alternative*: a deployment-wide strict record per provider account, for a guarantee across accounts.
4. **A move to spam or trash is a deletion** for Permaily, and the copy goes.
5. **Changed. Retention as forwarding's** (7 to 365 days, 90 by default), counted from when Permaily copied the message; history choices only up to the retention.
6. **Every Gmail mailbox reconnects weekly during testing mode**, with a notice each time.
7. **History imports do not notify webhooks**; mail from the live stream, or dated at or after connecting, does, including delayed mail and mail found by a resync.
8. **Messages over 25 MiB are skipped and counted**, as the MX refuses them.
9. **Changed. Concurrency per enclave**: 8 polls, 1 per account, 4 IMAP sessions, 2 to one IMAP failure domain.
10. **Import bounds**: 20,000 messages, 10 GiB, 24 hours a mailbox; how many were copied shown, not which.
11. **Changed. A person's delete in Permaily deletes only Permaily's copy**; the copy is not imported again while its claim lives (the copy's retention), under honest storage.
12. **The IMAP list**: nine host entries under six brands and six failure domains, each kept only if the spike confirms it.
13. **No fresh proof to connect**; the session's standing read again immediately before the commit; a fresh proof to disconnect (as today).
14. **What disconnecting does to the copy**: deleted, as forwarding.
15. **A broken grant is told by mail once per break**, and shown in the app.
16. **Sent mail sent to oneself is read** in Gmail (it carries `INBOX`).
17. **Changed. No strict record for synchronisation**: cursors are sealed entries, because no cursor authorises a deletion.
18. **Changed (decision 13). Every provider kind dormant until the opening release**, which opens the three together.
19. **Changed. The uses of provider mail compiled per kind from the owner's determination**, enforced at the mailboxes' access table.
20. **Changed. IMAP moves and renames are a deletion and a new copy**: the old copy's kept decisions are lost; two copies in two folders are two messages.
21. **Changed. No revocation call at disconnect**: the token becomes unreadable and is deleted; the person removes Permaily at the provider. *Alternative*: a best-effort revocation at Google, which also ends every other Permaily mailbox of that Google account and can end a grant made again within seconds.
22. **Changed. A floor and an opening release**, not a compatibility release: providers dormant from the first release carrying their code; the opening release only once older releases' keys are removed.
23. **DNS from the host**, untrusted; WebPKI identity, not address, is what is guaranteed. This amends the design brief, which asked for DNS over HTTPS. *Alternative*: the providers' names over DNS over HTTPS, which adds a resolver to the egress and still attests no location.
24. **Changed. Fixed per-enclave quota budgets**, set so two enclaves together stay under each provider's limit, as buckets that start empty (G16) and hold at most fifteen seconds of their rate, so a mailbox's Gmail spend through one enclave is at most 3,000 units in any minute, 2,400 sustained (amended, H12). *Alternative*: a shared budget in the storage, written per call.
25. **Changed. No provider counts on `/status`**; counts in the log.
26. **Drafts and the providers' system folders are excluded** with sent mail (decision 9 names spam, trash and sent).
27. **Receipts outlive a mirrored deletion**, as they outlive a person's delete.
28. **New. History is listed in the order it was copied**, each message dated with its own date; identifiers are never before the mailbox's creation.
29. **New. A mirrored deletion leaves ADR 0031's strict hold** for the message until the copy's original expiry plus an hour, holding nothing, as a person's delete does.
30. **New; amended (G14). IMAP deletions are mirrored within about an hour** (the presence check), within about 90 minutes for a mailbox of 200 folders; Gmail's and Graph's within a poll.
31. **New; amended (G4, H16). A resync runs when the provider loses the position or a local checkpoint is lost** (a `sync` entry, or a `fold#` entry `sync.folders` expects), never on a schedule.
32. **New. A sign-out that lands in the last moment of connecting does not undo it**; the mailbox can be disconnected.
33. **New. Staging opens providers only for listed test subjects**, with every use closed.
34. **New (G10); amended (H9). That no runtime admitted before the pre-floor keys were retired still runs is the operator's assertion**, checked against AWS's control plane (instance launch or start times after each key's `ScheduleKeyDeletion`, its state `PendingDeletion` or deleted), not proved by attestation. *Alternative*: a data-access fence in the storage that every release before the floor would have had to honour, which those releases do not have.
35. **New (G10). Joint readiness holds at every connect**: while either client secret is missing no mailbox of any kind can be connected; a kind whose secret is removed after opening is `provider_off`, and the others keep polling. *Alternative*: a stored "opened once" flag after which kinds are offered one by one, which is state a rollback or a storage writer could change.
36. **New (G9). What a provider's date cannot prove is not copied, and said**: Graph and IMAP mail added between connecting and a folder's first baseline (seconds) or during Graph's first round, and any provider's mail added while a reset's position was lost, is admitted only when dated inside the history window; a backdated message there is not copied, and Graph's first round decides its origin by date. Gmail's baseline is taken in the trip before `connected_at` and sealed in the record, so Gmail has no such gap, and its backfill leaves to the live stream every message changed after it (amended, H6). *Alternative*: a baseline for every IMAP folder inside the connect request, which makes connecting as slow as listing 200 folders.
37. **New (G5). A vanished IMAP folder's copies are deleted only on `EXAMINE`'s `[NONEXISTENT]` or a greater `UIDVALIDITY`**; on a host that sends neither, they stay until their retention ends.
38. **New (G2). A deletion stopped after its commit is finished**, and a message restored after that commit is copied again as a new message, without its kept decisions, as a message restored after a completed deletion is.
39. **New (G7). Two bits per kind**: a legal determination finer than "model" and "vendors" per kind is compiled as the closed bit.
40. **New (G21). The seven-day staging run needs a staging target that is not production's instance** for that week (a second instance for the week, or a staging week with production down, as docs/operations.md says staging runs today); the owner picks which.
41. **New (H1). A message written late, after the person deleted it, may be listed again for a few minutes**, from its writer's `PutItem` until the sweep's pass over the deletion's mark after `SweepGrace`, which deletes it for good; forwarded mail too, from the floor on. A webhook notice never derives from such an item: the admission check and the evaluation read the deletion mark first (amended, J1). *Alternative*: the item written in one transaction with a check of its claim, which `internal/store` has no primitive for.
42. **New (H4). A Graph folder moved into an included place in the moment after a step's last read** may lose the copies that step deleted on evidence read before the move; the folder's next discovery copies them again, within the admission rule, as new copies without their kept decisions (as choice 38). *No longer arises after the fifth review*: Graph deletes on no folder's ancestry (choice 48), so a folder's move deletes nothing.
43. **New (H7). A provider message whose headers or MIME structure exceed the preflight's bounds is not copied, and counted** (`too_complex`), as one too large is (choice 8). *Alternative*: stored whole, unparsed and without DKIM, as forwarding stores a message past them with `Partial`, which needs a second storing path.
44. **New (H8). A `sync` entry lost during a reconciliation repeats that reconciliation's provider calls** once per loss; a lost page write repeats none.
45. **New (H11). A reauth notice found by the repair after its worker crashed is queued with the finder's time**, so it may be sent up to about 47 hours after the break; a break found after 23 hours is shown in the app only.
46. **New (H5). A frozen webhook notice any of whose sources no longer opens is remade, never sent as frozen**, for every kind: a forwarding mailbox disconnected, or a message deleted, between the freeze and a retry changes the notice that goes (a new identifier, from what still opens) or sends none.
47. **New (H13). No callback clears the trip's cookie**; it expires at its ten minutes, and a consumed entry makes it inert.
48. **New (I2). Graph deletes a copy only on its message's own evidence**: `ErrorItemNotFound`, or the message itself in junk, deleted items or another excluded well-known folder. A Graph folder moved under one of them (a folder the person deleted, which Outlook keeps in deleted items) is no longer read, and its copies stay, readable and open to the uses the image allows, until its messages are deleted for good (deleted items emptied, the folder deleted, or a message deleted there) or their retention ends; a deletion for good there is mirrored (amended, J2): by the folder's delta, still read for removals once the folder had a `cursor`, within a poll; otherwise by the hourly pass over copies kept away, which is given at least half of each step's Graph requests after its fixed calls while it is due, within about four and a half hours for a mailbox of up to 2,400 such copies under a steady live load and about three when quiet (amended, K4); a message moved into an included folder whose first round has not completed stays watched by that pass until it has (K2); and at the latest when the copy's retention ends; a message moved into a subfolder of junk or deleted items is kept the same way; and a message that arrives in a folder at the moment the folder moves under one of them may be copied, and notified, as mail of an included folder. This narrows choice 4 for Graph folder moves. *Alternative*: read the whole ancestry again around every message read, which costs up to eight requests a message and still leaves a window, because Graph's folder listing is not one snapshot.
49. **New (I4). A provider message whose body holds a run of blank lines and spaces longer than 64 KiB is too complex** (counted, not copied, as in choice 43), so a DKIM body canonicalizer holds back a bounded amount; and a DNS-over-HTTPS answer for DKIM is read through a 256 KiB bound, for forwarded mail too. Amended (K7): so is a message whose top header block has more than 2,000 lines, so DKIM's header parse holds a bounded number of strings; and a DNS-over-HTTPS reply that is not valid UTF-8 is a temporary failure, for forwarded mail too. *Alternative*: a DKIM body canonicalizer of Permaily's own with a bounded buffer, which replaces part of the pinned library.
50. **New (J7). A Graph message listed by a folder's round (history, a folder newly included, a reset) and moved into an excluded folder before its body is read may be downloaded and dropped**, never stored or notified; a live addition reads its parent before its body, at one request more, so a round's addition costs two requests and 20,000 messages stay inside the 24-hour import bound (choice 10). *Alternative*: the parent read before every body, three requests a message, which puts a full Graph history import past 24 hours at 40 requests a minute.
51. **New (J5). A DNS-over-HTTPS reply for DKIM with more than 16 answers is a temporary failure**, for forwarded mail too, an empty or `null` answer counting as one (amended, K8); a DKIM key's TXT answer is one record, or a few behind a CNAME. *Alternative*: a streaming decoder that counts answers, which is code of Permaily's own beside the standard decoder.
52. **New (J6). A webhook notice whose source check errs, or cannot finish four seconds into its permit's five, is tried again a minute later**, frozen as it was, until `GiveUpAfter`; a match whose sources are consistently too slow to check is given up rather than sent unchecked. A match not yet frozen whose check errs is not evaluated again before that minute either, whatever else wakes the connection (amended, K9).

## Risks

- **The legal gate may not open**, or may open only for some uses: then no provider opens (decision 13), or provider mail opens with some uses closed, readable in the app and kept from what the determination closes.
- **Google's seven-day refresh tokens in testing mode** make every Gmail mailbox reconnect weekly.
- **Microsoft publisher verification** may take time; without it many work accounts cannot consent.
- **IMAP providers change** their app-password rules and limits; Permaily's single address may be throttled.
- **go-imap v2 is beta**: the wrapper's preflight and the command writer hold what it may do wrong.
- **Quotas are published, not contracted**: Google and Microsoft may lower them; the budgets keep Permaily under them at the cost of delay.
- **Resyncs are costly when they come**: a Gmail resync spends hours of one mailbox's budget on evidence; rare by design.
- **IMAP moves and renames lose decisions** (choice 20), and IMAP deletions take up to an hour to mirror (choice 30).
- **Disconnecting does not revoke at the provider** (choice 21): a person who does not remove Permaily there leaves a token Google still lists, unreadable to anyone.
- **Mirrored deletions are a way to remove evidence** from Permaily's copy; receipts stay.
- **Five minutes is not instant.**

### The P1 findings

1. **K1 · Stale Graph cleanup can erase a restored copy's deletion observer. Accepted.** An `away#` entry is no longer only its key: its sealed value names the local message identifier of the copy it watches and a generation of 16 random bytes drawn for every write, as the deletion mark's generation does (`goneMark`). It is written again, with a fresh generation and the claim's current copy, before every write that sends a claim back `stored` with its message outside the folders included and `live`, by the live removal and by the hourly pass alike. It is deleted only by `DeleteEntryAt` at the exact version and value its deleter read after its own `checking` write (or, finishing a stale `deleting`, read then), and only when that value names a copy committed `deleting` or `gone`, or found in a folder included and `live`. So the review's sequence ends with A's delete conflicting: B's write changed the version, and a remade entry at the same version has another generation. Invariant: an `away#` entry naming a claim's current copy is never removed while that copy is `stored` and its message is outside every folder included and `live`. ("Records and layout", "Per provider", "Graph's folder changes", "Deletions"; T184.)
2. **K2 · An included Graph folder can lose the only observer before its first delta. Accepted.** `sync.folders` gains `live`: the folder's first round has completed and its `deltaLink` is in its `fold#`, set by the end write after that `fold#` write and cleared by a reset's new baseline for that folder. The step's `sync` read comes before every per-message read, so a folder seen `live` there had a delta position from before the message was read. A removal skips the `away#` entry, and an away check deletes it, only when the message's parent is included **and** `live` in that read; a parent included but not yet `live` is treated as away: the entry is written or kept, and the next pass checks it again. Invariant: every Graph copy kept after a removal is either in a folder whose delta position predates the read that placed it there, or named by an `away#` entry, so a deletion for good before a new folder's first delta is still seen by the message's own `ErrorItemNotFound`. ("Records and layout", "Per provider", "Graph's folder changes", "Deletions"; T185.)
3. **K3 · A frozen notice can pass after the mirrored deletion commits. Accepted, by reading the claim.** Of the two fixes the review offered, writing the mark before or with `deleting` would put a mark on a copy whose commit then fails (a restore that wrote the claim first) or needs a transaction the store does not have, so the check reads the claim instead. For a provider contributor, after its mark and its item, the check reads the claim its `StoredMeta.Claim` names through a new `inbox.Reader.Mirrored`, and a claim that is `deleting`, `gone` or `excluded`, names another message identifier, or is missing is a confirmed change; a failed read is `ErrSourceUnavailable`. The mark stays first, the transient contract is unchanged, and the evaluation reads the same claim before a provider message enters a match. Forwarded mail is unaffected: its claims are `seen#` and a hex digest, never under `seen#p`, so no claim is read for it, which T186 counts. ### The P1 findings

1. **H1 · A late item write can resurrect a deleted copy. Accepted; the mark drives a durable cleanup.** The writer's read of the mark after `PutItem` stays as the fast path, but a writer stopped before it is no longer relied on: the sweep's pass over the marks, after `SweepGrace`, deletes the raw object, the item, `txt#`, `confirm#` and `dec#` once more (by the identifiers the mark's key names, without the mailbox's key) and deletes the mark only after all of them succeeded. Invariant: every writer that can still write a deleted message took its claim before the delete's `stored` write and lands within `IngestDeadline` plus `ClockSkew`, inside `SweepGrace`; so its every part lands before a pass that deletes it, the mark outlives the cleanup, and once the mark goes the claim is `stored` and nothing writes the message again. What is weaker, and chosen (choice 41): a late item can be listed for a few minutes until that pass; the alternative, publishing the item conditioned on the claim in one operation, needs a store primitive that does not exist. Raw and search staging get the same path. It holds for forwarded mail from the floor on; a release before the floor sweeps the mark as today, the mark's format unchanged, so it stays readable across that rollback. ("Effect boundaries", "Storing"; T153.)
2. **H3 · A sync unit can repeatedly check its prefix and never reach new mail. Accepted.** A step writes the unit's start before its first effect (the cursor's second write in a step, at the version read), stops starting effects ten seconds before its deadline, and binds the skip to the unit as observed instead of to clocks: the start holds the provider's unit identity, a digest of what the page listed and a fresh nonce, every claim write of the unit carries that nonce, and a retry skips an item whose claim holds it only while identity and digest are unchanged; a changed page restarts the proof. What is the operator's fact (no pre-floor enclave still running) is said ("Launch: one barrier"; T68, T118, T128).
3. **E3 · Epoch rollover admitted a stale successor and lost the tail (P1). Accepted; removed.** There is no strict sync record and so no rollover: cursors are sealed entries whose replay only causes work ("Records and layout"; T18, T76; T79 removed).
4. **E4 · A 1,000-version epoch exceeded the strict store's cold-read budget (P1). Accepted; removed.** Confirmed against `internal/store` (`MaxHistoryReads` 500, `maxCommitObjects` 10,000, 16 fetches, 100,000 cached objects). Polling now writes no strict record: a quiet poll writes only its schedule entry twice (take and release), DynamoDB items; the mailbox record, read strictly at each step, changes at most a few times a month, so its history stays short ("Records and layout", "The poller"; T108 counts the writes).
5. **E5 · Grant changes left a live window for old-run effects (P1). Accepted; the window is made harmless.** The grant is one entry written only at the version read, so a stale worker can neither put back an old token nor mark a new grant broken; whatever it imports is the same account's mail through claims; nothing it publishes is authorised by stored state. Pauses, a secret's removal and disconnects stop new steps; a step in flight may finish its reads and imports within `IngestDeadline`, as a delivery in flight does ("Tokens", "States"; T14, T22, T77).
6. **E6 · Occurrences and the reverse index did not fence visibility or deletion (P1). Accepted; replaced by ADR 0026's claim.** Confirmed: both delete routes call `Reader.Delete` and the writer never reads the mark (`deliver.go`, `read.go`). There are no identity rows, no `msgocc#`, no suppression blocks: the claim, living as long as the copy, is the only per-message state; a person's delete through either route keeps it, a mirrored deletion deletes the copy then the claim, and a claim taken over reads the deletion mark first, which also closes the same window for forwarded mail. A stopped mirrored deletion is finished by the next evidence; its one gap (a restore before the claim goes) is stated ("Storing", "Deletions"; T61, T121, T122; T87, T88, T111 removed).
7. **E7 · The decision-forgetting sweep could miss its deadline and reopen a hold (P1). Accepted; removed.** Confirmed: ADR 0031 keeps a decision an hour past its message's expiry and a person's delete does not forget it. A mirrored deletion now does exactly what a person's delete does: `dec#` goes with the copy, and the strict hold stays to the copy's expiry, holding nothing; its old versions stay in immutable history as for any decision. Identifiers are never backdated, so ADR 0026's walk of decision days from creation holds and no lower bound is carried through removal (choice 29; T89; T90 removed).
8. **E8 · Gmail pagination could stall; Graph and IMAP could miss connect-time mail (P1). Accepted.** Gmail's cursor moves per history record once its effects are done, so any cut resumes past finished work; a resync keeps its page token. `connected_at` is taken before any baseline, and one admission rule by provider date with a ten-minute margin covers every folder's handoff, folders discovered later and resets ("Bootstrap", "Per provider"; T80, T91, T93, T122).
9. **E9 · Graph egress omitted the lookups needed for folder exclusions (P1). Accepted as proposed.** `GET /v1.0/me/mailFolders/{id}` is in the policy for the well-known names; roles come only from those lookups by opaque id; folder listings' continuations are checked against their route; a folder with incomplete ancestry is `unknown`, never imported and never evidence ("Egress", "Folders"; T31, T119).
10. **E10 · An IMAP content match could transfer the wrong message's decisions (P1). Accepted; the match is removed.** A move is a deletion and a new copy; presence evidence is per completed `UID SEARCH` command under the `UIDVALIDITY` the claims were made under, never a snapshot across commands; a `UIDVALIDITY` change is evidence for the old claims ("Per provider", "Deletions"; choice 20; T36, T37, T120).
11. **E11 · The trip cookie was rejected and the session check preceded the effect (P1). Accepted.** Confirmed: `__Host-` requires `Path=/`, as `internal/httpapi/accounts.go` already sets. The cookie now follows that pattern, is checked before the state is consumed and cleared with the same attributes; one trip per browser; the standing check is the last read before the record's commit, and a sign-out after it does not undo what it made, which is the same rule a forwarding creation has (choice 32); connecting again needs no free slot ("The trip"; T7, T113, T114).
12. **E12 · The source policy missed direct model callers and final vendor delivery (P1). Accepted; one choke point.** Before the opening release no provider mailbox exists in production, so no path can meet provider mail. After it, and in a dormant release after a rollback, the mailboxes service's access table refuses, by the signed kind, `AccessModel` (the new access for every person's request that may ask the model: search and matches with meaning, criteria and removal previews) and `AccessConnection` (every connection path, including the recheck before each webhook send) when the compiled uses close them; every path to the model or a vendor opens a mailbox through those two functions. One test runs every route and MCP tool against a failing model and egress and fails on a route without a case. Staging opens only for listed test subjects with every use closed ("The owner's legal gate"; T82 to T84, T116, T117).
13. **E13 · Google's reconnect block had no transaction with revocation (P1). Accepted; revocation is dropped.** No stored row can serialise with Google's project-wide revocation and its propagation. Permaily no longer calls it: the token becomes unreadable at the disconnect's commit and is deleted by the sweep, and the person is told where to remove Permaily, as for Microsoft and IMAP. Reconnecting is never blocked (choice 21; T5; T15, T73 removed).
14. **E14 · Byte admission left permitted in-flight allocations unbudgeted (P1). Accepted.** One pool in `providersync` reserves every class before allocating (raw imports, pages, IMAP non-literal answers), with the IMAP wrapper streaming body literals and bounding everything else at 1 MiB a command; the `CONNECT` reply is bounded at 4 KiB; entries are bounded by their codecs; an over-bound answer is asked again smaller, and a too-large message is skipped and counted with the cursor moving past it. The journal and the 32 KiB sync record are gone ("Memory", "The client and its bounds"; T35, T47, T81, T123).
15. **E15 · Choice 23 depended on the trust model and changed the brief's DNS over HTTPS (P2). Accepted as an explicit amendment.** Choice 23 now says it amends the brief and claims only a WebPKI logical-origin guarantee; the binding is specified (the policy's name as SNI and verified name, no byte before the handshake ends, a bounded `CONNECT` reply), the ID-token keys use the same egress, and HTTP versions per destination come from the spike rather than an assumption ("Egress"; T57, T124; spike item 8).
16. **E16 · The two quota halves were not a deployment-wide budget (P2). Accepted, with a weaker stated claim.** Budgets are fixed per enclave and set so that two together stay under each provider's per-user limit; nothing coordinates them. What they do not hold is said: two Permaily mailboxes of one provider account have two budgets, and the provider's 429 then rules. `Retry-After` is honoured up to seven days; a lost schedule costs one early request; `labels.list` is no longer used; IMAP limits are per failure domain ("Quotas and backoff"; T24, T48, T99, T127; T100 removed).
17. **E17 · The repair could not keep the poll and notice guarantees (P2). Accepted.** The lease is released by the poll's last write, so a healthy mailbox is not skipped; failover is within one lease. The notice is queued with the finder's own time (inside ADR 0041's five minutes) under a key per break, and the grant's `noticed` stops a second queuing; reconnect writes a new grant and so a new break gets a new key. The repair walks the index, and the person's list covers a mailbox missing from it ("The poller", "Notices by mail"; T56, T101, T126).
18. **E18 · Public buckets and retention copy understated observable events (P2). Accepted.** `/status` publishes no provider counts; the provider account is sealed in the record and nothing joins mailboxes across accounts; the privacy text lists every entry's lifetime and what the storage's reader sees, including per-folder claim counts ("The privacy page and terms", "The threat model"; choice 25; T104 removed).
19. **E19 · Tests and ownership left integration cuts without a buildable owner (P2). Accepted.** Each test's brackets now agree with the table; the composition with the real store, the delete and matches routes (their `Access`), notices and templates, settings and retention, the applications record's seam, and the final vendor send (through the recheck) each have one owner; T106 sits in the pull request that holds both copies; T97 and T54's first fixture run a test-only open policy, T54's second fixture and T71 a dormant image, and T116 the closed uses (corrected after the third review, G21); T46 and T105 cover the composed run; fuzz is pull request 6's, reproducibility and the spike's artefact pull request 9's and the spike's; every pull request passes `make check` on its merge base ("Tests", "Implementation pull requests").
20. **E20 · The eight-week chain did not hold (P2). Accepted.** The calendar now assigns each person each week, starts every pull request only after its dependencies, and places the day-14 reading, the seven-day cycle, the floor's checklist and the gate; the critical path is 7 weeks to the dormant release, stated as the estimate of the rows without external waits ("Calendar").
21. **E21 · Successful OAuth responses needed an omitted-field contract (P2). Accepted.** An omitted `scope` is the requested set at the first exchange and the stored set at a refresh, an empty one refused; `tid` and `oid` are required through an exported check without the `sub` fallback; a refresh without a new refresh token keeps the stored one, distinct from `invalid_grant` ("Scopes"; T115).
22. **E22 · Backfill order, retention facts and webhook origin conflicted (P2). Accepted.** Origin is `live` for the live stream or a provider date at or after connecting, so a race between streams cannot silence new mail; a listing never admits a message with no date, a future date, or one older than the retention, so an expired copy is not admitted again; retention counts from the copy; no order is claimed for history, and the coverage words say only how many ("Bootstrap", "Storing", "History import"; T41, T42, T86, T97).
23. **E23 · The IMAP table enumerated six failure domains (P3). Accepted.** Six, named, and limits apply per failure domain ("The closed list").
24. **E24 · The release plan contradicted the one-step launch (P1). Accepted, with the owner's decision 13.** The three kinds are dormant together and open together in one release after the gate and the barrier; staging is isolated; uses are per kind; the folder choices beyond decision 9 are still listed for the owner (choice 26) ("Launch: one barrier", "The owner's legal gate"; T49, T71, T128).
