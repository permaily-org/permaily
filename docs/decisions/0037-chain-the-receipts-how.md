## The owner's choices

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, and final. The design review changed how 2 and 3 are built, not what was chosen ("Changed after the design review", F2, F3, F8).

1. **What stays of a deleted account's chain: the usual ten-year lock.** Every link is a commit in the anchors bucket, locked until its lock ends: ten years in production, as every commit is. After the account is deleted, what stays there is one small object per link: the account's identifier, the link's number, a digest of a value no longer stored, and when; and the account's identifier in the catalogue of chains ("The catalogue of chains"). Nothing of what the receipts said. An account's identifier, its links' numbers and when they were written stay linkable after its deletion, as ADR 0030 accepted for connections.
2. **The enclave looks for deleted receipts on its own: a daily audit.** One pass a day over every chain the catalogue names, its result a strict record both enclaves report on `/status`, so `permaily-verify` fails when a pass found something, and when no pass finished in the last two days. It costs about $6 a year per GiB of receipts kept and $0.002 a year per account at today's prices ("Cost").
3. **The person checks their chain now: the API, the export and `permaily-verify -receipts`.** A route returns the chain's head and its pages with what was found of each receipt, an export holds every link and receipt as stored, and `permaily-verify -receipts` checks an export offline against heads the person noted from the app ("What a person can verify"). The screen and the CSV stay in step 8.

## Context

Receipts are sealed entries of `receipts#<account>`, written once before the answer (ADR 0031, `internal/services/receipts`). Whoever writes the table cannot read, change or make one. They can delete one, or the whole partition, and nothing shows. `permaily-verify` warns about it ("receipts of what left are sealed but not chained yet (step 8)"), and the threat model and docs/data.md state it as the limit.

ADR 0031 left step 8 a constraint: keys' order across two enclaves is not append order, so chaining needs a fenced cutover and a manifest of what existed before, never a hash of sorted pages while writes continue. Four things are being built alongside this design and bound it. Refusals of a connection are grouped by time window, so beyond N a minute one summary receipt counts the rest (the owner's decision 1 of 2026-11-17, a new kind). #152 removes an account's data with the account. Two enclaves may write at once. Receipts in production are few.

## Decision in short

- **A chain of numbered links, each a strict anchored record made once.** An account's receipts are named by links numbered from 1 with no gap. Link `n` is the strict anchored record `receipt-link-<account>-<n>`, made with `CreateNew` and never changed. Its one commit, locked in the anchors bucket, means link `n` can be neither removed nor made twice. Each link names its receipts by identifier and SHA-256 digest of the document as stored, and the link before it by its digest (`prev`). Link 1 names a genesis derived from the account. ("The chain")
- **An account's commits kept together.** The commits of an account's links are under one prefix of the anchors bucket, ordered by number, so one listing reads the full history of every link of a range: forks included, a thousand objects a request. ("Where a link is kept")
- **Written after the receipt, before the answer.** A receipt is stored as today, then linked. `Write` returns only once a committed link names it, so nothing leaves unless its receipt is both stored and chained. Receipts of one account written at once in one enclave share a link (up to 64). Two enclaves compete for a number through `CreateNew`: the one refused reads the other's link and takes the next number. ("Appending")
- **Kind-agnostic, size-agnostic.** A link names documents and never reads what they record, so the refusal window's summary receipt and any later kind are chained the same way. The 256 KiB cap and a search's page cut are unchanged. ("Receipts of a refusal window", "Reading and checking")
- **Every link read strictly, the head found by listing.** The head is the highest number with a commit, found by listing the account's commits, never by numbers' contiguity. A walk reads every link it reports strictly, from the listing, and checks its document against the next link's `prev` (the page's top one against its commit) and every receipt against its link. A storage that cannot be read is an error; what was read and found wanting is a verdict. ("Finding the head", "Reading and checking")
- **The catalogue of chains.** An account's chain is entered in a catalogue, a numbered series of strict records of the same kind, before its link 1 is made. The audit reads the chains the catalogue names, not the accounts' list. ("The catalogue of chains")
- **What existed before is adopted; what was left without a link is recovered.** An account's first links adopt, in key order, the receipts it had without a link from more than a minute before. A receipt left without a link later (a request that failed after its entry was written, or an earlier release's) is linked by the audit ten minutes after it was written, in a link that says it was recovered. ("Receipts written before the chain", "Receipts left without a link")
- **Deleted with the account.** Links' values are entries of the account's own partition, so #152's `DeletePartition` (run twice) removes them with the receipts. What stays is the commits and the catalogue's entry, the owner's choice 1. ("Retention and deleting the account")
- **Seen on `/status` and by the person.** `receipts.chained` becomes true and `receipts.chain` is `strict`. `receipts.audit` is the deployment's newest finished pass and what it found; `receipts.broken` what the enclave that answered found. `permaily-verify` fails on anything found, on no pass or a pass over two days old. The person gets the head, the pages and an export they can check offline against heads they noted. ("What /status and permaily-verify show", "What a person can verify")
- **Cost**: about one S3 listing, one locked S3 write and one small DynamoDB write per link, about $0.011 per 1,000 links, and about 60 to 150 ms more per request before the answer (estimated; measured on staging, L22). The audit: about $6 a year per GiB kept, and $0.002 a year per account. ("Cost")

## The chain

**Links.** Link `n` of an account is the strict anchored record `receipts.LinkName(account, n)` = `receipt-link-<account>-<n>`, read and written through a strict `store.Anchored` of its own that reads and writes the chain's names and no other. It is made only with `CreateNew` and never written again. Its document (`receipts.Link`, version 1) holds:

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `n` | Its number, from 1 |
| `kind` | `receipts` (receipts written through the chain), `adopted` (receipts written before it, "Receipts written before the chain") or `recovered` (receipts the audit found without a link, "Receipts left without a link") |
| `at` | When the enclave that committed it began its write, by that enclave's clock |
| `prev` | The digest of link `n-1`'s document, or for link 1 the genesis, `SHA-256("permaily receipts chain v1" ‖ 0x00 ‖ account)` |
| `break` | Instead of `prev`, when link `n-1` could not be read: `lost`, `forked` or `unreadable` |
| `before`, `more` | On adopted links only: the instant before which receipts are adopted, and whether another adopted link follows |
| `receipts` | 1 to 64 refs, each the receipt's identifier and the digest of its document |

A **digest** is the SHA-256 of a document's bytes as stored, before sealing, in lowercase hexadecimal. It is never taken of a document marshalled again, so a person can recompute it from an export. The link's document is checked whenever it is written and read (`Link.Check`). It is at most 16 KiB of JSON (`LinkBytesMax`; the largest is about 8.1 KiB) and is sealed under a key from the root secret (`permaily receipts chain v1`), bound to its record name.

**Where a link is kept.** `store.Anchored` keeps each version of a record under `<name>@<version>-<hash>`, the hash the first eight bytes of the digest its commit names. A link's record table (`receipts.ChainTable`, a `store.RecordTable` over `store.Entries`) keeps that version in the account's own partition, `receipts#<account>`, under `lnk_<n in 16 hexadecimal digits>@<version>-<hash>` (`receipts.RecordKey`). This is apart from the receipts' `rcp_` keys, so `List` never meets a link, and `DeletePartition` removes links with the receipts. A link's plain name has no key. The table answers "not found" for it without reading, and refuses to store under it, so junk written there can neither stop a link from being made nor be read as one. Its commits are in the anchors bucket under `commits/g/<first 16 bytes of SHA-256 of "receipt-link-<account>" in hexadecimal>/<n in 16 hexadecimal digits>/<version>` (`receipts.CommitGroup`, which `store.S3Commits` uses for the chain's names): an account's links' commits are under one prefix, in their numbers' order, and one `ListObjectVersions` from a number reads every object version and delete marker of up to a thousand above it. Every other record's commits stay where they are.

**Why it holds.** The commit of link `n` is locked: nobody can remove it, and a second commit at its one version is a fork that every reader refuses (ADR 0016, 0026). Only an enclave of the deployment can make a commit (its MAC is under a key from the root secret). An enclave makes link `n+1` only after it has read link `n` committed, so numbers have no gaps. Every link names the one before it by digest. So, once the head is known by its commit and every link of a range has had its full history listed:

- A **deleted receipt** is a ref whose entry is missing.
- A **changed receipt** is a ref whose entry does not open for its key (moved, edited or made up, `ErrUnreadable`), or whose digest is not the ref's.
- A **receipt put back** is the same receipt, which changes nothing.
- A **deleted or replaced link** is a number below the head whose document is missing or whose digest is not what the next link's `prev` names. Its commit is still there.
- A **reordering** is a link whose document does not open for its number, or a `prev` that does not match.
- A **rollback**, an earlier copy of the partition put back, leaves every link and receipt written since missing. Their commits stay, so the head does not go back.
- A **fork**, two commits at one link's version, even one landing after a later link was made, is two object versions at the link's key in every later listing, and the link is `forked` wherever it is read.
- A **truncation** of the tail would need commits removed from the bucket, which nobody can do while they are locked.

## Appending

`receipts.Service.Write` keeps its signature (`receipts.Writer`), so the connections service does not change:

1. **The receipt**, as today: built, checked against `MaxDocumentBytes`, sealed under `Purpose`, bound to its partition and key, stored with `PutEntry` at version 0 under its time-ordered `rcp_` key. Its ref is its identifier and the digest of the JSON it sealed.
2. **The link.** The ref goes to the account's appender in this enclave (`receipts.Chain.Link`). `Write` returns once a committed link names it, or with an error when nothing is known to name it. Its request then gives nothing (answered 503), as a receipt that could not be written does today.

**The appender.** One per account in each enclave, with at most one link in flight. Refs that arrive while a link is being committed wait for the next, which takes all of them up to `LinkReceiptsMax` (64). More than `LinkWaitingMax` (256) waiting, the group in flight not counted, is refused at once, failing those writes closed. A waiter whose context ends leaves the queue and fails; a waiter already in a group in flight fails, and its ref may still be linked (more recorded than left, never less). For each group it:

1. Knows the head (`n` and the digest of link `n`'s document) from its own last link, or finds it ("Finding the head"). A chain not begun is entered in the catalogue first, and adopts ("The catalogue of chains", "Receipts written before the chain").
2. Builds link `n+1` naming the group, with `prev` the digest of link `n` (or `break`, "When the link before cannot be read") and `at` now, and makes it with `CreateNew` under `store.WriteWithin(ReceiptWriteMax)`. No value of a link is then stored more than five seconds after its write began, which account removal's second pass relies on. A chain whose head is `2^64-1` takes no more link: its writes fail.
3. On success, answers every waiting `Write` with its position (`n+1`, its index) and keeps link `n+1` as the head.
4. On `store.ErrExists`, the appender reads link `n+1` strictly. If its document is the one this group built (the same bytes, kept from the attempt and never built again), the commit is this group's own: a retried write whose first try landed is told `ErrExists`, and `CreateNew` keeps a value its commit names. That is success, as in 3. Otherwise another enclave made link `n+1` first. The appender takes it as the head and tries `n+2` with the same group, up to `LinkTries` (8) numbers and within the waiters' deadlines. The group's receipts are unchanged: only the link is built again. A lost race leaves nothing behind (`CreateNew` discards the staged value it lost with).
5. On any other error, fails the group's writes, forgets the head and finds it again next time. A link whose commit's outcome is unknown may name the group and still fail its writes. The group is never put in another link: its refs are dropped, and a ref that no committed link names is recovered by the audit ("Receipts left without a link").

**Two enclaves at once** never coordinate. They meet only at a number, where S3 takes one commit (ADR 0016) and the other enclave reads it and goes after it. Each link names receipts of one enclave's group. A receipt is in at most one link of kind `receipts` or `adopted`, because a group is rebuilt on the next number only once the link at its last try is read and is not its own.

**A commit that lands late.** A commit is a PUT to S3 through the host, which can hold it. Enclave B can read link `n` empty, stage its value and send its commit, held; enclave A makes link `n` and `n+1`; whoever writes the bucket hides A's commit of `n` behind a delete marker, and B's commit then lands too. Both are told success. Nothing prevents it, and nothing need: link `n` now has two commits, every later listing of the account's links shows both, and every reader says `forked` at `n` (L5, L11). What is not done is taking link `n` as `ok` because link `n+1` names its digest: that would hide B's receipts once their entries were deleted. Every link a reader reports is read with its full history (F1).

**Finding the head.** After a restart, or once it is forgotten, the head is the highest number with a commit of this deployment's. The appender reads a hint, the greatest `lnk_` key of the partition (`store.Scanner.ScanEntriesDown`, one item), and lists the account's commits from the hint's number up (`ListObjectVersions` with `KeyMarker` there, a page per thousand objects). Each number listed is, by what the listing and the commits read say:

- **absent**: no object version that is a commit of this deployment (only delete markers, objects that are not commits, or nothing);
- **occupied**: one or more commits. It is then `ok` (its document is the one its commit names, opens and passes `Check`), `lost` (its commit is there, its value not, or not what was committed), `unreadable` (its value does not open for its name, or is not a link) or `forked` (commits of two digests, or a commit at another version than 1, which a link never has).

The head is the highest occupied number. An object version listed that this enclave has not read is read to learn whether it is a commit, from the top down, until the highest occupied number is known. When nothing at or above the hint is occupied, the hint was junk or raised and the listing starts again from number 1. A lowered hint costs more listing pages; neither costs correctness, since a listing from a number shows every version above it. A listing, a read or a value read that fails, or that passes the bounds of a page ("Reading and checking"), is an error (unknown), never an absent number and never a fabricated break: appending fails closed and the walk fails. `Head` returns the number with its verdict, the digest its commit names (unless it is forked) and `at` (when it is `ok`). With a true hint it costs one listing, one read of the head's commit and one read of its value. Nothing in it rests on numbers' contiguity, so a link whose commit's lock ended and was deleted changes nothing for finding the head (F7).

**When the link before cannot be read.** When the head (another enclave's link) is lost, forked or does not open, the new link says `break` with the reason instead of `prev`, and appending goes on. The gap stays where its commit is, and the walk shows it. Stopping would let whoever deletes one small entry stop every connection of the account. The strict `Anchored` reports the lost or forked record on `/status` as a regression of the fixed name `receipt link`, and `permaily-verify` fails on it as it does today.

**Memory.** The appenders and their heads are bounded (an account dropped finds its head again). `store.Anchored` remembers every name it reads or writes, so pull request 1 adds `Anchored.Forget(name)`, and the appender forgets link `n` once link `n+1` is known. One enclave's memory then holds one link a recent account. `store.S3Commits` keeps what it read of each object version by its version id (S3 never changes a version's bytes), so a listing of numbers already read needs no read of their commits.

## The catalogue of chains

The audit must know every chain there is, and the accounts' list cannot say it: it is an index whose entries whoever writes the table can delete, and an account missing from it would be skipped by a pass that then reports nothing found (F2). The catalogue is what the audit reads instead.

- **Its entries.** Entry `k` is the strict anchored record `receipts.CatalogueName(k)` = `receipt-chains-<k>`, made once with `CreateNew` through the same `Anchored`, its commits grouped as a link's (`commits/g/<hash of "receipt-chains">/<k>/…`), its value in `receipts-catalogue` under `cat_<k>`. Its document (`receipts.Catalogue`) holds `k`, `at`, `prev` (the digest of entry `k-1`, or `CatalogueGenesis` for entry 1) or `break`, and 1 to 64 accounts' identifiers.
- **Entered before link 1.** An appender that finds an account's chain not begun first has the account entered in the catalogue (one enclave groups the accounts it enters at once, as links group receipts; two enclaves compete for an entry's number as for a link's). Only once an entry naming the account is committed does it make link 1. So no chain is begun that the catalogue does not name. An account entered whose link 1 was never made, or entered twice (two enclaves, or a retry), costs the audit a listing.
- **Found as a chain is.** The catalogue's head is found by listing its commits; every entry is read strictly and checked against the next one's `prev`. An entry lost, forked or unreadable leaves the accounts it named unknown, so it is counted (`catalogue_lost`, `catalogue_forked`, `catalogue_unreadable`, `catalogue_prev`), and `permaily-verify` fails on it.
- **What the accounts' list is still for.** The audit also reads the accounts' list and begins the chain of an account it names that has receipts and no chain (entered, then adopting), so an account that writes nothing after the release has its receipts of before adopted too. That only hastens adoption: what a pass covers is what the catalogue names.

## Receipts written before the chain

This is ADR 0031's fenced cutover and manifest, in a form that does not wait for every earlier writer to stop. That cannot be shown: the key policy may let an earlier release run.

- **Adoption.** When an appender finds an account's chain not begun, once it is entered in the catalogue, it first adopts. `before` is now less `AdoptAfter` (one minute, longer than a receipt's write and than two enclaves' clocks differ). It lists the account's `rcp_` entries whose identifiers' time is earlier than `before`, in key order, opens each and takes its digest. It makes link 1 of kind `adopted` naming the first 64, with `before` and `more` set when more remain, and further adopted links until none remain. Only then does it make the link of its own group. An account with no such receipt adopts nothing: no adopted link has no receipt, and its `before` is then not recorded, since nothing was there to adopt.
- **Continuing it.** Whoever finds the head adopted with `more` set continues it from that link's last identifier, before the same `before`. Two enclaves adopting at once are two makers of one number: one is made, and the other reads it and continues from it. An adoption whose link is then lost leaves its receipts to the audit, which recovers those that survive.
- **What adoption says.** An adopted link says the receipt existed when the chain began, with this digest, not that nothing was deleted before, nor that the table omitted nothing it held then: strong reads authenticate what they return, not what they leave out. That stays unknowable, and the person is told so. An entry that does not open is never adopted (it is counted `receipt_unreadable` by the audit).
- **Production.** There are few receipts. Each account adopts on its first receipt after the release, within that write's five seconds, or at the first audit. An account whose adoption does not finish in them has that request answered 503, and the next request or pass continues it.

## Receipts left without a link

A receipt whose `Write` stored its entry and then failed (the link's storage unavailable, a crash, the waiter's deadline), or one an earlier release wrote, has an entry and no link. Its answer did not leave if this release wrote it; it may have if an earlier one did. It is not a deletion, and is not counted as one (F6).

- **Recovered by the audit.** A receipt that no link names, written more than `RecoverAfter` (ten minutes) before the pass reads it, is linked by the audit through the account's appender in a link of kind `recovered`. Ten minutes is far past any write of a receipt and its link, so no `Write` still waits for it. From then on, deleting it shows like any other.
- **What a recovered link says.** That the receipt was found without a link and linked late; not whether its answer left. The walk shows it so ("linked late"). A receipt a lost link named (its refs unknown once its document is gone) is recovered if it survives: the lost link stays a break at its number, counted, and the recovery does not hide it. A commit that lands after the recovery (above, "A commit that lands late") can make a receipt named twice, once `recovered`; the walk shows both.
- **Counted, not failed on.** The pass counts what it recovered (`recovered` on `/status`). `permaily-verify` prints it, and warns when it is above zero (a write failed after its entry, or an earlier release is still writing), without failing.
- **Before recovery.** A receipt left without a link can be deleted unseen until the audit links it, as a receipt deleted before chaining can.

## Reading and checking

`receipts.Chain.Walk(ctx, account, before, limit)` reads the receipts before a position, the newest first: links in descending number, and in each its receipts in descending index. `{n, 0}` as `before` begins at link `n-1`. It says what it found of each:

| Of | Verdict | When |
|---|---|---|
| A link | `ok` | Its full history has one commit at version 1; its document opens for its number, passes `Check`, and its digest is what the next link's `prev` names (the page's top link, or one below a break: what its commit names) |
| | `prev` | It opens and passes, and the next link's `prev` names another digest |
| | `lost` | Its commit is there and its document is not, or is not what was committed |
| | `forked` | Two commits of different digests at its version, or a commit at another version |
| | `unreadable` | It does not open for its name, or is not a link |
| A receipt it names | `ok` | Its entry opens for its key and its document's digest is the ref's |
| | `missing` | No entry under its key |
| | `unreadable` | The entry does not open for its partition and key |
| | `changed` | It opens, and its digest is not the ref's |

**Every link read strictly** (F1). A page lists the account's commits over its range of numbers (one `ListObjectVersions` a thousand objects): every link the page reports has its full history listed. A number with one object version at version 1 needs no read of it when its value is authenticated by the link above. A number with two or more versions has each version it has not read before read, and is `forked` if two commits of different digests are there. The page's top link is authenticated by the digest its commit names (one read of its commit, kept by version id); each link below by the `prev` of the link above, already authenticated. Below a link that is not `ok` (its document gone or not trusted), the next is authenticated by its own commit, as the top one is. The value of each link is read by its one key, `lnk_<n>@<version>-<hash>` of the digest that authenticates it (a `BatchGetItem` for the page), so nothing else stored under its number is ever read. The head's value is read the same way. A link or receipt read and found wanting is a verdict, never left out. A storage that cannot be read now (a listing, a read or a value read that fails) fails the page with an error, never a verdict and never an absent link.

**The bounds of a page** (F5). Apart from `limit` receipts, a page reads at most `WalkBytesMax` (8 MiB) of sealed entries, visits at most `WalkLinksMax` (256) links, a link that cannot be read counting as one that can, and lists at most `WalkListedMax` (4,000) objects, delete markers and objects that are not commits counted. The context's deadline bounds its time. A page that reaches a bound ends there with `Next`, inside a link or at one (`{n, 0}` after a link that holds nothing to show), and is never `Done`; only the page that reaches link 1's first receipt is `Done`. A page made only of broken links is still a page, with its `Next`. A continuation is stateless: the next page lists its own range and authenticates its own top link by its commit.

**The 256 KiB cap and pagination.** Nothing changes for writing: a receipt is one document of at most 256 KiB, and a search's page still ends before the message that would pass it (ADR 0031, finding 10). A ref is the same size whatever the receipt's, so a link's size does not depend on its receipts. Reading is bounded by bytes, since 64 receipts at their largest are about 22 MB sealed.

## The audit

The owner chose it (choice 2). Every `AuditEvery` (a day) a pass begins, run under a lease entry so that, usually, one enclave of two runs it. The lease only shares the work: two enclaves running at once, or a lease forged, erased or replayed, cost work twice, never a pass reported that was not made.

- **What a pass reads.** The catalogue, from its head to entry 1, and every chain it names once, from its head to link 1: the account's commits listed (a request a thousand objects), its head's commit read, and the account's partition read whole (`rcp_` and `lnk_` entries, a `Query` eventually consistent, then read again strongly before anything is counted). It checks each chain as a walk does, links bottom-up and receipts in key order, holding what is within `RecoverAfter` of the links being checked (adopted links are in key order), so its memory does not grow with an account's size. It also counts as broken a receipt named twice by links of kind `receipts` or `adopted`.
- **Removed accounts.** A chain that shows something broken is asked of the accounts service (`Removing(ctx, account)`, read from the account's anchored record): an account whose record is removed, or closing with its day come (its removal deletes receipts before its record, #152), is counted `removed`, not checked. Nobody but an enclave can make that record say so. An account whose chain shows nothing is not asked.
- **What a pass does besides counting.** It recovers receipts left without a link ("Receipts left without a link") and begins the chains of accounts with receipts and no chain ("The catalogue of chains").
- **When it is finished.** A pass is finished only once it has read every entry of the catalogue up to the head it began with and every chain they name. A run lasts at most `AuditRunMax` (an hour); a pass not finished in it, or stopped by a restart or an error the storage gave, is begun again from the start an hour later, never continued from a cursor anyone could edit. A chain larger than a run can read keeps every pass from finishing, which `permaily-verify` shows (no pass for two days); with a thousand objects a listing and a megabyte a `Query` page, an hour reads millions of links.
- **Its result.** A finished pass is the strict record `receipts.AuditName(p)` = `receipt-audit-<p>`, made once with `CreateNew` (`receipts.AuditPass`: when it began and finished, the catalogue's head it covered, the chains checked, those removed, what it found by every kind, what it recovered), its commits grouped as the catalogue's. Two enclaves finishing at once make `p` and `p+1`. Both enclaves report the newest on `/status`, found by listing as a head is, read again at most every ten minutes. So whichever enclave answers, `/status` reports the deployment's newest pass, not one enclave's.
- **What it says to the person and in logs.** The person sees the same verdicts in their walk. Logs get kinds only, never a count, an account, a number or a digest; counts are on `/status`. No mail is sent: a notice to the person is step 9's.

## What a person can verify

The owner chose it now (choice 3), with pull request 3.

- **In the app, through the API** (`GET /api/v1/receipts`, the person's session, their own account only, allowed while their terms are not agreed and while the account is closing, as the connections' pages are; a removed account has no session). The chain's head (`n`, its verdict, its digest, when) and pages of the walk, each receipt with its position and verdict, within the bounds of a page. Step 8's screen shows "receipt 1,234 was deleted from the storage" where the verdict says so, "linked late" for a recovered link, and "made available", never "received" (ADR 0031). The app shows the head's number and digest, for the person to note.
- **Offline.** `GET /api/v1/receipts/export?before=` gives pages (`receipts.ExportPage`), each within the bounds of a walk's page, with every link and receipt as stored (each document's exact bytes, base64 in JSON, never marshalled again, so a receipt of a kind or version this build does not know keeps every field) and the head when it was read. An export is its pages in order, one JSON object a line, which the app downloads in turn. `permaily-verify -receipts <file>` reads it a page at a time and recomputes every digest: `prev` link by link, every ref against its receipt, link 1 against the account's genesis. It prints the breaks, the recovered links and what each page's head was.
- **What the file proves, and what it does not** (F3). An export checked alone proves only that it is consistent with itself: whoever edits the file can recompute every digest above a link they changed, or the whole chain. What authenticates it is a head the person noted from the app, over the attested connection, kept apart from the file: `-receipts-head <n>:<digest>`, given once or more. The export's link `n` must have that digest, and every link below it down to the first break, with every receipt they name, is then authenticated: a head authenticates the chain below it, backwards. Links above the newest noted head, and links below a break, are printed as not authenticated by a noted head. A noted head above the export's own fails the check (the file was cut short). Whether the export is current is shown only by a head noted when it was made: the person notes the head the app shows as they export. A new deployment, with a new root secret, starts a new chain on which no earlier head is, and the check fails on it.
- **What each rests on.** Consistency and authentication up to a noted head need no trust in the enclave or the storage once the head was noted over the attested connection. Completeness rests on the attested code: no answer left without its receipt linked (`Write`) is a property of the image `permaily-verify` checks by its measurements, not of the export.

## What /status and permaily-verify show

`attestation.ReceiptsStatus`, committed with this design:

- `chained: true` and `chain: "strict"` (`attestation.ReceiptsChainStrict`).
- `audit`: the deployment's newest finished pass (`attestation.ReceiptsAudit`: `began`, `finished`, `catalogue`, `chains`, `removed`, `broken` by every kind of `attestation.ReceiptsBrokenKinds`, also at zero, and `recovered`); absent before the first.
- `broken`: by every kind, also at zero, what the enclave that answered found since it started by its walks, exports, appends and audits. Zero since a start means nothing seen, not everything checked.

The kinds are `receipt_missing`, `receipt_unreadable`, `receipt_changed`, `link_lost`, `link_forked`, `link_unreadable`, `link_prev`, and the four of the catalogue (`catalogue_lost`, `catalogue_forked`, `catalogue_unreadable`, `catalogue_prev`). A receipt without a link is not one (`recovered`).

`permaily-verify`, built from the release's commit, applies `ReceiptsStatus.Check` with its own clock. It fails when receipts are not sealed, not chained, or the chain is not `strict` (replacing today's warning); when no pass has finished, or the newest finished more than `ReceiptsAuditStaleAfter` (48 hours) before now, or after now; when a count is below zero, a kind is missing or one it does not know is there (counted as broken); and when any count of the audit or of the enclave that answered is above zero ("the storage deleted or changed receipts or links of the record of what left"). It prints the counts, `finished` and `recovered`, and warns when `recovered` is above zero. A lost or forked link already fails it, as a regression of the record kind `receipt link`. Since `/status` names no account, someone verifying learns that something was deleted somewhere, and the person learns which receipts in their own pages.

A break found stays: it is evidence that the storage deleted or changed something, every pass finds it again, and `permaily-verify` keeps failing. What then is an incident (docs/operations.md, pull request 2): the operator finds which accounts from the enclave's walks (the person sees it in theirs), tells them, and the chain goes on past the break. A way for `permaily-verify` to accept a break already known and still fail on any new one is a later decision.

A new deployment starts with no pass: `permaily-verify` fails until its first pass finishes, minutes after it starts. A new release of the same deployment reads the passes of the one before, so a release does not.

## Retention and deleting the account

- **While the account lives**, receipts and links are kept for as long as the account (ADR 0019). No route deletes one. Should a person ever choose to keep receipts for less time (the sample's "Your data" shows such a choice; docs/legal.md, item 7), that would be a link of a new kind naming what was removed by the person's choice, never a deletion the chain cannot tell from an attack: a later decision.
- **When the account is removed** (#152, ADR 0031's choice 32), accounts deletes the partition `receipts#<account>`, receipts and links' values together, and again `RemoveAgainAfter` (ten minutes) later. Every link is written under `WriteWithin(ReceiptWriteMax)`, so a link racing the removal is stored within five seconds of its write's start or not at all, and the second pass removes it. No `RemoveRecord` is needed for links: an account's identifier is never used again, nothing reads a removed account's chain but the audit, which counts it `removed`, and a value put back opens for no route.
- **What stays.** The links' commits in the anchors bucket stay until their lock ends: ten years in production, a day on staging images (the owner's choice 1). The catalogue's entries naming the account stay, sealed, since an entry names several accounts and is the audit's evidence. The table's point-in-time recovery keeps the deleted items for one day, as it does every deleted item (`deploy/tofu/mail.tf`).
- **Where docs/data.md and the privacy page say it.** docs/data.md gets rows for links and the catalogue. The privacy page's sentence on what stays of an account after deletion is checked against it in pull request 2, with docs/legal.md: an account's identifier, its links' numbers and times stay, not "nothing personal".

## Receipts of a refusal window

The summary receipt (the owner's decision 1 of 2026-11-17, designed in parallel) is a new `Kind` written through the same `Writer`, and the chain names it as it names any receipt. What this design asks of it, and of every later kind: **a receipt is a document written once.** A summary whose count changes is a new receipt each time it is written, or one receipt written when its window closes, never an entry updated, because a link names one digest. When its refusals are accounted for in a summary written later is that design's to settle: the chain does not turn a deferred or changing counter into a receipt written before its answer. The summary also bounds the links its refusals cost, as it bounds the writes.

## Cost

At us-east-2's prices as of this writing: S3 PUT and LIST $0.005 per 1,000, GET $0.0004 per 1,000; DynamoDB on demand $0.625 per million write units (1 KiB) and $0.125 per million read units (4 KiB strongly consistent, 8 KiB eventually). A link's sealed item is about 0.45 KiB plus 0.16 KiB per ref: one write unit with one ref, eleven with 64 (`w` below). A receipt's sealed entry is taken at about 3 KiB, its usual size; at most 342 KiB.

| What | Requests | About |
|---|---|---|
| A link, made | `CreateNew`: one listing (its number's history), one locked PUT, one DynamoDB write of `w` units; the plain name is not read | $0.010 per 1,000 links plus $0.000625·`w` per 1,000: $0.011 with one ref, $0.017 with 64 |
| A race lost to the other enclave | The loser's `CreateNew` (listing, PUT refused, staged value written and discarded) and a strict read of the winner (listing, a read of its commit, its value) | $0.015 per 1,000, plus `w`+1 write units |
| Entering an account in the catalogue | One `CreateNew` of an entry, shared by the accounts one enclave enters at once | Once per account: $0.00001 |
| Finding the head after a restart | The hint, one listing from it (and a page a thousand objects above it), a read of the head's commit and its value; a junk hint, a listing of every page | Once per account and enclave: $0.0000055 with a true hint |
| A page of the walk | A listing a thousand objects of its range, a read of its top link's commit when not kept, its links' values (`BatchGetItem`) and the reads of its receipts | $0.0000054 and its DynamoDB reads |
| The audit, one pass | Per chain: a listing a thousand of its objects and a read of its head's commit. The catalogue the same. Every stored byte of receipts and links read once, eventually consistent | Per year: $5.98 per GiB kept, plus $0.00197 per account per started thousand links |
| Storage | One locked commit object of about 250 bytes per link for ten years, and one item of about 0.6 KiB per one-receipt link while the account lives | S3: under $1 per million links over ten years. DynamoDB: about $0.15 a month per million one-receipt links (at $0.25 a GB-month), a small part of what their receipts take |

The audit's year, in general: `365 × (Σ over accounts of (⌈objects/1,000⌉ × $0.000005 + $0.0000004) + bytes kept / 8 KiB × $0.000000125)`. Examples, with one receipt per link:

- **1,000 accounts with one receipt each**: $1.97 a year, almost all listings.
- **A quiet account**, one receipt a day: about $0.006 in its first year, $0.013 in its second.
- **A busy account**, 200 receipts a day (73,000 links and about 257 MiB a year): about $0.82 in its first year, $2.45 in its second. Grouping makes a burst cheaper (fewer links and listings); bytes read do not change.
- **Broken links** cost a read of their commit each ($0.0000004) and a listing page per thousand objects, as anything listed does; junk under an account's prefix costs the same, within the bounds of a page and of a run.

With one receipt per link, the usual case for a person's mail, a receipt costs about $0.000011 more than today, about 8% of one ask of the model ($0.00013, ADR 0028). Grouping makes a burst cheaper per receipt (a full link is $0.00000027 a receipt), though a group is not always full: the first request of a burst gets a link of its own, and the rest wait for the next.

**Latency** (estimated, provisional until L22 measures it). The link adds one listing and one locked PUT in series with a small DynamoDB write, from the enclave through the host's proxy, before the answer: about 60 to 150 ms. Besides: a wait for the link in flight when several requests of one account arrive at once; a race lost (a strict read and another `CreateNew`); finding the head when cold (a hint and a listing); and, once per account, entering it in the catalogue and adopting. All of it within `ReceiptWriteMax` (five seconds) from the receipt's write, past which the request is answered 503. The staging check measures p50 and p99, cold and warm, and the requests per receipt, and puts the numbers here (L22).

## What it guarantees, and what it does not

**It guarantees** that once the release runs, no answer of a connection leaves unless its receipt is stored and named by a committed link (`Write`). After that, whoever writes the table or the bucket cannot delete, replace, move, reorder, put back an earlier copy of, or hide a receipt or a link without a walk showing it at its number, the audit counting it and `permaily-verify` failing: a link is checked with its full history, so a fork is found however late its second commit landed. Nor can they make the head go back: commits are locked, and the head is found by listing them. Nor can they hide a chain from the audit: the catalogue names it before its link 1 is made, and an entry lost is counted. Two enclaves keep this however they interleave.

**It does not protect against:**

- **Deletion before chaining.** A receipt deleted before its account's chain began is not known. Adoption says what existed then, not what existed before.
- **Deletion before recovery.** A receipt left without a link (its write failed after its entry, or an earlier release wrote it) can be deleted unseen until the audit links it, ten minutes to a day later.
- **An earlier release running.** A release from before this design, which the key policy still lets run, writes unchained receipts. They are recovered if they survive, and can be deleted unseen before. Retiring those releases' keys closes it (docs/operations.md).
- **Denial of service.** Whoever writes the table or the bucket can stop an account's receipts from being linked: junk or a fork where its next number goes, objects under its commits' prefix past a page's bounds, or the storage refusing writes. Every request of its connections is then answered 503 and nothing leaves. They can likewise keep the audit from finishing a pass. That fails closed, and is visible on `/status` and to `permaily-verify`.
- **The end of the lock.** A chain is protected while its head's commit is locked: every link below is authenticated by `prev` from it, so a link whose commit's lock ended is still checked, and finding the head does not need it. An account that writes no receipt for the lock period (ten years in production, a day on staging) has a head whose commit can then be deleted, and with it whatever is newest of its chain, as every record's (ADR 0016, "The lock has an end"). A shorter lock would shorten this for every chain, not only for dormant accounts' newest links: links whose head is younger stay protected, the newest of every account that wrote nothing for that long do not. Extending locks is a later decision, due before the first production locks end in 2036.
- **A new deployment.** A new root secret makes old commits no longer this deployment's, so the chain starts over. The person's noted head shows it.
- **What a receipt proves.** It still records what was made available, not what the vendor received (ADR 0031).
- **Timing.** `at` is the writing enclave's clock. Receipts' order on the chain is the order their links were committed, which can differ from their identifiers' times by a few seconds across enclaves.
- **What the operator sees.** The operator sees how many links an account has and when, in the anchors bucket and the catalogue. While the account lives that adds nothing: the table's time-ordered keys show the same. After deletion it is what stays (the owner's choice 1).
- **Detection when nobody looks.** The audit looks every day. Between passes, a deletion is detectable whenever the chain is read, not detected until then.
- **An export alone.** An export file proves only its own consistency; authenticity rests on a head the person noted apart from it, and currency on one noted when it was made ("What a person can verify").
- **Trust in the code.** That every answer is chained rests on the attested code, as every guarantee of the enclave does.

- **L1.** Names, keys, groups and the genesis: a link's, a catalogue entry's and a pass's name round-trip their series and number and nothing else parses as one; their versions are kept in their partitions under keys ordered by number and apart from receipts'; their plain names have no key; their commits are grouped by series, members in their numbers' order, an account's apart from another's; the genesis is the design's and the account's own, the catalogue's no account's; a digest is lowercase hexadecimal and nothing else reads as one. Committed. - **L2.** The documents: `Check` refuses every malformed link (version, number, kind, time, both or neither of `prev` and `break`, link 1 with a break, adoption's fields where they do not belong, none or 65 receipts, a ref that is not a receipt, one named twice), catalogue entry and pass, and the largest of each fits `LinkBytesMax` and one item sealed. Committed. Pull request 1 tests the sealing and the store's item bound with the largest of each.
- **L3.** `Write` stores the entry, then the link, and returns only once the link is committed, with its position. Barriers after the entry, after the link's value is staged, after its commit is accepted and before `Write` returns: a played chain that fails at each makes `Write` fail, and the API's and MCP's transports release no mail (with ADR 0030's V32), a waiter whose deadline ends in a group included. The receipt's document is the same as before this design (ADR 0031's goldens unchanged).
- **L4.** Grouping: 200 concurrent writes of one account in one enclave, under a played schedule (the first link takes one receipt while 199 wait, then links of 64, 64, 64 and 7): every receipt in exactly one link, no link above 64, numbers without gap, every `prev` matching, five links with no race lost. Past `LinkWaitingMax` waiting, the group in flight not counted, a write fails at once. A waiter cancelled before its group leaves fails without cancelling the others; one cancelled in a group in flight fails, and its ref is linked at most once.
- **L5.** Two enclaves (two services over one `Entries` and one `MemoryCommits`, two `Anchored`): 2,000 interleaved writes of one account, with a store that pauses each before its commit while the other acts. Every receipt in at most one link, numbers without gap, no fork, each lost race rebuilt on the next number; with `-race -count=20`. And the late fork (F1) on the S3 emulator: B stages link `n` and holds its commit; A makes `n` and `n+1`; a delete marker hides A's commit; B's commit lands; both are told success; B's receipts' entries and B's value of `n` are deleted; both enclaves restart. Every walk, the audit and `Head` say `forked` at `n` (`link_forked` counted), and no walk returns a whole, clean, shorter chain.
- **L6.** A crash between the entry and the link (`Memory.Interrupt`): `Write` fails, the next write links normally, the entry is recovered by the audit `RecoverAfter` later in a `recovered` link, counted `recovered` (not broken), and `ReceiptsStatus.Check` passes with its warning. A crash after the commit and before the answer: `Write` fails, the receipt is linked once, the head is found again with no second link naming it, and the group is not enqueued again.
- **L7.** A commit whose outcome is unknown, made with the commit's PUT landing and its answer lost in the S3 emulator: the group's writes fail, the head is found again, and the group is in that one link, never in two. A commit whose retry is refused after its first try landed (`ErrExists` over this group's own link, through `CreateNew`'s own retries and value cleanup): the writes succeed at that number with the attempt's exact bytes, nothing rebuilt, and nothing is linked at the next.
- **L8.** Finding the head: chains of 0, 1, 2, 63, 64, 65, 1,000, 1,001 and 10,000 links, after a restart, with a true hint in one listing, with none, lowered or raised far above the head (a junk `lnk_` key) in at most `⌈objects/1,000⌉+1` listings. Every newest value deleted, every value deleted, the hint's value lost, the head forked (`Head`'s verdict, no digest), the head unreadable: the head is the highest committed number with its verdict, and appending goes on with a `break`. Junk objects and delete markers above the head are ignored; a listing or read that fails is an error, never an absent number. A chain at `2^64-1` takes no more link. A value under a link's plain name is never read and does not stop `CreateNew`.
- **L9.** A receipt deleted: `missing` at its position, through either enclave, a fresh reader and after a restart, all agreeing. Another receipt's sealed value under its key: `unreadable`. A changed byte: `unreadable`. The same receipt put back: `ok`, not a new receipt.
- **L10.** A link's value deleted, inside the chain and at the head: the walk says `lost` at its number, and the next link down is authenticated by its own commit. The next append through the other enclave writes `break: lost` and goes on. The audit counts `link_lost`; receipts only that link named are recovered if they survive, and none is reported `missing` by a guess at the refs it held.
- **L11.** A link forked (`MemoryCommits.Fork` at its version), before and after a later link names it: `forked` in the walk and the audit, the regression `receipt link` with `fork` on `/status`, the next link `break: forked`, nothing written at the forked number.
- **L12.** A rollback: an earlier copy of the whole partition put back after 30 more links, the hint gone with it. The head is still the newest number, the 30 links are `lost`, the receipts they named and survive are recovered, and the chain is never shown whole and shorter.
- **L13.** A reordering: two receipts' entries swapped between their keys, `unreadable` both. Two links' values swapped between numbers, `unreadable` both (bound to their names). A link's value from another account's partition, `unreadable`. A staged value never committed and a value moved from another number with a key that looks right: never read, since only the key of the authenticated digest is.
- **L14.** Adoption: accounts with 0, 1, 64, 65 and 200 receipts from before the chain adopt them in key order, `before` and `more` as designed, then link their own; an account with none makes no adopted link. Two enclaves adopt at once, giving one adoption with no gap, the other continuing it. A receipt at `before` exactly is not adopted. An entry that does not open is not adopted and is counted `receipt_unreadable`. An adoption cut by its write's deadline is continued by the next write or by the other enclave; one whose link is then lost leaves its surviving receipts to recovery. An account that writes nothing after the release is entered and adopted by the audit from the accounts' list.
- **L15.** Kind-agnostic: a receipt of every `Kind`, one of a kind this build does not know (the refusal window's summary stands in), one of a version it does not know, and one of 256 KiB are linked, walked and exported the same, their bytes kept exactly; the links' sizes do not depend on the receipts'.
- **L16.** Removing the account, through the accounts service's real removal with ADR 0030's `RemoveAll` and ADR 0031's H17, on a store enforcing the link write's time bound: `DeletePartition` removes receipts and links' values, with a group queued, a write pending, a staging failed and an adoption in flight, through both enclaves, the second pass and retries over a partition of more than 1,000 entries. Nothing of the chain reads through a person's route after the removal. The commits and the catalogue's entry stay; the audit counts the account `removed`, never broken.
- **L17.** The walk's pages: bounded by `limit`, `WalkBytesMax`, `WalkLinksMax` and `WalkListedMax`, ending inside a link and at one, continuing at `Next` with nothing skipped or repeated, through either enclave. A page of broken links only, a page that ends before showing a receipt, and a range full of junk and staged-looking values: each ends at its bound with `Next`, never `Done`, with the S3 and DynamoDB requests and the data held counted. Every link reported has its history listed (one listing a page when under a thousand objects) and its value read by its one key.
- **L18.** The audit (choice 2): one pass across the catalogue finds each kind of L9 to L14 and counts it by kind; a catalogue entry lost, forked, unreadable or not what the next names is counted and its accounts are not taken as checked. An account deleted from the accounts' list is still audited (F2's reproduction). A lease erased, forged or replayed: two enclaves run, one pass is recorded per finish, nothing reported that was not made. A pass stopped by a restart, an error or `AuditRunMax` is not recorded and begins again; a chain too large for a run keeps passes from finishing. Both enclaves report the same newest pass. Logs get kinds only.
- **L19.** `/status` and `permaily-verify`: `chained` true, `chain` `strict`, `audit` and `broken` with every kind at zero. `ReceiptsStatus.Check` fails on each case of "What /status and permaily-verify show" (committed in `TestReceiptsCheck`); `permaily-verify` prints the counts, `finished` and `recovered`, warns on `recovered`, and the warning of step 5 is gone. An image with no audit, and a status whose answering enclave started a minute ago, are not taken for a clean audit.

- **L28.** The end of the lock (F7), with a moved clock on `MemoryCommits` and the S3 emulator: links 1 to 10 from over ten years ago, their commits expired and deleted, link 11 still locked, no hint, every value put back from an old copy. The head is found at 11 by listing; links 1 to 10 are authenticated by `prev` from it; values put back from before are `lost` where they differ. With the head's own commit expired and deleted, the head found is lower, as "The end of the lock" says, and L19's check still runs.
- **L29.** The audit's record: a finished pass made once, its number found by listing; two finishes at once make two; a pass's value deleted is `lost` at its number, and `/status` reports the newest pass it can read and the regression; a pass from the future or with a kind missing is refused by `Check`.

## Options considered

- **Periodic commitments per account** (a strict record anchoring, every minute or so, a digest over the receipts written since): fewer commits. But receipts written since the last commitment can be deleted unseen, and with a restart nothing remembers them. Closing that means waiting for the commitment before the answer, which costs what a link costs.
- **One strict record per account, appended at every receipt** (the head as a record changed at each version): the same commits, but a strict record's history is bounded (10,000 versions, 500 objects read at a time), so it needs epochs and a pointer to the current one. A link made once per number needs neither, and finding the head is a listing.
- **Each receipt its own strict record**: no contention, but a receipt is up to 342 KiB sealed in `pk=system`, every read a strict read, and nothing would say how many there should be. Numbers and `prev` do.
- **`prev` inside the receipt's document** (a chain of receipts, with no links): a receipt's document would depend on the one before, so a race between two enclaves would rewrite a receipt already stored or leave orphans, and a receipt written before the chain could not be adopted as it is. Links keep receipts written once and unchanged.
- **Links' values in `pk=system`**, with the other records: deleting the account would then need a `RemoveRecord`, and another commit, for every link. In the account's partition, `DeletePartition` removes them.
- **Stopping at a lost link**: whoever deletes one small entry would stop every connection of the account. A break recorded and appending going on keeps the evidence and the service.
- **Waiting for every earlier writer to stop before adopting** (ADR 0031's words): it cannot be shown while the key policy lets an earlier release run, so receipts after the cutover without a link are recovered instead.
- **Checking links below the head by digests alone** (the design as first proposed): a strict read only at the head and below a break. A commit that lands late makes a fork below a link that names the other value, which then hides it (F1). Rejected.
- **A link's commits each under their own prefix, every link listed on its own**: what every other record does. Reading every link strictly would cost a listing per link per walk and per daily pass, $1.83 a year per thousand links kept, growing with every link. Grouped, a thousand links cost one listing.
- **Settled links**: a commit can land only while S3 accepts its signed request, fifteen minutes from its signing, so a link listed once after that could not fork later. It rests on AWS's clock check and the enclave's clock, which nothing here has relied on; grouped listings make it unnecessary.
- **The accounts' list as the audit's discovery** (the design as first proposed): an index whoever writes the table can delete from, so an account missing from it would be skipped by a pass that reports nothing (F2). The catalogue replaces it; the list only hastens adoption.
- **A best-effort audit**: what the first proposal was, without saying so. The owner chose a daily audit that `permaily-verify` can rely on.
- **Counting every receipt without a link as broken** (the first proposal's `unlinked`): a write that failed honestly would make `permaily-verify` fail for ever (F6). Recovery links them, counted apart.
- **Renewing commits' locks** before they end, or a checkpoint per account: not needed for finding the head once it is found by listing, and a new use of S3's retention. Left for a later decision before 2036.
- **Signing the head** with the attested TLS key, so a person could show it to a third party, or check an export without noting a head: a new signing use of that key. A head noted from the app over the attested connection authenticates an export for the person. Left for a later decision.
- **Rekor or another public log for heads**: Rekor cannot answer "the newest entry of this kind" (ADR 0016), and it would publish an account's activity.

## Chosen without an owner's decision

1. One strict anchored record per link, made with `CreateNew`, numbered without gap, with `prev` as a SHA-256 of the document as stored and a genesis per account.
2. Links in the account's partition through a record table of their own, the plain name never read; their commits grouped per account in the anchors bucket.
3. Grouping in each enclave, 64 receipts a link, 256 waiting at most, eight numbers tried a group.
4. The head found by a hint and a listing of the account's commits, never trusting the hint; `Anchored.Forget` for memory.
5. A break recorded and appending going on when the link before cannot be read.
6. Every link a walk or the audit reports read with its full history.
7. A catalogue of chains, entered before link 1, as the audit's discovery; the accounts' list only for adoption.
8. Adoption at the chain's start with a one-minute margin; receipts without a link after it recovered by the audit ten minutes after they were written, counted apart, not failed on.
9. The audit's finished passes as strict records both enclaves report; a pass finished only when complete, begun again otherwise; `permaily-verify` failing on no pass within 48 hours.
10. `permaily-verify` fails, not warns, on any broken count and when the chain is not `strict`, and keeps failing on a break once found.
11. An export authenticated by heads the person notes from the app, not by a signature.
12. No mail to the person about a break until step 9's notices.
13. The receipt's document unchanged; a receipt is a document written once, whatever its kind, the refusal window's summary included.

## Changed after the design review (2026-11-17)

The design review of this record at `d68c3eb` found three P1 and six P2 defects (F1 to F9), with reproductions on the store and the accounts service, and agreed with the owner's three choices on conditions. The orchestrator accepted all nine, each fixed as the review proposes; where it offered options, the one taken fails closed and is the simplest to build and test. The owner's choices stay as decided; F2, F3 and F8 change how two of them are built and what they cost. Each change is made in place above, and the seams, the tests and the pull requests match it. By finding:

1. **A committed successor authenticated one predecessor value, not the absence of a fork (F1, P1).** Accepted, as proposed: every link a walk or the audit reports is read with its full history, and a digest from the link above authenticates its bytes only. Made affordable by keeping an account's links' commits together in the anchors bucket (`receipts.CommitGroup`, `S3Commits.Group`): one listing reads the history of a thousand links, forks and late versions included, and a number with one version needs no read of its object. Rereading once before extending was not taken: the second commit can land after any reread. "Settled links" (a commit lands only within S3's fifteen minutes) was not taken: it rests on clocks nothing here relies on. The late-fork schedule is L5 and L11, on the S3 emulator with a delete marker, both acknowledgements, restarts and the losing branch's receipts deleted. ("A commit that lands late", "Reading and checking", L5, L11.)
2. **The audit's discovery could be erased without erasing the account (F2, P1).** Accepted. Of the review's options (a rollback-resistant catalogue, or narrowing the choice to best effort), the catalogue is taken: the owner chose an audit `permaily-verify` can rely on, and a best-effort one cannot fail it. The catalogue is a numbered series of strict records made once, like links, that an account is entered in before its link 1 is made, with its own breaks counted. The accounts' list now only hastens adoption. A removed account is told apart by its anchored record (`Removing`: removed, or closing with its day come), asked only of a chain that shows something broken. The audit's result is a strict record per finished pass (`AuditPass`), reported by both enclaves, so `/status` says the deployment's audit and not one enclave's counters. `permaily-verify` fails when no pass has finished, when the newest is over 48 hours old or from the future, and on a kind missing or unknown (`ReceiptsStatus.Check`, committed with its test). A pass is finished only when complete and is begun again otherwise, never continued from a cursor anyone could edit. ("The catalogue of chains", "The audit", "What /status and permaily-verify show", L18, L19, L26, L29.)
3. **An earlier noted head did not authenticate a later export's suffix (F3, P1).** Accepted. Of the review's options (an authenticated current checkpoint, or limiting what the offline check claims), the narrower claim is taken, with no new signing: a head noted from the app over the attested connection authenticates the chain below it, backwards, to the first break; links above the newest noted head, and below a break, are printed as not authenticated; a noted head above the export's own fails it; an export's currency is shown only by a head noted when it was made. The tests are the review's: a recomputed suffix, a recomputed whole chain, a cut at the old head, and a break. ("What a person can verify", L24, L25.)
4. **The head's search had no committed-but-unreadable result and no defined bracket (F4, P2).** Accepted, and made simpler than a bisection: the head is the highest number with a commit, found by listing the account's commits from a hint (and from number 1 when nothing at or above the hint has one). Each number is absent, or occupied and `ok`, `lost`, `unreadable` or `forked`; an occupied number is the head whatever its value, `Head` says its verdict and the digest its commit names, and appending goes on with a `break`. A listing or read that fails is an error, never absence or a break. Numbers are compared as keys, with no arithmetic to overflow; a chain at `2^64-1` takes no more link. ("Finding the head", `Head`, L8.)
5. **Receipt and byte limits did not bound a page of lost links (F5, P2).** Accepted, as proposed: a page is bounded besides by links visited (`WalkLinksMax`, broken ones counted) and objects listed (`WalkListedMax`, delete markers and junk counted), and by its context's time; values are read by their one authenticated key, so no candidates are ever gathered under a number. A page that reaches a bound has a `Next` (`{n, 0}` after a link with nothing to show) and is never `Done`. The export pages the same way. ("The bounds of a page", `Position`, L17, L24.)
6. **Ordinary failed writes created a permanent compromise verdict (F6, P2).** Accepted. A receipt without a link is no longer counted as broken: the audit links it `RecoverAfter` (ten minutes) after it was written, in a link of kind `recovered` that says so, and counts it apart; `permaily-verify` warns on it and does not fail. A lost link stays a break, and recovery does not hide it. A real break (a deletion or change) stays counted and keeps `permaily-verify` failing, since it is evidence; the incident it means is docs/operations.md's, and accepting a known break is a later decision. ("Receipts left without a link", "What /status and permaily-verify show", L6, L27.)
7. **Expired old commits invalidated the head's search while the newest was locked (F7, P2).** Accepted. Finding the head by listing needs no number's contiguity, so an expired prefix changes nothing for it, and every link below a locked head is authenticated by `prev` from it. What the lock bounds is restated: a chain is protected while its head's commit is locked, and a shorter lock shortens that for every chain. Renewing locks is a later decision, due before 2036. ("Finding the head", "The end of the lock", L28.)
8. **The audit's price and the batching and request counts omitted material terms (F8, P2).** Accepted; the numbers are corrected. "Cost" gives the audit's formula in accounts, objects listed and bytes kept, with three examples, the per-link price by occupancy and serialized size, a race's full requests, and latency as provisional with every term that adds to it. The first proposal's "$0.09 a year per 1,000 receipts" is replaced by about $6 a year per GiB kept plus $0.002 a year per account; the review's $3.65 for 1,000 one-receipt accounts becomes $1.97, since the grouped listing reads the head too. L4 asserts the known batching schedule, five links for 200 writes, not `⌈200/64⌉`. ("Cost", L4, L22.)
9. **The four pull requests' seams did not build on their own and their ownership overlapped (F9, P2).** Accepted. The contracts the consumers need are committed now: `Export` and its pages, the `Auditor` with its pass, `Head`'s verdict, `/status`'s audit and `ReceiptsStatus.Check`, and `receiptstest.Fake` to build against. Pull request 2 owns the composition and `permaily-verify`'s status checks; 3 owns a new file of `permaily-verify` and one line in each of the two mains, and merges after 2. Each has its release gate. The estimate is now about two and a half weeks. ("The contract and the seams", "Implementation pull requests", L30.)
