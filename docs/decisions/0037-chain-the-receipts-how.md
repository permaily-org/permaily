## The owner's choices

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-17, and final. The two design reviews changed how 2 and 3 are built, not what was chosen ("Changed after the design review", F2, F3, F8; "Changed after the second design review", R1, R6).

1. **What stays of a deleted account's chain: the usual ten-year lock.** Every link is a commit in the anchors bucket, locked until its lock ends: ten years from its write in production, as every commit is; deleting the account neither ends nor extends it. After the account is deleted, what stays there is one small object per link: the account's identifier, the link's number, a digest of a value no longer stored, and when; and the account's identifier in the catalogue of chains ("The catalogue of chains"). Nothing of what the receipts said. An account's identifier, its links' numbers and when they were written stay linkable after its deletion, as ADR 0030 accepted for connections.
2. **The enclave looks for deleted receipts on its own: a daily audit.** One pass a day over every chain the catalogue names, its result a strict record both enclaves report on `/status`, so `permaily-verify` fails when anything was ever found (the record of incidents, which a later clean pass does not empty) and when no pass finished in the last two days. It costs about $12 a year per GiB of receipts kept and $0.002 a year per account at today's prices ("Cost"; $6 before the second review found the audit must read strongly, R6).
3. **The person checks their chain now: the API, the export and `permaily-verify -receipts`.** A route returns the chain's head and its pages with what was found of each receipt, an export holds every link and receipt as stored, and `permaily-verify -receipts` checks an export offline against heads the person noted from the app ("What a person can verify"). The screen and the CSV stay in step 8.

## Context

Receipts are sealed entries of `receipts#<account>`, written once before the answer (ADR 0031, `internal/services/receipts`). Whoever writes the table cannot read, change or make one. They can delete one, or the whole partition, and nothing shows. `permaily-verify` warns about it ("receipts of what left are sealed but not chained yet (step 8)"), and the threat model and docs/data.md state it as the limit.

ADR 0031 left step 8 a constraint: keys' order across two enclaves is not append order, so chaining needs a fenced cutover and a manifest of what existed before, never a hash of sorted pages while writes continue. Four things are being built alongside this design and bound it. Refusals of a connection are grouped by time window, so beyond N a minute one summary receipt counts the rest (the owner's decision 1 of 2026-11-17, a new kind). #152 removes an account's data with the account. Two enclaves may write at once. Receipts in production are few.

## Decision in short

- **A chain of numbered links, each a strict anchored record made once.** An account's receipts are named by links numbered from 1 with no gap. Link `n` is the strict anchored record `receipt-link-<account>-<n>`, made with `CreateNew` and never changed. Its one commit, locked in the anchors bucket, means link `n` can be neither removed nor made twice. Each link names its receipts by identifier and SHA-256 digest of the document as stored, and the link before it by its digest (`prev`). Link 1 names a genesis derived from the account. ("The chain")
- **An account's commits kept together.** The commits of an account's links are under one prefix of the anchors bucket, ordered by number, so one listing reads the full history of every link of a range: forks included, a thousand objects a request. ("Where a link is kept")
- **Written after the receipt, before the answer.** A receipt is stored as today, then linked. `Write` returns only once a committed link names it, so nothing leaves unless its receipt is both stored and chained. Receipts of one account written at once in one enclave share a link (up to 64). Two enclaves compete for a number through `CreateNew`: the one refused reads the other's link and takes the next number. ("Appending")
- **Kind-agnostic, size-agnostic.** A link names documents and never reads what they record, so the refusal window's summary receipt and any later kind are chained the same way. The 256 KiB cap and a search's page cut are unchanged. ("Receipts of a refusal window", "Reading and checking")
- **Every link read strictly, the head found by listing.** The head is the highest number with a commit, found by listing the account's commits, never by numbers' contiguity. A walk reads every link it reports strictly, from the listing, and checks its document against the next link's `prev` (the page's top one against its commit) and every receipt against its link. A storage that cannot be read is an error; what was read and found wanting is a verdict. ("Finding the head", "Reading and checking")
- **The catalogue of chains.** An account's chain is entered in a catalogue, a numbered series of strict records of the same kind, before its link 1 is made. The audit reads the chains the catalogue names, not the accounts' list. ("The catalogue of chains")
- **What existed before is adopted; what was left without a link is recovered, for three days.** An account's first links adopt, in key order, the receipts it had without a link from more than a minute before. A receipt left without a link later (a request that failed after its entry was written, or an earlier release's) is linked by the audit from ten minutes to three days after it was written, in a link that says it was recovered; one older than that is counted `unlinked` and warned about, never recovered. The audit's writes for an account stop once its removal begins. ("Receipts written before the chain", "Receipts left without a link", "The audit")
- **What was found is kept.** Every deletion or change found, by a walk, an export, an append or the audit, is recorded before it is reported in the record of incidents, a fourth numbered series of strict records. `/status` reports how many entries it holds, and `permaily-verify` fails while it holds any: a clean pass after it, a restart, a release, the other enclave or the account's removal never takes one back. ("The record of incidents")
- **Deleted with the account.** Links' values are entries of the account's own partition, so #152's `DeletePartition` (run twice) removes them with the receipts. What stays is the commits and the catalogue's entry, the owner's choice 1. ("Retention and deleting the account")
- **Seen on `/status` and by the person.** `receipts.chained` becomes true and `receipts.chain` is `strict`. `receipts.incidents` is how many entries the record of incidents holds; `receipts.audit` the deployment's newest finished pass and what it found; `receipts.broken` what the enclave that answered found and has not recorded yet. `permaily-verify` fails on any incident, anything found, no pass or a pass over two days old. The person gets the head, the pages and an export they can check offline against heads they noted. ("What /status and permaily-verify show", "What a person can verify")
- **Cost**: about one S3 listing, one locked S3 write and one small DynamoDB write per link, about $0.011 per 1,000 links, and, measured on staging (L22, 2026-11-18), about 45 to 55 ms more per request at p50 and 70 ms at p99 one at a time, 80 to 120 ms on the first request after a restart, and up to 170 ms at p50 and 620 ms at p99 with 20 requests of one account at once. The audit, which reads strongly: about $12 a year per GiB kept, and $0.002 a year per account. ("Cost")

## The chain

**Links.** Link `n` of an account is the strict anchored record `receipts.LinkName(account, n)` = `receipt-link-<account>-<n>`, read and written through a strict `store.Anchored` of its own that reads and writes the chain's names and no other. It is made only with `CreateNew` and never written again. Its document (`receipts.Link`, version 1) holds:

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `n` | Its number, from 1 |
| `kind` | `receipts` (receipts written through the chain), `adopted` (receipts written before it, "Receipts written before the chain") or `recovered` (receipts the audit found without a link, "Receipts left without a link") |
| `at` | When the enclave that committed it began its write, by that enclave's clock |
| `prev` | The digest of link `n-1`'s document, or for link 1 the genesis, `SHA-256("permaily receipts chain v1" ‖ 0x00 ‖ account)` |
| `break` | Instead of `prev`, when link `n-1` could not be read: `lost`, `forked` or `unreadable` |
| `before`, `more` | On adopted links only: the instant before which receipts are adopted, and whether another adopted link follows |
| `receipts` | 1 to 64 refs, each the receipt's identifier and the digest of its document |

A **digest** is the SHA-256 of a document's bytes as stored, before sealing, in lowercase hexadecimal. It is never taken of a document marshalled again, so a person can recompute it from an export. The link's document is checked whenever it is written and read (`Link.Check`). It is at most 16 KiB of JSON (`LinkBytesMax`; the largest is about 8.1 KiB) and is sealed under a key from the root secret (`permaily receipts chain v1`), bound to its record name.

**Where a link is kept.** `store.Anchored` keeps each version of a record under `<name>@<version>-<hash>`, the hash the first eight bytes of the digest its commit names. A link's record table (`receipts.ChainTable`, a `store.RecordTable` over `store.Entries`) keeps that version in the account's own partition, `receipts#<account>`, under `lnk_<n in 16 hexadecimal digits>@<version>-<hash>` (`receipts.RecordKey`). This is apart from the receipts' `rcp_` keys, so `List` never meets a link, and `DeletePartition` removes links with the receipts. A link's plain name has no key. The table answers "not found" for it without reading, and refuses to store under it, so junk written there can neither stop a link from being made nor be read as one. Its commits are in the anchors bucket under `commits/g/<first 16 bytes of SHA-256 of "receipt-link-<account>" in hexadecimal>/<n in 16 hexadecimal digits>/<version>` (`receipts.CommitGroup`, which `store.S3Commits` uses for the chain's names): an account's links' commits are under one prefix, in their numbers' order, and one `ListObjectVersions` from a number reads every object version and delete marker of up to a thousand above it. Every other record's commits stay where they are.

**Why it holds.** The commit of link `n` is locked: nobody can remove it, and a second commit at its one version is a fork that every reader refuses (ADR 0016, 0026). Only an enclave of the deployment can make a commit (its MAC is under a key from the root secret). An enclave makes link `n+1` only after it has read link `n` committed, so numbers have no gaps. Every link names the one before it by digest. So, once the head is known by its commit and every link of a range has had its full history listed:

- A **deleted receipt** is a ref whose entry is missing.
- A **changed receipt** is a ref whose entry does not open for its key (moved, edited or made up, `ErrUnreadable`), or whose digest is not the ref's.
- A **receipt put back** is the same receipt, which changes nothing.
- A **deleted or replaced link** is a number below the head whose document is missing or whose digest is not what the next link's `prev` names. Its commit is still there.
- A **reordering** is a link whose document does not open for its number, or a `prev` that does not match.
- A **rollback**, an earlier copy of the partition put back, leaves every link and receipt written since missing. Their commits stay, so the head does not go back.
- A **fork**, two commits at one link's version, even one landing after a later link was made, is two object versions at the link's key in every later listing, and the link is `forked` wherever it is read.
- A **truncation** of the tail would need commits removed from the bucket, which nobody can do while they are locked.

## Appending

`receipts.Service.Write` keeps its signature (`receipts.Writer`), so the connections service does not change:

1. **The receipt**, as today: built, checked against `MaxDocumentBytes`, sealed under `Purpose`, bound to its partition and key, stored with `PutEntry` at version 0 under its time-ordered `rcp_` key. Its ref is its identifier and the digest of the JSON it sealed.
2. **The link.** The ref goes to the account's appender in this enclave (`receipts.Chain.Link`). `Write` returns once a committed link names it, or with an error when nothing is known to name it. Its request then gives nothing (answered 503), as a receipt that could not be written does today.

**The appender.** One per account in each enclave, with at most one link in flight. Refs that arrive while a link is being committed wait for the next, which takes all of them up to `LinkReceiptsMax` (64). More than `LinkWaitingMax` (256) waiting, the group in flight not counted, is refused at once, failing those writes closed. A waiter whose context ends leaves the queue and fails; a waiter already in a group in flight fails, and its ref may still be linked (more recorded than left, never less). For each group it:

1. Knows the head (`n` and the digest of link `n`'s document) from its own last link, or finds it ("Finding the head"). A chain not begun is entered in the catalogue first, and adopts ("The catalogue of chains", "Receipts written before the chain").
2. Builds link `n+1` naming the group, with `prev` the digest of link `n` (or `break`, "When the link before cannot be read") and `at` now, and makes it with `CreateNew` under `store.WriteWithin(ReceiptWriteMax)`. No value of a link is then stored more than five seconds after its write began, which account removal's second pass relies on. A chain whose head is `2^64-1` takes no more link: its writes fail.
3. On success, answers every waiting `Write` with its position (`n+1`, its index) and keeps link `n+1` as the head.
4. On `store.ErrExists`, the appender reads link `n+1` strictly. If its document is the one this group built (the same bytes, kept from the attempt and never built again), the commit is this group's own: a retried write whose first try landed is told `ErrExists`, and `CreateNew` keeps a value its commit names. That is success, as in 3. Otherwise another enclave made link `n+1` first. The appender takes it as the head and tries `n+2` with the same group, up to `LinkTries` (8) numbers and within the waiters' deadlines. The group's receipts are unchanged: only the link is built again. A lost race leaves nothing behind (`CreateNew` discards the staged value it lost with).
5. On any other error, fails the group's writes, forgets the head and finds it again next time. A link whose commit's outcome is unknown may name the group and still fail its writes. The group is never put in another link: its refs are dropped, and a ref that no committed link names is recovered by the audit ("Receipts left without a link").

**Two enclaves at once** never coordinate. They meet only at a number, where S3 takes one commit (ADR 0016) and the other enclave reads it and goes after it. Each link names receipts of one enclave's group. A receipt is in at most one link of kind `receipts` or `adopted`, because a group is rebuilt on the next number only once the link at its last try is read and is not its own; only a receipt written again with its own identity (#161), after a write whose outcome was not known, is linked again, and may then be named by two links, never left out ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", 10).

**A commit that lands late.** A commit is a PUT to S3 through the host, which can hold it. Enclave B can read link `n` empty, stage its value and send its commit, held; enclave A makes link `n` and `n+1`; whoever writes the bucket hides A's commit of `n` behind a delete marker, and B's commit then lands too. Both are told success. Nothing prevents it, and nothing need: link `n` now has two commits, every later listing of the account's links shows both, and every reader says `forked` at `n` (L5, L11). What is not done is taking link `n` as `ok` because link `n+1` names its digest: that would hide B's receipts once their entries were deleted. Every link a reader reports is read with its full history (F1).

**Finding the head.** After a restart, or once it is forgotten, the head is the highest number with a commit of this deployment's. The appender reads a hint, the greatest `lnk_` key of the partition (`store.Scanner.ScanEntriesDown`, one item), and lists the account's commits from the hint's number up (`ListObjectVersions` with `KeyMarker` there, a page per thousand objects). Each number listed is, by what the listing and the commits read say:

- **absent**: no object version that is a commit of this deployment (only delete markers, objects that are not commits, or nothing);
- **occupied**: one or more commits. It is then `ok` (its document is the one its commit names, opens and passes `Check`), `lost` (its commit is there, its value not, or not what was committed), `unreadable` (its value does not open for its name, or is not a link) or `forked` (commits of two digests, or a commit at another version than 1, which a link never has).

The head is the highest occupied number. An object version listed that this enclave has not read is read to learn whether it is a commit, from the top down, until the highest occupied number is known. When nothing at or above the hint is occupied, the hint was junk or raised and the listing starts again from number 1. A lowered or missing hint costs more listing pages; neither costs correctness, since a listing from a number shows every version above it. Finding the head is not bounded by a page's objects (R4): it lists to the end of the group, page by page, bounded only by its caller's deadline (a write's five seconds, a walk's, a run of the audit). It holds the last page listed and the two markers (`KeyMarker`, `VersionIdMarker`) each earlier page began at, so a chain of any size is found in memory of one page and a marker pair a thousand objects: when the last page holds no commit, the page before is listed again from its markers. A listing, a read or a value read that fails, or a deadline that ends first, is an error (unknown), never an absent number and never a fabricated break: appending fails closed and the walk fails. `Head` returns the number with its verdict and, when it is `ok`, the digest of its document and `at` ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", 4). With a true hint it costs one listing, one read of the head's commit and one read of its value. Nothing in it rests on numbers' contiguity, so a link whose commit's lock ended and was deleted changes nothing for finding the head (F7).

**When the link before cannot be read.** When the head (another enclave's link) is lost, forked or does not open, the new link says `break` with the reason instead of `prev`, and appending goes on. The gap stays where its commit is, and the walk shows it. Stopping would let whoever deletes one small entry stop every connection of the account. The strict `Anchored` reports the lost or forked record on `/status` as a regression of the fixed name `receipt link`, and `permaily-verify` fails on it as it does today.

**Memory.** The appenders and their heads are bounded (an account dropped finds its head again). `store.Anchored` remembers every name it reads or writes, so pull request 1 adds `Anchored.Forget(name)`, and the appender forgets link `n` once link `n+1` is known. One enclave's memory then holds one link a recent account. `store.S3Commits` keeps what it read of each object version by its version id (S3 never changes a version's bytes), so a listing of numbers already read needs no read of their commits.

## The catalogue of chains

The audit must know every chain there is, and the accounts' list cannot say it: it is an index whose entries whoever writes the table can delete, and an account missing from it would be skipped by a pass that then reports nothing found (F2). The catalogue is what the audit reads instead.

- **Its entries.** Entry `k` is the strict anchored record `receipts.CatalogueName(k)` = `receipt-chains-<k>`, made once with `CreateNew` through the same `Anchored`, its commits grouped as a link's (`commits/g/<hash of "receipt-chains">/<k>/…`), its value in `receipts-catalogue` under `cat_<k>`. Its document (`receipts.Catalogue`) holds `k`, `at`, `prev` (the digest of entry `k-1`, or `CatalogueGenesis` for entry 1) or `break`, and 1 to 64 accounts' identifiers.
- **Entered before link 1.** An appender that finds an account's chain not begun first has the account entered in the catalogue (one enclave groups the accounts it enters at once, as links group receipts; two enclaves compete for an entry's number as for a link's). Only once an entry naming the account is committed does it make link 1. So no chain is begun that the catalogue does not name. An account entered whose link 1 was never made, or entered twice (two enclaves, or a retry), costs the audit a listing.
- **Found as a chain is.** The catalogue's head is found by listing its commits; every entry is read strictly and checked against the next one's `prev`. An entry lost, forked or unreadable leaves the accounts it named unknown, so it is counted (`catalogue_lost`, `catalogue_forked`, `catalogue_unreadable`, `catalogue_prev`), and `permaily-verify` fails on it.
- **What the accounts' list is still for.** The audit also reads the accounts' list and begins the chain of an account it names that has receipts and no chain (entered, then adopting), so an account that writes nothing after the release has its receipts of before adopted too, under the fence of an account's removal ("The audit", R3). That only hastens adoption: what a pass covers is what the catalogue names.

## Receipts written before the chain

This is ADR 0031's fenced cutover and manifest, in a form that does not wait for every earlier writer to stop. That cannot be shown: the key policy may let an earlier release run.

- **Adoption.** When an appender finds an account's chain not begun, once it is entered in the catalogue, it first adopts. `before` is now less `AdoptAfter` (one minute, longer than a receipt's write and than two enclaves' clocks differ). It lists the account's `rcp_` entries whose identifiers' time is earlier than `before`, in key order, opens each and takes its digest. It makes link 1 of kind `adopted` naming the first 64, with `before` and `more` set when more remain, and further adopted links until none remain. Only then does it make the link of its own group. An account with no such receipt adopts nothing: no adopted link has no receipt, and its `before` is then not recorded, since nothing was there to adopt.
- **Continuing it.** Whoever finds the head adopted with `more` set continues it from that link's last identifier, before the same `before`. Two enclaves adopting at once are two makers of one number: one is made, and the other reads it and continues from it. An adoption whose link is then lost leaves its receipts to the audit, which recovers those that survive.
- **What adoption says.** An adopted link says the receipt existed when the chain began, with this digest, not that nothing was deleted before, nor that the table omitted nothing it held then: strong reads authenticate what they return, not what they leave out. That stays unknowable, and the person is told so. An entry that does not open is never adopted (it is counted `receipt_unreadable` by the audit).
- **Production.** There are few receipts. Each account adopts on its first receipt after the release, within that write's five seconds, or at the first audit. An account whose adoption does not finish in them has that request answered 503, and the next request or pass continues it.

## Receipts left without a link

A receipt whose `Write` stored its entry and then failed (the link's storage unavailable, a crash, the waiter's deadline), or one an earlier release wrote, has an entry and no link. Its answer did not leave if this release wrote it; it may have if an earlier one did. It is not a deletion, and is not counted as one (F6).

- **Recovered by the audit, within a window.** A receipt that no link names, whose identifier's time (sealed with it, so bound to its key, never the entry's mutable attributes) is between `RecoverWithin` (three days) and `RecoverAfter` (ten minutes) before the pass began, is linked by the audit in a link of kind `recovered`. Ten minutes is far past any write of a receipt and its link, so no `Write` still waits for it; three days is three daily passes, so one or two passes that did not finish lose nothing. From then on, deleting it shows like any other.
- **Which receipts a link names** (R2). A link names only receipts already stored, so it is written after them: its `at` is no earlier than its receipts' identifiers' times less `AdoptAfter` (a minute, the bound on two enclaves' clocks the design already rests on). Adopted links are the chain's first, in key order. So whether a receipt of time `t` within the window is named is known from the adopted links and the links whose `at` is no earlier than `t` less `AdoptAfter`: the audit holds the refs of links written since the window began, never the chain's whole history (at most `AuditWindowRefsMax`, 65,536, a chain; more fails the pass). A receipt older than the window is never recovered, whatever it looks like: an old receipt deleted and put back is the receipt it was, named by its link or not, and never relinked as new.
- **Older than the window: counted, not recovered.** A receipt older than `RecoverWithin` that no readable link names (the audit joins refs and stored receipts in one pass, "The audit") is counted `unlinked`: a write that failed and was never recovered, or an earlier release's receipt found too late. It can be deleted unseen, as a receipt before the chain can, and is not a deletion. This narrows the first revision's "recovered whenever found" (R2): recovering at any age needs the whole chain's refs in memory, or a second index, for little gain.
- **Two audits recovering at once** (R2). Recovery goes through the account's appender, and a recovered link is made only over a head whose every link since the pass read the chain was read and names none of its refs. On `ErrExists`, the audit reads the winning link, drops from its group every ref it names, and tries the next number with what remains; nothing remaining, it stops. A link above the pass's head that is lost, forked or unreadable stops recovery for that chain until the next pass. So a receipt is in at most one `recovered` link, and in no `recovered` link if a readable link names it.
- **What a recovered link says.** That the receipt was found without a link and linked late; not whether its answer left. The walk shows it so ("linked late"). A receipt a lost link named (its refs unknown once its document is gone) is recovered if it survives within the window: the lost link stays a break at its number, recorded and counted, and the recovery does not hide it. A commit that lands after the recovery (above, "A commit that lands late") lands at a number already taken, so it is a fork there.
- **Counted, not failed on.** The pass counts what it recovered (`recovered` on `/status`) and what it found unlinked (`unlinked`). `permaily-verify` prints both and warns when either is above zero (a write failed after its entry, or an earlier release is still writing), without failing.
- **Before recovery.** A receipt left without a link can be deleted unseen until the audit links it, and for ever once it is older than the window, as a receipt deleted before chaining can.

## Reading and checking

`receipts.Chain.Walk(ctx, account, before, limit)` reads the receipts before a position, the newest first: links in descending number, and in each its receipts in descending index. `{n, 0}` as `before` begins at link `n-1`. It says what it found of each:

| Of | Verdict | When |
|---|---|---|
| A link | `ok` | Its full history has one commit at version 1; its document opens for its number, passes `Check`, and its digest is what the next link's `prev` names (the page's top link, or one below a break: what its commit names) |
| | `prev` | It opens and passes, and the next link's `prev` names another digest |
| | `lost` | Its commit is there and its document is not, or is not what was committed; or its commit expired and no document is stored under the digest the next link names, or nothing above authenticates one |
| | `forked` | Two commits of different digests at its version, or a commit at another version |
| | `unreadable` | It does not open for its name, or is not a link |
| | `expired` | Its history holds no commit any more (its lock ended and it was deleted), and its document's digest is what the next link's `prev` names: its bytes are authenticated, and a fork at its number can no longer be shown (R5). Not `ok`, and not a break |
| A receipt it names | `ok` | Its entry opens for its key and its document's digest is the ref's |
| | `missing` | No entry under its key |
| | `unreadable` | The entry does not open for its partition and key |
| | `changed` | It opens, and its digest is not the ref's |

**Every link read strictly** (F1). A page lists the account's commits over its range of numbers (one `ListObjectVersions` a thousand objects): every link the page reports has its full history listed. A number with one object version at version 1 needs no read of it when its value is authenticated by the link above. A number with two or more versions has each version it has not read before read, and is `forked` if two commits of different digests are there. The page's top link is authenticated by the digest its commit names (one read of its commit, kept by version id); each link below by the `prev` of the link above, already authenticated. Below a link that is not `ok` (its document gone or not trusted), the next is authenticated by its own commit, as the top one is; a link with no commit left and nothing above to authenticate it (its lock ended below a break) is `lost`, since nothing says which value was its. The value of each link is read by its one key, `lnk_<n>@<version>-<hash>` of the digest that authenticates it (a strongly consistent `GetEntry` each, through the existing `store.Entries`; no batch read is added, R6), so nothing else stored under its number is ever read. The head's value is read the same way. A link or receipt read and found wanting is a verdict, never left out, and a page holding a verdict of a broken kind is returned only once it is recorded ("The record of incidents"). A storage that cannot be read now (a listing, a read or a value read that fails) fails the page with an error, never a verdict and never an absent link.

**The bounds of a page** (F5, R4). Apart from `limit` receipts, a page reads at most `WalkBytesMax` (8 MiB) of sealed entries (`SealedBytes` of each document, at least), visits at most `WalkLinksMax` (256) links, a link that cannot be read counting as one that can, and lists at most `WalkListedMax` (4,000) objects in one try, delete markers and objects that are not commits counted. The context's deadline bounds its time. A page that reaches the bytes or the links ends there with `Next`, inside a link or at one (`{n, 0}` after a link that holds nothing to show), and is never `Done`; only the page that reaches link 1's first receipt is `Done`. A page made only of broken links is still a page, with its `Next`. A page always holds at least one link, so the next moves on. Every page an export returns passes `ExportPage.Check`, the contract's check that the service, `receiptstest.Fake` and `permaily-verify -receipts` share (R7).

**Complete histories only** (R4). S3 lists ascending and a page reports from its top down, so a page lists its range from its lowest number up, and reports only links whose full history it listed: a member is complete once the listing has passed every object version and delete marker of its key, across responses (`S3Commits.HistoryRange` carries both of S3's markers, merges a key's versions split between two responses, and refuses a marker that does not move on). When a try reaches `WalkListedMax` before the page's top link is complete, the page lists again over half as many links, from a higher number, down to one; one link whose history alone passes `WalkListedMax` fails the page with `ErrHistoryTooLarge`, an error and never a verdict, and never a `Next` past it. So junk under an account's commits can stop its walk (denial of service, failing closed, "What it guarantees") and never make a link be skipped or shown clean. A continuation lists its own range and authenticates its own top link by its commit; one whose top link has no commit left is authenticated by the links above it, read again down to it within the page's bounds, or fails with `ErrContinuation` and is asked again ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", F3). Finding the head and the audit are not bounded by a page's objects ("Finding the head", "The audit").

**What a page's listing sees** (R4). S3 is strongly consistent, but a listing of several requests is not a snapshot. What a page says of a link is as of its history's listing: a commit landing behind the listing's markers after it passed (a late fork) is not on that page, and is on the next page or pass that lists that number, which says `forked`. A pass is never said to include a commit that landed after its member was listed.

**The 256 KiB cap and pagination.** Nothing changes for writing: a receipt is one document of at most 256 KiB, and a search's page still ends before the message that would pass it (ADR 0031, finding 10). A ref is the same size whatever the receipt's, so a link's size does not depend on its receipts. Reading is bounded by bytes, since 64 receipts at their largest are about 22 MB sealed.

## The audit

The owner chose it (choice 2). Every `AuditEvery` (a day) a pass begins, run under a lease entry so that, usually, one enclave of two runs it. The lease only shares the work: two enclaves running at once, or a lease forged, erased or replayed, cost work twice, never a pass reported that was not made.

- **What a pass reads.** The catalogue, from its head to entry 1, and every chain it names once, from its head to link 1: the account's commits listed (a request a thousand objects, with no bound but the run's deadline, R4), its head's commit read, and the account's partition read whole, strongly consistent, links from the head down and receipts through a descending `store.Scanner` cursor ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", 5; R6: no eventually consistent `Query` and no batch read; a page that comes back empty with more to read moves on, and a read that fails fails the pass). It checks each chain as a walk does, links bottom-up, every link with its full history.
- **Joining refs and receipts** (R2, R6). The two cursors advance together: links in number order (their `at` in order but for two clocks' minute), receipts in key order (their identifiers' times). A receipt of time `t` is named, if at all, by an adopted link or by a link whose `at` is between `t` less `AdoptAfter` and `t` plus `RecoverWithin`, `AuditRunMax` and `AdoptAfter`, so the pass holds the refs of the links in that window only (at most `AuditWindowRefsMax`, 65,536, a chain; more fails the pass), and adopted links' refs, in key order, as one more stream. A ref whose receipt the cursor did not return is read again with `GetEntry` before it is counted `missing` (a receipt stored behind the cursor, with a clock a minute behind, is found there); a receipt no held ref names is in flight (younger than `RecoverAfter`), recovered (within the window), or `unlinked` (older). A receipt named twice is checked against each ref and is not a fault of the storage's: only an enclave makes a link, the appender never puts a group in two links (L4, L5, L7, L27), and only a receipt written again with its own identity is named again; the first revision's count of it is dropped. The window bounds every ref held, each link naming a receipt counted, not the receipts alone ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", F4).
- **Removed accounts.** A chain that shows something broken is asked of the accounts service (`Removing(ctx, account)`, read from the account's anchored record with its whole history, `Anchored.CheckHistory`, "Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", F2), which answers live or removed: the record removed by its terminal commit, or saying it is being removed (`Removing`, written before the removal deletes anything, #152). A removed account is counted `removed`, not checked. Any error (the record lost, forked or not found, or the storage failing) fails the pass, which begins again, never taken as removed or as live. Nobody but an enclave can make that record say so.
- **The fence of an account's removal** (R3). The pass's writes for an account (a recovered or adopted link, an entry of the catalogue, link 1) are its own work, not a person's request, so they ask `Removing` first, whatever the chain showed, and begin within `AuditWriteAfterCheck` (a minute) of its answer, or ask again; each is under `WriteWithin(ReceiptWriteMax)`, and under `store.WriteBy` that minute's end, so the store stages no value past it however late the write begins ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", F1). A removed account gets no write, and work queued for it is dropped. So a value the audit stores for an account whose removal began after the check is stored within about a minute and five seconds of it, before the removal's second sweep, ten minutes after its first, removes it; a check after the removal began sees it and writes nothing. Registration without link 1 changes nothing: link 1 is one of these writes.
- **What a pass does besides counting.** It recovers receipts left without a link within the window ("Receipts left without a link") and begins the chains of accounts with receipts and no chain ("The catalogue of chains"), each under the fence above.
- **When it is finished.** A pass is finished only once it has read every entry of the catalogue up to the head it began with and every chain they name, and every finding it counts as broken is recorded ("The record of incidents"). A run lasts at most `AuditRunMax` (an hour); a pass not finished in it, or stopped by a restart or an error the storage gave, is begun again from the start an hour later, never continued from a cursor anyone could edit. A chain larger than a run can read keeps every pass from finishing, which `permaily-verify` shows (no pass for two days); with a thousand objects a listing and a megabyte a `Query` page, an hour reads millions of links.
- **Its result.** A finished pass is the strict record `receipts.AuditName(p)` = `receipt-audit-<p>`, made once with `CreateNew` (`receipts.AuditPass`: when it began and finished, the catalogue's head it covered, the chains checked, those removed, what it found by every kind, what it recovered), its commits grouped as the catalogue's. Two enclaves finishing at once with different documents make `p` and `p+1`; the same bytes are one record ("Changed while building pull request 1 (2026-11-17)", 9). Both enclaves report the newest on `/status`, found by listing as a head is, read again at most every ten minutes. So whichever enclave answers, `/status` reports the deployment's newest pass, not one enclave's. A pass's counts are what that pass found; what the deployment ever found is the record of incidents, which a later pass cannot empty (R1).
- **What it says to the person and in logs.** The person sees the same verdicts in their walk. Logs get kinds only, never a count, an account, a number or a digest; counts are on `/status`. No mail is sent: a notice to the person is step 9's.

## The record of incidents

The second design review found that a pass's counts and an enclave's counters forget (R1): a receipt deleted, found missing by one pass and put back, is clean in the next; an enclave's counters start at zero after a restart; two passes finishing out of order can leave the clean one newest. What the deployment found must outlive all of that, so it is recorded where nobody can take it back.

- **Its entries.** Entry `i` is the strict anchored record `receipts.IncidentName(i)` = `receipt-incidents-<i>`, made once with `CreateNew` through the chain's `Anchored`, its commits grouped as the catalogue's, its value in `receipts-incidents` under `inc_<i>`. Its document (`receipts.Incident`) holds `i`, `at`, `prev` (the digest of entry `i-1`, or `IncidentGenesis` for entry 1) or `break`, and 1 to 64 findings (`receipts.Finding`): a kind of `ReceiptsBrokenKinds` and where it was found (an account and a link's number, with the receipt's identifier for a receipt's kind; the entry's number for the catalogue's). Sealed, like everything of the chain.
- **Recorded before it is reported.** An enclave that finds something broken records it before anything says so: a walk's or an export's page holding a broken verdict, a `Head` that is not `ok`, an append about to write a `break`, and a pass about to finish. A record that fails fails what was to report it (the page, the append, the pass), and the finding stays in the enclave's `broken` on `/status` until a record of it is committed, so a failure to record is visible and a restart before it is not taken for nothing found. An enclave does not record again what it recorded since it started (a bounded memory of its findings); after a restart, or in the other enclave, the same finding may be recorded twice, which only adds an entry.
- **Two enclaves at once** append to it as to a chain: they meet at a number, the one refused reads the other's entry and takes the next. The record is the union of both, entry by entry.
- **What `/status` reports**: `incidents.recorded`, the record's head, found by listing its commits, and `incidents.read`, when, read at most every ten minutes. An entry whose value is lost, forked or does not open still counts, since its commit is there; so nothing but the end of the lock, ten years on, takes an entry away. `permaily-verify` fails while it is above zero, and when it was not read in the last 30 minutes (`ReceiptsIncidentsStaleAfter`) or the status does not say it.
- **Kept whatever happens after.** A clean pass, the receipt put back, a restart, a release of the same deployment (which reads the same series), the other enclave and the account's removal leave it as it is: its values are in no account's partition, and its commits are locked. Accepting a known incident, so `permaily-verify` passes again while still failing on a new one, is a later decision; neither removing an account nor restarting is that acceptance.

## What a person can verify

The owner chose it now (choice 3), with pull request 3.

- **In the app, through the API** (`GET /api/v1/receipts`, the person's session, their own account only, allowed while their terms are not agreed and while the account is closing, as the connections' pages are; a removed account has no session). The chain's head (`n`, its verdict, its digest, when) and pages of the walk, each receipt with its position and verdict, within the bounds of a page. Step 8's screen shows "receipt 1,234 was deleted from the storage" where the verdict says so, "linked late" for a recovered link, and "made available", never "received" (ADR 0031). The app shows the head's number and digest, for the person to note.
- **Offline.** `GET /api/v1/receipts/export?before=` gives pages (`receipts.ExportPage`), each within the bounds of a walk's page, with every link and receipt as stored (each document's exact bytes, base64 in JSON, never marshalled again, so a receipt of a kind or version this build does not know keeps every field) and the head when it was read. An export is its pages in order, one JSON object a line, which the app downloads in turn. `permaily-verify -receipts <file>` reads it a page at a time and recomputes every digest: `prev` link by link, every ref against its receipt, link 1 against the account's genesis. It prints the breaks, the recovered links and what each page's head was, and fails an export that reports any break ("Changed while building pull request 3", 6).
- **What the file proves, and what it does not** (F3). An export checked alone proves only that it is consistent with itself: whoever edits the file can recompute every digest above a link they changed, or the whole chain. What authenticates it is a head the person noted from the app, over the attested connection, kept apart from the file: `-receipts-head <n>:<digest>`, given once or more. The export's link `n` must have that digest, and every link below it down to the first break, with every receipt they name, is then authenticated: a head authenticates the chain below it, backwards. Links above the newest noted head, and links below a break, are printed as not authenticated by a noted head. A noted head above the export's own fails the check (the file was cut short). Whether the export is current is shown only by a head noted when it was made: the person notes the head the app shows as they export. A new deployment, with a new root secret, starts a new chain on which no earlier head is, and the check fails on it.
- **What each rests on.** Consistency and authentication up to a noted head need no trust in the enclave or the storage once the head was noted over the attested connection. Completeness rests on the attested code: no answer left without its receipt linked (`Write`) is a property of the image `permaily-verify` checks by its measurements, not of the export.

## What /status and permaily-verify show

`attestation.ReceiptsStatus`, committed with this design:

- `chained: true` and `chain: "strict"` (`attestation.ReceiptsChainStrict`).
- `incidents`: the record of incidents as the enclave last read it (`attestation.ReceiptsIncidents`: `recorded`, how many entries, and `read`, when).
- `audit`: the deployment's newest finished pass (`attestation.ReceiptsAudit`: `began`, `finished`, `catalogue`, `chains`, `removed`, `broken` by every kind of `attestation.ReceiptsBrokenKinds`, also at zero, `recovered`, `unlinked` and `expired`); absent before the first.
- `broken`: by every kind, also at zero, what the enclave that answered found by its walks, exports, appends and audits and has not recorded yet (its record pending or failed). Zero means nothing waiting to be recorded, not everything checked.

The kinds are `receipt_missing`, `receipt_unreadable`, `receipt_changed`, `link_lost`, `link_forked`, `link_unreadable`, `link_prev`, and the four of the catalogue (`catalogue_lost`, `catalogue_forked`, `catalogue_unreadable`, `catalogue_prev`). A receipt without a link is not one (`recovered`).

`permaily-verify`, built from the release's commit, applies `ReceiptsStatus.Check` with its own clock. It fails when receipts are not sealed, not chained, or the chain is not `strict` (replacing today's warning); when `incidents` is absent, was read more than 30 minutes before now or after now, or is above zero; when no pass has finished, or the newest finished more than `ReceiptsAuditStaleAfter` (48 hours) before now, or after now; when a count is below zero, a kind is missing or one it does not know is there (counted as broken); and when any count of the audit or of the enclave that answered is above zero ("the storage deleted or changed receipts or links of the record of what left"). It prints the counts, `finished`, `recovered`, `unlinked` and `expired`, and warns when any of the last three is above zero. A lost or forked link already fails it, as a regression of the record kind `receipt link`. Since `/status` names no account, someone verifying learns that something was deleted somewhere, and the person learns which receipts in their own pages.

A break found stays: it is evidence that the storage deleted or changed something, it is in the record of incidents for good, and `permaily-verify` keeps failing, even once the receipt is put back or the account removed (R1). What then is an incident (docs/operations.md, pull request 2), and it does not wait for `permaily-verify` to pass: the operator says publicly that the verifier fails and why (its message names the kinds and counts, never an account), looks for how the storage was written (the table's and the bucket's access, AWS's records of it), and keeps the deployment running, since the chain goes on past the break. The operator cannot learn which accounts or receipts were affected: the record of incidents is sealed, and the person's routes are the person's own. The person sees it in their own walk. Telling the person is step 9's notice, which the enclave can send from the record. A way for `permaily-verify` to accept an incident already known and still fail on any new one is a later decision.

A new deployment starts with no pass: `permaily-verify` fails until its first pass finishes, minutes after it starts. A new release of the same deployment reads the passes of the one before, so a release does not.

## Retention and deleting the account

- **While the account lives**, receipts and links are kept for as long as the account (ADR 0019). No route deletes one. Should a person ever choose to keep receipts for less time (the sample's "Your data" shows such a choice; docs/legal.md, item 7), that would be a link of a new kind naming what was removed by the person's choice, never a deletion the chain cannot tell from an attack: a later decision.
- **When the account is removed** (#152, ADR 0031's choice 32), accounts deletes the partition `receipts#<account>`, receipts and links' values together, and again `RemoveAgainAfter` (ten minutes) later. Every link is written under `WriteWithin(ReceiptWriteMax)`, so a link racing the removal is stored within five seconds of its write's start or not at all, and the second pass removes it. The audit's own writes for an account (recovery, adoption, the catalogue, link 1) begin within a minute of asking whether the account is being removed, and are not made once it is ("The audit", R3), so the second pass removes them too. No `RemoveRecord` is needed for links: an account's identifier is never used again, nothing reads a removed account's chain but the audit, which counts it `removed`, and a value put back opens for no route.
- **What stays.** The links' commits in the anchors bucket stay until their lock ends: ten years from each commit's write in production, a day on staging images (the owner's choice 1); the account's removal neither ends nor extends a lock, so a commit stays for what is left of its own. The catalogue's entries naming the account stay, sealed, since an entry names several accounts and is the audit's evidence: an entry of the table, not locked itself, whose commit is locked like a link's, so its deletion shows. So do the record of incidents' entries naming it, likewise, if anything of its chain was ever found broken: sealed, with its identifier, the numbers of the links and the identifiers of the receipts found, never what a receipt said; the same kind of thing choice 1 keeps. The table's point-in-time recovery keeps the deleted items for one day, as it does every deleted item (`deploy/tofu/mail.tf`).
- **Where docs/data.md and the privacy page say it.** docs/data.md gets rows for links and the catalogue. The privacy page's sentence on what stays of an account after deletion is checked against it in pull request 2, with docs/legal.md: an account's identifier, its links' numbers and times stay, not "nothing personal".

## Receipts of a refusal window

The summary receipt (the owner's decision 1 of 2026-11-17, designed in parallel) is a new `Kind` written through the same `Writer`, and the chain names it as it names any receipt. What this design asks of it, and of every later kind: **a receipt is a document written once.** A summary whose count changes is a new receipt each time it is written, or one receipt written when its window closes, never an entry updated, because a link names one digest. When its refusals are accounted for in a summary written later is that design's to settle: the chain does not turn a deferred or changing counter into a receipt written before its answer. The summary also bounds the links its refusals cost, as it bounds the writes.

## Cost

At us-east-2's prices as of this writing: S3 PUT and LIST $0.005 per 1,000, GET $0.0004 per 1,000; DynamoDB on demand $0.625 per million write units (1 KiB) and $0.125 per million read units (4 KiB strongly consistent, 8 KiB eventually). Every read here is strongly consistent (R6). A link's sealed item is about 0.45 KiB plus 0.16 KiB per ref: one write unit with one ref, eleven with 64 (`w` below). A receipt's sealed entry is taken at about 3 KiB, its usual size; at most 342 KiB.

| What | Requests | About |
|---|---|---|
| A link, made | `CreateNew`: one listing (its number's history), one locked PUT, one DynamoDB write of `w` units; the plain name is not read | $0.010 per 1,000 links plus $0.000625·`w` per 1,000: $0.011 with one ref, $0.017 with 64 |
| A race lost to the other enclave | The loser's `CreateNew` (listing, PUT refused, staged value written and discarded) and a strict read of the winner (listing, a read of its commit, its value) | $0.015 per 1,000, plus `w`+1 write units |
| Entering an account in the catalogue | One `CreateNew` of an entry, shared by the accounts one enclave enters at once | Once per account: $0.00001 |
| Finding the head after a restart | The hint, one listing from it (and a page a thousand objects above it), a read of the head's commit and its value; a junk hint, a listing of every page | Once per account and enclave: $0.0000055 with a true hint |
| A page of the walk | A listing a thousand objects of its range (again over half as many links when junk fills it), a read of its top link's commit when not kept, its links' values and its receipts (a `GetEntry` each) | $0.0000054 and a read unit per link and per 4 KiB of receipt |
| The audit, one pass | Per chain: a listing a thousand of its objects and a read of its head's commit. The catalogue the same. Every stored byte of receipts and links read once, strongly consistent; a `GetEntry` for each ref the cursor did not return, which a healthy chain has none of | Per year: $11.96 per GiB kept, plus $0.00197 per account per started thousand links |
| Recording an incident | One `CreateNew` of an entry naming up to 64 findings, only when something is found | $0.00001 an entry |
| Storage | One locked commit object of about 250 bytes per link for ten years, and one item of about 0.6 KiB per one-receipt link while the account lives | S3: under $1 per million links over ten years. DynamoDB: about $0.15 a month per million one-receipt links (at $0.25 a GB-month), a small part of what their receipts take |

The audit's year, in general: `365 × (Σ over accounts of (⌈objects/1,000⌉ × $0.000005 + $0.0000004) + bytes kept / 4 KiB × $0.000000125)`. The first revision divided by 8 KiB, the eventually consistent unit, while its algorithm read again strongly (R6); with strong reads only, the bytes' part doubles and nothing is read twice. Examples, with one receipt per link:

- **1,000 accounts with one receipt each**: about $2.02 a year, almost all listings.
- **A quiet account**, one receipt a day: about $0.010 in its first year, $0.024 in its second.
- **A busy account**, 200 receipts a day (73,000 links and about 257 MiB a year): about $1.57 in its first year, $4.70 in its second. Grouping makes a burst cheaper (fewer links and listings); bytes read do not change.
- **Broken links** cost a read of their commit each ($0.0000004) and a listing page per thousand objects, as anything listed does; junk under an account's prefix costs the same, within the bounds of a page and of a run.

With one receipt per link, the usual case for a person's mail, a receipt costs about $0.000011 more than today, about 8% of one ask of the model ($0.00013, ADR 0028). Grouping makes a burst cheaper per receipt (a full link is $0.00000027 a receipt), though a group is not always full: the first request of a burst gets a link of its own, and the rest wait for the next.

**Latency** (estimated before L22; measured below). The link adds one listing and one locked PUT in series with a small DynamoDB write, from the enclave through the host's proxy, before the answer: about 60 to 150 ms. Besides: a wait for the link in flight when several requests of one account arrive at once; a race lost (a strict read and another `CreateNew`); finding the head when cold (a hint and a listing); and, once per account, entering it in the catalogue and adopting. All of it within `ReceiptWriteMax` (five seconds) from the receipt's write, past which the request is answered 503.

## What it guarantees, and what it does not

**It guarantees** that once the release runs, no answer of a connection leaves unless its receipt is stored and named by a committed link (`Write`). After that, whoever writes the table or the bucket cannot delete, replace, move, reorder, put back an earlier copy of, or hide a receipt or a link without a walk showing it at its number, the audit counting it and `permaily-verify` failing: a link is checked with its full history, so a fork is found however late its second commit landed. Nor can they make the head go back: commits are locked, and the head is found by listing them. Nor can they hide a chain from the audit: the catalogue names it before its link 1 is made, and an entry lost is counted. Nor can they make a deletion found be forgotten: it is in the record of incidents before anyone is told, and putting the receipt back, a clean pass, a restart or removing the account leaves it there. Two enclaves keep this however they interleave.

**It does not protect against:**

- **Deletion before chaining.** A receipt deleted before its account's chain began is not known. Adoption says what existed then, not what existed before.
- **Deletion before recovery.** A receipt left without a link (its write failed after its entry, or an earlier release wrote it) can be deleted unseen until the audit links it, ten minutes to a day later, and for ever if no pass links it within three days: it is then counted `unlinked`, a warning.
- **A finding never recorded.** A deletion an enclave saw and could not record before it restarted, and that was undone before anyone looked again, is not known: nothing was reported of it either.
- **An earlier release running.** A release from before this design, which the key policy still lets run, writes unchained receipts. They are recovered if they survive, and can be deleted unseen before. Retiring those releases' keys closes it (docs/operations.md).
- **Denial of service.** Whoever writes the table or the bucket can stop an account's receipts from being linked: junk or a fork where its next number goes, objects under its commits' prefix past a page's bounds, or the storage refusing writes. Every request of its connections is then answered 503 and nothing leaves. They can likewise keep the audit from finishing a pass. That fails closed, and is visible on `/status` and to `permaily-verify`.
- **The end of the lock.** A chain is protected while its head's commit is locked, and what is protected below it narrows as older commits expire (R5). A link whose commit's lock ended and was deleted is still found and its bytes checked: its digest is what the link after names, so it is `expired`, never `ok`. What is lost with its commit is the evidence of a fork at its number: once every commit there expired, whoever writes the bucket can remove a losing branch's commit, and nothing shows that one was ever made. Below a break, a link whose commit expired has nothing to authenticate it and is `lost`. An account that writes no receipt for the lock period (ten years in production, a day on staging) has a head whose commit can then be deleted, and with it whatever is newest of its chain, as every record's (ADR 0016, "The lock has an end"). The catalogue's and the record of incidents' entries expire the same way. A shorter lock would shorten this for every chain. Extending locks is a later decision, due before the first production locks end in 2036, and must keep the evidence of forks and the catalogue's completeness, not only the newest link.
- **A new deployment.** A new root secret makes old commits no longer this deployment's, so the chain starts over. The person's noted head shows it.
- **What a receipt proves.** It still records what was made available, not what the vendor received (ADR 0031).
- **Timing.** `at` is the writing enclave's clock. Receipts' order on the chain is the order their links were committed, which can differ from their identifiers' times by a few seconds across enclaves.
- **What the operator sees.** The operator sees how many links an account has and when, in the anchors bucket and the catalogue, and how many incidents were recorded and when. While the account lives that adds nothing: the table's time-ordered keys show the same. After deletion it is what stays (the owner's choice 1).
- **Detection when nobody looks.** The audit looks every day. Between passes, a deletion is detectable whenever the chain is read, not detected until then. A commit that lands after a page or a pass listed its number is seen by the next one that lists it.
- **An export alone.** An export file proves only its own consistency; authenticity rests on a head the person noted apart from it, and currency on one noted when it was made ("What a person can verify").
- **Trust in the code.** That every answer is chained rests on the attested code, as every guarantee of the enclave does.

## Options considered

- **Periodic commitments per account** (a strict record anchoring, every minute or so, a digest over the receipts written since): fewer commits. But receipts written since the last commitment can be deleted unseen, and with a restart nothing remembers them. Closing that means waiting for the commitment before the answer, which costs what a link costs.
- **One strict record per account, appended at every receipt** (the head as a record changed at each version): the same commits, but a strict record's history is bounded (10,000 versions, 500 objects read at a time), so it needs epochs and a pointer to the current one. A link made once per number needs neither, and finding the head is a listing.
- **Each receipt its own strict record**: no contention, but a receipt is up to 342 KiB sealed in `pk=system`, every read a strict read, and nothing would say how many there should be. Numbers and `prev` do.
- **`prev` inside the receipt's document** (a chain of receipts, with no links): a receipt's document would depend on the one before, so a race between two enclaves would rewrite a receipt already stored or leave orphans, and a receipt written before the chain could not be adopted as it is. Links keep receipts written once and unchanged.
- **Links' values in `pk=system`**, with the other records: deleting the account would then need a `RemoveRecord`, and another commit, for every link. In the account's partition, `DeletePartition` removes them.
- **Stopping at a lost link**: whoever deletes one small entry would stop every connection of the account. A break recorded and appending going on keeps the evidence and the service.
- **Waiting for every earlier writer to stop before adopting** (ADR 0031's words): it cannot be shown while the key policy lets an earlier release run, so receipts after the cutover without a link are recovered instead.
- **Checking links below the head by digests alone** (the design as first proposed): a strict read only at the head and below a break. A commit that lands late makes a fork below a link that names the other value, which then hides it (F1). Rejected.
- **A link's commits each under their own prefix, every link listed on its own**: what every other record does. Reading every link strictly would cost a listing per link per walk and per daily pass, $1.83 a year per thousand links kept, growing with every link. Grouped, a thousand links cost one listing.
- **Settled links**: a commit can land only while S3 accepts its signed request, fifteen minutes from its signing, so a link listed once after that could not fork later. It rests on AWS's clock check and the enclave's clock, which nothing here has relied on; grouped listings make it unnecessary.
- **The accounts' list as the audit's discovery** (the design as first proposed): an index whoever writes the table can delete from, so an account missing from it would be skipped by a pass that reports nothing (F2). The catalogue replaces it; the list only hastens adoption.
- **A best-effort audit**: what the first proposal was, without saying so. The owner chose a daily audit that `permaily-verify` can rely on.
- **Counting every receipt without a link as broken** (the first proposal's `unlinked`): a write that failed honestly would make `permaily-verify` fail for ever (F6). Recovery links them, counted apart.
- **Renewing commits' locks** before they end, or a checkpoint per account: not needed for finding the head once it is found by listing, and a new use of S3's retention. Left for a later decision before 2036.
- **Signing the head** with the attested TLS key, so a person could show it to a third party, or check an export without noting a head: a new signing use of that key. A head noted from the app over the attested connection authenticates an export for the person. Left for a later decision.
- **Rekor or another public log for heads**: Rekor cannot answer "the newest entry of this kind" (ADR 0016), and it would publish an account's activity.
- **What was found kept only in the newest pass and each enclave's counters** (the first revision): forgotten by a clean pass, a restart, passes finishing out of order or the account's removal (R1). The record of incidents replaces it.
- **Recovering a receipt without a link at any age** (the first revision): sound only with every ref of the chain in memory, or a second index whose absence proves nothing (R2). Recovery is narrowed to three days; older ones are counted `unlinked`.
- **Eventually consistent queries and batch reads for the audit and the walk** (the first revision's price): a new store interface, its emulator tests, and a strong read again before anything is counted anyway (R6). Strong reads through the existing `store.Entries` instead, at about twice the bytes' price.
- **A continuation carrying a partial history** (R4): a cursor with authenticated state between pages. Halving a page's range is stateless and fails closed.
- **Failing on an expired link** (R5): every chain older than the lock would fail `permaily-verify` from 2036, though nothing was deleted unlawfully. `expired` says what is still known, and the decision on locks before 2036 decides the rest.

5. **The audit's reads.** Links are read from the head down, each value with one `GetEntry`, and receipts through a descending strongly consistent `store.Scanner.ScanEntriesDown` cursor, joined from the top down, in place of two ascending `ListEntries` cursors. A healthy pass reads no receipt by key (L18). "Every stored byte read once" is the join's: finding a chain's head also reads its value, which the join reads again, and finding the catalogue's head likewise; L22 counts those reads.
6. **Adopted refs share `AuditWindowRefsMax`.** The bound is on the refs the join holds at once, each occurrence counted (F4), not on an account's size: the adopted stream is descending too, and a ref is let go once its receipt is met. What fails a pass is that peak, including refs whose receipt is missing, late or named again, not how many receipts an account had before the chain. A pass that fails is never reported as finished.
7. **No grouping of accounts in the catalogue.** One enclave enters one account per catalogue entry. What an enclave keeps is bounded: appenders (1,024, a target while they are busy, not a bound on the accounts writing at once), accounts entered (4,096; one forgotten costs a duplicate entry) and findings recorded (65,536; one forgotten is recorded again).
8. **Contention fails closed.** Under L5's contention one enclave can lose `LinkTries` races in a row: that write fails (503), as "Appending" allows. The test checks the chain (no gap, no fork, no receipt in two links) and that a receipt whose write exhausted its tries is in no link; a write cancelled, or whose outcome was not known, may still be linked.
9. **The same bytes are one record.** Two enclaves that build byte-identical documents (the same instants and contents) make one record, the one both are told is theirs: two passes in the same second with the same counts are one pass. Documents that differ race to separate numbers. Not every concurrent finish adds a pass.
10. **A receipt written again with its own identity may be named twice.** #161's `writeAs` links the receipt after storing it, also on a retry whose earlier try stored it and may have linked it, so a retry can name it again: it is never left out, the audit checks each ref, and the window counts each (F4). The text's "at most one link" holds for `Write`'s receipts.
11. **Regressions name the chain's records.** The chain's `Anchored` reports a lost or forked link as a regression named by its record, which holds the account's identifier. Pull request 2 reports them under the fixed name `receipt link` and never publishes the names on `/status`; nothing of the chain logs an account, a number or a digest (L20).
12. **`receipts.ReceiptWriteMax` duplicates `connections.ReceiptWriteMax`**, since receipts cannot import connections; a test holds them equal. It bounds a write from its start, not from an authority's answer: F1.
13. **What is not here.** L19, L21 and L22 are pull request 2's (the composition, the lease, `/status`, the staging check), with L3's "the transports release no mail" (`TestMailEndToEnd`); L23, L24 and L25's offline half are pull request 3's. L16 runs through the accounts service's real removal (`RemoveDue`: the partition deleted, and again `RemoveAgainAfter` later), in the accounts package's tests, since receipts cannot import accounts.
