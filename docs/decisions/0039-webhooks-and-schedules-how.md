# 0039 Delivery on new mail and on a schedule: how (webhooks, the queue and the scheduler)

Status: Proposed. Designed on 2026-11-20 for step 6 of F2 (docs/plan.md) while the owner was away, who asked for steps 6 to 9 to be done without waiting; not built. Builds on ADR 0019 ("Delivery, three ways") and amends it ("What changes in ADR 0019 and 0030"); builds on ADR 0016 and 0026 (strict anchored records), 0024 (two enclaves), 0030 (connections, the authorization server, pushed requests, the vendor egress on vsock 8004, the release order, revocation within five seconds) and 0031 (the default rule, receipts). Its pull requests are in "Implementation pull requests"; its tests are K1 to K62. Date: 2026-11-20

## Context

ADR 0019 decided three ways for mail to reach a vendor: on demand, through the API and MCP (built in step 5, ADR 0030); on new mail, "when a message that matches arrives, Permaily calls the vendor's webhook"; and on a schedule "the vendor asked for and the person approved, in the person's time zone". It decided what a call carries: "a signed notice that there is something to fetch, never mail: the vendor fetches with its token, through the same checks, leaving the same receipts". It left the rest to this step: retries, signatures, what happens when a vendor does not answer.

What step 5 built, and this step must not weaken: a connection is a strict anchored record that cannot be put back or hidden; nothing leaves before its receipt is written and chained (ADR 0037); every read goes through the default rule first, and nothing at all leaves while the model cannot be asked (ADR 0030's owner's decision 9); a revocation stops every byte within five seconds in the other enclave (decisions 6 and 14); the only door to the open internet is the vendor egress (vsock 8004), which today carries a credential-free bodiless `GET` of a vendor's own document; nothing of a person, a token or a formula is logged. The consent request carries no delivery field today: `oauth.Pushed` takes `query`, `mail_since` and `ends_in_days`, and `contracts.AuthorizationRequest` has nothing about delivery (`contracts.ConnectionDelivery` says only whether a connection gets mail now).

## Decision in short

- **A webhook is asked for only in a pushed request**, as a `webhook` parameter (`contracts.WebhookRequest`: its URL, `new_mail`, a `schedule`), so only apps ask for one; MCP clients, which send no pushed request, get none. Its URL must be on **exactly the host of the vendor's `client_id`**, under the same URL rules, so a vendor can only have Permaily call itself, and the consent screen shows one domain. ("Where the webhook comes from")
- **The person accepts it with the request, as a whole**: the screen says the vendor is told when mail it may read arrives, or at the time chosen, and never the mail. The person's time zone comes from the browser on the consent screen, is shown, and is kept sealed in the connection; the vendor is never told it. ("The consent screen")
- **The notice holds no mail**: its type, its identifier, a subscription identifier made for the vendor (never the connection's), the `client_id`, the URL called (`aud`), when it was made, the minute to fetch from, and for a schedule its time. No address, no account, mailbox or message identifier, no count. ("The notice")
- **Signed as Standard Webhooks' asymmetric `v2a` says** (the specification at commit `3d65b18`, pinned): Ed25519 over the SHA-256 of `subscription.id.timestamp.body`, the subscription being the endpoint identifier the vendor is given in `GET /v1/connection`, by one key derived from the root secret, so both enclaves and every release that receives the secret sign with it; its public half is published at `https://api.permaily.com/.well-known/permaily-webhook-keys` and on `/status`, which `permaily-verify` compares. The specification's own libraries verify a notice (vectors computed by its JavaScript library). Replay: a timestamp within five minutes, a notice no older than its 72 hours of retries, an identifier taken once and kept for those 72 hours and ten minutes, `aud` the vendor's own URL. ("Signatures")
- **A notice of new mail only for mail that would leave, under the rules in force when it is sent**: the arrival is evaluated as a read of the connection would be, the account's rules first (ADR 0040, through its `accountrules.Source`) and the model asked where a read would ask it, so a held message (a sign-in code) never makes a notice; the match is bound to the rules' and the connection's versions it was evaluated under, checked again before every attempt, and discarded and evaluated again when either changed or its messages are gone. Progress is a `(received, mailbox, message)` cursor that always moves forward, with a separate, bounded late sweep. Nothing is evaluated, and nothing is sent, while the model or the rules cannot be used. ("New mail")
- **Schedules are two fixed choices**: every day, or every week on one weekday, at a quarter hour, in the person's time zone, from the zones compiled into the image (`time/tzdata`); a time the clocks skip runs when they change, one that happens twice runs the first time; runs missed while the service was down make one late notice, which tells the vendor to fetch from the last run it took. A scheduled notice is sent whether or not mail matches, so it says nothing of the mail. ("Schedules")
- **The queue is sealed entries that decide nothing**: one per connection with its notice in flight (frozen, identifier and body, from its first attempt), the generation waiting behind it, its lease, its progress of new mail and its schedule's coverage boundary, written at the version read so two enclaves share the work; an index of due minutes the workers list, fairly across vendors' hosts and accounts; an hourly repair that rebuilds the queue from the connections' strict records. Whether a notice may be sent is read, before every call, from the connection's strict record and the account's rules. Coalescing: at most one notice a minute per connection; ordering is not promised; at least once, the same identifier and body in every retry. ("The queue")
- **Calls go through the vendor egress (8004), HTTP/2 only, following no redirect**, a POST of at most 2 KiB with no cookie or credential, written in one TLS record or not at all, ten seconds, at most 8 KiB of the answer's headers and 4 KiB of its body read, no connection kept; at most two calls at once and 60 a minute to one vendor's host from an enclave. 2xx is taken; 410 stops the webhook for good; anything else is retried at one, five and fifteen minutes, one, three, six and twelve hours, then every twelve, for 72 hours, then dropped. The person's connection page shows the state, the next run, the last notice taken, and since when and why the calls fail, in fixed words. ("Calling a webhook", "Retries and giving up")
- **Stopping is immediate**: a call starts only under a permit the connections service registers on a read at most five seconds old that found the connection active, with an active device, its account open, the model askable and the rules readable; every stop through that enclave cuts the permit before it is acknowledged, and the other enclave's stops reach it within the five seconds, by which its notice is written in full or not at all. ("Revocation, the end and removal")
- **A notice is not a disclosure**: it writes no receipt; the vendor's fetch writes them. The consent receipt records the webhook approved; turning it off writes one too. ("Receipts")
- **`/status` and `permaily-verify`** show how notices are signed, the keys, that a notice holds no mail, the same-host rule, HTTP/2 and no redirects, the bounds, and this enclave's calls by fixed outcome; never a URL, a vendor that is not verified, a subscription or a count of webhooks. ("What /status and permaily-verify show")
- **Seven pull requests**: this one with the contract, the pure package `internal/webhook` (the URL rule, the strict request, schedules with their time zones and the coverage boundary, the notice, its `v2a` signature with independent vectors and the key set, a call, its write boundary and its outcomes, the bounds) with its tests, `webhooktest` (a played vendor that keeps identifiers as a vendor must, and a scripted sender), the service's seams (the basis, the cursor and its window, the match, the permit, the recheck, the enumeration) and `webhookstest` (the fake connections and the contract the real ones pass); then the sender, the queue and scheduler, the routes and the web app written at the same time; the connections' half once ADR 0040's rules service and its disclosure are merged; the image last. About three weeks and a half. ("Implementation pull requests")

## Where the webhook comes from

**Options.** (a) A member of the vendor's client metadata document (CIMD), as its redirect URIs are. (b) A parameter of the request, which only a pushed request can carry, as `query` is. (c) A registration of the vendor's, which there is not (ADR 0030 has no registration).

**Decision: (b), the pushed request's `webhook` parameter**, a JSON object of at most 1 KiB with no member but `url`, `new_mail` and `schedule` (`webhook.ParseRequest`, strict: read exactly through `internal/jws.Object`, as client assertions are, so names are matched byte for byte, a name twice in any case is refused and nothing may follow the object; `url` a string, `new_mail` the literal `true` or `false`, `schedule` an object read the same way with `every`, `on` and `at` strings; an unknown or case-folded member, a repeated one, a stray delimiter, a value of another type, neither `new_mail` nor a schedule are `invalid_request` with no details, as a refused `query` is). Why: a webhook is per connection, not per client: an app routes a notice to its own user by the URL it asked for (`/hooks/permaily/<its user>`), which a document shared by every person cannot say; a pushed request is a back channel the vendor writes, never the address bar, so the URL never sits in a browser's history or a log of one; and it keeps the document's contract as ADR 0030 pinned it (`oauth.Document` ignores unknown members, which would have to become meaningful). (a) is kept as the alternative if a vendor with many people sharing one receiver asks for it. MCP clients send no pushed request and so ask for no webhook; nothing of MCP changes.

**Same origin: exactly the `client_id`'s host** (`webhook.URL`). The URL passes every rule of a `client_id` (`oauth.ClientID`: `https`, no user, no port, not even `:443`, no query, no fragment, a path, no dot segment, a public DNS name of two labels or more, no IP literal, not a reserved name, not the deployment's) and its host equals the `client_id`'s host byte for byte after the same normalisation. Not the registrable domain: telling `hooks.trip-app.com` from `trip-app.com.evil.com` safely needs the public suffix list in the image, which is more trusted data, changes, and is the kind of thing a lookalike exploits; a vendor that receives elsewhere publishes its document on that host. So a vendor can make Permaily call only the host whose document it controls, the screen shows one domain for both, and the egress learns nothing new (it already reaches that host for the document).

## The consent screen

`contracts.AuthorizationRequest.Webhook` (`WebhookAsked`) says the host, whether new mail is told and the schedule. The screen (web, pull request 5) adds one item to "What it asks for", in the request's words: "Tells trip-app.com when new mail it may read arrives" and "Tells trip-app.com every Monday at 08:00, your time (Europe/Madrid)", followed by "It is told only that there is mail to fetch, never the mail; it fetches with this connection, and every message it gets is in your activity." The person accepts or refuses the request as a whole, as ADR 0019 says; the webhook cannot be removed from it on the screen (choice 13). The time zone is the browser's (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`), preselected in a picker of the zones the image knows, and sent with the approval (`AuthorizationDecision.TimeZone`); an approval of a request with a schedule without a zone the image knows is `time_zone_invalid` (`contracts.ErrTimeZoneInvalid`), and the zone is ignored for any other. It is fixed for the connection: a person who moves asks the vendor for a new request (choice 8). A request without a webhook shows nothing new.

**Asking again** (ADR 0030, the owner's decisions 7 and 10). The webhook is part of the scope: a request whose webhook is absent or the same (`webhook.Same`: the same URL, called for the same things) may be added to the client's connection as another device, and keeps the connection's webhook; any other (a webhook where there was none, another URL, another schedule) is "any other scope" and replaces the connection after fresh consent, the old one's notices ending with it. A new device never changes a webhook: it is per connection, not per device. Renewing keeps it.

## The notice

The body is `contracts.WebhookNotice`, JSON, at most 2 KiB (`webhook.Body`, which checks every member before it is sent; `TestANoticeHoldsOnlyWhatTheADRSays` pins the members):

| Member | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `v` | 1 | versioned |
| `type` | `mail.matched` or `schedule.due` | what to do |
| `id` | `ntc_` and 26 base32 characters, the `webhook-id` header too; it and the whole body are frozen when the notice's first call starts, and every retry carries both | the vendor takes it once (and keeps it 72 hours and ten minutes, "Replay") |
| `subscription` | `sub_` and 26 base32 characters, made for the connection's webhook, sealed in its record, also in `GET /v1/connection` (`VendorWebhook`); the endpoint identifier every signature is bound to | which of its tokens to fetch with, and which identifier to verify with; never the connection's identifier, which the operator sees in the table |
| `client_id`, `aud` | the vendor's client and the URL called | a notice for another URL is refused by the vendor (`webhook.Open`) |
| `created_at` | when it was made; a retry keeps it | |
| `since` | the minute to fetch from: for new mail the minute the earliest message it is about arrived in, for a schedule the coverage boundary (the scheduled time of the last scheduled notice the vendor took, or the minute the connection was made: `webhook.Coverage`) | the vendor searches `received since` it (`VendorSearch`), which the `sender` part gives every connection |
| `scheduled_at`, `late` | for a schedule: the time it is for, and whether it is sent more than 15 minutes after it or stands for more than one missed run | |

What it never holds: mail, an address, a name, a subject, an account's, a mailbox's, a connection's or a message's identifier, a count of messages, the person's time zone, a token. What it says is one bit and a minute: that mail this connection may read arrived around then, which the vendor learns anyway when it fetches that mail, and which the call's timing already says. A vendor's logs, a proxy's in front of its receiver and the host see nothing a vendor's token-bearing fetch would not tell the vendor itself.

## Signatures

**Options.** (a) HMAC-SHA256 with a secret per connection (Standard Webhooks' `v1`), handed to the vendor in the token answer and kept sealed in the connection's record (what ADR 0030's table of later steps sketched). (b) Ed25519 with one key of the enclave's under Standard Webhooks' asymmetric profile, its public half published: `v2a`, the current one, which binds each signature to an endpoint identifier the producer gives the consumer, so one key serves every endpoint; `v1a`, which it deprecated, wanted a key per endpoint. (c) A JWS of the body.

**Decision: (b), `v2a`**, as the specification says at commit `3d65b1824b6697f05a5a4584e7062bd23512fc8e` of `github.com/standard-webhooks/standard-webhooks` (`webhook.SpecRevision`; 2026-11-18, which introduced `v2a` and deprecated `v1a`). The signing key's seed is `Keys.Derive("permaily webhooks v1 wk1")` from the root secret (`webhook.KeyPurpose`), so both enclaves and every release that receives the secret sign with the same key, and no secret is stored per connection, handed through the token answer (whose shape ADR 0030 and every client already have), lost by a vendor or stolen from the table. A vendor verifies with the public key, which the api host publishes as a JWK set at `https://api.permaily.com/.well-known/permaily-webhook-keys` (`webhook.KeySet`, `Cache-Control: public, max-age=3600`, CORS `*`) over the enclave's own certificate (ADR 0012), and `/status` reports the same keys over the attested TLS key: `permaily-verify` fails when the two differ, so anyone can check that a notice was signed by the attested image. The developers' page gives each key also as the libraries take it (`whpk_` and its base64, `webhook.LibraryKey`). A notice proves it came from Permaily's enclave and for which subscription: the signature covers the subscription's identifier, so one signed for another subscription fails, whatever its `aud`.

**The signed content and headers** are the specification's: `webhook-id` (the notice's `id`), `webhook-timestamp` (Unix seconds of this call), `webhook-signature` (`v2a,` and the base64 Ed25519 signature of the SHA-256 digest of `endpoint_id.id.timestamp.body`, the endpoint identifier being the subscription's; several, separated by spaces, during a rotation). The endpoint identifier is never in a call: the vendor is given it in `GET /v1/connection` (`VendorWebhook.Subscription`) and verifies with that one, as the specification asks. None of the three parts holds a dot (`webhook.Sign` refuses one). `webhook.Sign` and `webhook.Verify` are the reference; `internal/webhook/testdata/v2a.json` holds positive and negative vectors computed by the specification's own JavaScript library at the pinned commit (`testdata/v2a.gen.ts`, nothing of ours), and the specification's own example: `TestANoticeVerifiesWithTheStandardsLibraries` checks that `Sign` writes what the library writes and `Verify` takes and refuses what it does. What those vectors prove is that a vendor using the specification's JavaScript library at that revision verifies a notice with no code of ours, and that `Sign` and `Verify` agree with it byte for byte. The repository's other libraries at that revision (Go, Python, Java, C#, PHP, Ruby, Elixir, Rust) implement the same `v2a` and are expected to agree; the vectors do not prove it for them, and pull request 1 adds the Go library's verdicts to the vectors (a test-only run outside the module, as `v2a.gen.ts` is). Nothing is claimed of a library at an earlier revision, which knows only `v1a` and does not verify a notice, nor of whether a package registry already carries that revision: the developers' page names the revision.

**Replay.** A vendor refuses a timestamp more than five minutes from its clock (`webhook.TimestampTolerance`), a signature for another subscription, a body whose `aud` is not the URL it was called on or whose `id` is not the header's, and a notice made more than 72 hours and five minutes ago (`webhook.NoticeLife`: `webhook.Open`). It keeps every identifier it took for 72 hours and ten minutes after it first took it (`webhook.DedupKeepFor`: the 72 hours of retries, each end five minutes off its clock) and takes each once: that is the vendor's documented duty, since a retry of a notice whose 2xx was lost comes freshly signed for as long as the notice is retried, and a vendor that forgets sooner processes it again (`TestAVendorTakesARetryOnceOnlyIfItKeepsIdentifiersLongEnough`). A notice older than its life is not one Permaily sends (the queue gives it up at 72 hours) and is refused, so nothing older needs keeping. A notice replayed to the vendor within its life is one it already took; one replayed to another vendor or subscription is refused by `aud` and the signature. None of them can do more than make a vendor fetch with its own token.

**Rotation.** A key is named (`wk1`). A rotation is a release that adds the next key (`wk2`), publishes both and signs with both for at least 30 days, then a release that signs with the new one alone and keeps publishing the old one for 30 days more. A vendor that cached the set an hour sees the new key before it is used alone; the libraries accept a call when any of its signatures verifies with any key they trust. A compromise of the signing key is a compromise of the root secret, which ADR 0013 covers: every key derived from it is replaced.

## New mail

**When.** A notice of new mail is made only when a message arrives that a read of the connection would give: every rule first (the default rule holds sign-in codes and password resets, and the person's own rules of ADR 0040 apply as a read applies them: learned only through `accountrules.Source`, decided only through the discloser with the `inbox.Disclosure` the connections service builds, notifying only on a `Disclosed` outcome, as ADR 0040's "The seam with delivery" says), then the consented query, the mailboxes, the mail since, evaluated together (ADR 0030, "What a read gives"). A message a rule holds, one the formulas do not match and one that is a forwarding confirmation make nothing; so a notice never tells a vendor that something it cannot have arrived.

**The model.** Rules first means asking the model where the read would ask it (the default rule's `about`, the query's own `about` and `mentions`), at arrival, not at the vendor's fetch. Options: (a) ask at arrival, through the same discloser, so the decision is kept (ADR 0028's kept answers, ADR 0031's decisions in strict records) and the vendor's fetch reuses it without a second ask; (b) notify on exact conditions alone and let a message that needs the model wait for the vendor's next fetch or schedule; (c) notify on any new mail of the connection's mailboxes, which would tell the vendor when held mail arrived. (c) is refused: it leaks what the rules hold. **Decision: (a)** (choice 5): it costs one ask per message per condition, about $0.13 per 1,000 messages that need one (ADR 0028), most of which the fetch would have paid anyway; it is bounded by the spend guard and the account's share like every ask, and by the mailbox's delivery limits (600 messages an hour). While the model cannot be asked for the account, or its rules cannot be read (`accountrules.ErrUnavailable`), nothing is evaluated and the progress stays (`contracts.ErrModelUnavailable` or `webhooks.ErrRulesUnavailable` from `Connections.Pending`): no notice is made, as no mail leaves; when both are back, what arrived meanwhile is evaluated and told.

**How an arrival is found.** `services/mailboxes` calls `inbox.Arrivals.Arrived(mailbox, received)` after the mark is written (ADR 0026, "The sequence", 6): a hint that returns at once, never fails or slows the delivery, and carries nothing of the message. The webhooks service looks up the connections whose webhook wants that mailbox's new mail (the index `webhooks#mailbox#<mailbox>`) and marks each due now. The worker that takes a connection's entry calls `Connections.Pending(connection, progress)`: the connection's own service evaluates, through the discloser with the connection's formulas and the account's rules, at most 50 messages (`ArrivalPage`; more wait for the next pass, `More`), and answers a `Match` when one would leave and the progress after them. The progress (`webhooks.Progress`) is sealed in the queue entry, so each message is evaluated once, by either enclave, after a restart, and has two parts that never stand for each other:

- **The cursor** (`webhooks.Cursor`: received time, then mailbox, then message, the order `Cursor.Compare` defines across every mailbox of the connection): an evaluation reads only the messages after it, in that order, and the cursor moves to the last one read. So it always moves forward, a burst received in one instant has a stable order, and the last message of any burst is reached in as many passes as the page says (600 messages in 12), never re-reading what the cursor passed.
- **The late sweep**: a message whose storing finished after a later one's (a delivery's deadline is a minute from its start) is received before the cursor once it is stored. Each pass first reads, within `ArrivalLag` (two minutes) before the cursor, the messages received at or after `LateFrom` that are not in `Recent` (those evaluated within the same window, at most `RecentMax`, 64, kept by `webhooks.Window`). When more than 64 were evaluated within the window, the oldest are dropped from `Recent` and `LateFrom` moves just past them, so a dropped one is never evaluated again and a message stored late among them is not told: the vendor finds it at its next fetch. The set is bounded, never the cursor, and never read as "not evaluated".

A message stored more than two minutes late is not told either. A hint lost (a crash after the mark, a full buffer) is found by the hourly repair, which evaluates every new-mail subscription: a notice can be late by up to an hour, never lost while its connection lives.

**Under the rules in force when it is sent** (design review, finding 1). A match is evaluated under a basis (`webhooks.Basis`: the version of the connection's record and the epoch and version of the account's rules record, ADR 0040's `accountrules.Applying`), and it names that basis, the progress the evaluation began at (`From`) and the earliest messages that would leave (at most 16, `MatchMessagesMax`). Before every attempt of its notice (the first, each retry, after a restart, through either enclave) the queue calls `Connections.Recheck(match)`, which reads the basis again strictly (from a cache at most five seconds old in that enclave, at once after a change made through it) and answers true only when it is the match's and one of its messages is still in one of the connection's mailboxes and within its retention. Any change (a rule saved, changed or turned on, the default turned off, a mailbox taken out of the connection or any other new version of its record), the rules record unreadable, every message of the match deleted or expired: false, and the queue discards the pending match (`changed`, or `rules_unavailable`) and evaluates again from its `From` under the current basis, so the discloser decides again and a message a new rule holds is never told. A queue state put back from before a change names the older basis and is refused the same way. A notice already being written when a rule is saved finishes, as an answer already being prepared does (ADR 0040); the next attempt is rechecked. A rule saved through the other enclave applies to every attempt that starts five seconds after it, as it does to reads. The cost is a strict read per attempt (cached for five seconds) and, after a change, one evaluation again of what the discarded match covered, whose decisions are kept records and ask the model nothing twice (ADR 0031, H22).

Evaluating writes no receipt: nothing leaves. It is not a vendor's request, so it counts against no vendor's limits, and the model's asks are the account's, as the default rule's are.

## Schedules

**Fixed choices** (`contracts.WebhookSchedule`, `webhook.CheckSchedule`): every day at `HH:MM`, or every week on one weekday at `HH:MM`, the minutes on a quarter hour; one schedule per connection. Not cron: a cron expression needs a sentence the system writes for it, has corner cases (`*/7`, day-of-month and day-of-week together) a person cannot read, and lets a vendor ask for every minute; two choices are rendered in one sentence and tested exhaustively (choice 7).

**The person's time zone**, by its IANA name, from the zones compiled into the image: `internal/webhook` imports `time/tzdata`, and the enclave has no zoneinfo on disk, so the rules a schedule follows are pinned by the image's Go toolchain and measured with it; a government's change of its zone's rules reaches schedules with the next release built on a toolchain that has it. `webhook.Zone` refuses `Local`, paths and anything but a name of the database.

**Changes of the clocks** (`webhook.Next`, tested across a year in six zones, half-hour and 45-minute offsets and a skipped day included): a wall time that happens twice (01:30 the night the clocks go back) runs the first time only; a wall time the clocks skip (02:30 the night they go forward, or a day a zone skipped, as Samoa did on 2011-12-30) runs at the instant they change, the first one at or after it on the wall. So a schedule runs once on every day it names, never twice, never not at all.

**Missed runs** (`webhook.Due`): when the service was down, or a worker late, past one or more runs, one notice is sent for the latest of them, `late` when it is more than 15 minutes after it or stands for more than one; never one per run missed (choice 9). Its `since` is the coverage boundary (design review, finding 6): the queue keeps, apart from the latest run, the scheduled time of the last scheduled notice the vendor took (a 2xx), initially the minute the connection was made (`webhook.Coverage`, rounded down since `since` is on a minute); a run missed, skipped (`model_unavailable`, `rules_unavailable`) or given up leaves it where it was, and only a notice taken moves it to that notice's `scheduled_at`. So a notice standing for Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday after a downtime since Monday's run tells the vendor to fetch from Monday's, and every missed day's mail is covered.

**What a run does.** Options: (a) send the notice at every run, whatever the mail; (b) evaluate the connection's mail at the run and send only when something new would leave. **Decision: (a)** (choice 6): a scheduled notice then says nothing of the mail (its timing is the person's schedule), needs no evaluation and no model, and is the simplest to build and test; the vendor fetches since the coverage boundary and may find nothing. (b) would tell the vendor, by silence, that nothing matched, and would ask the model on the schedule. ADR 0019's "Permaily runs the query at that time and calls the webhook" becomes "Permaily calls the webhook at that time and the vendor runs its query", which gives the vendor the same mail and tells it less. While the model cannot be asked for the account, or its rules cannot be read, a run is skipped (`model_unavailable`, `rules_unavailable`), since a fetch would get nothing (choice 12); the boundary stays, so the next notice covers it.

## The queue

**Options.** (a) Strict anchored records per connection, as connections' own. (b) Sealed entries written at the version read, deciding nothing, with every authority read from the connection's strict record. (c) Memory only.

**Decision: (b).** (c) loses notices in a restart and cannot be shared by two enclaves. (a) would make every notice a strict write (an S3 commit kept ten years) for state whose going back costs nothing: what the queue could get wrong, if whoever writes the table deleted or put back its entries, is to drop a notice (as the host can, by refusing the call) or to repeat one (which a vendor takes as a fetch more). It can never send a notice to another URL (the URL is read from the connection's record), put anything in one (the body is built from fixed members), tell a match that the rules in force now would hold (`Connections.Recheck`, "New mail"), or call after a revocation, an end, the person turning it off, a 410 or an account's deletion: before every call, the connection's record, the account's list, its standing and its rules are read strictly (`Connections.Admit`), as a read of mail would, and the call is written under the permit that read registered. ADR 0016's bar ("every record whose going back matters") is met by where the authority is, not by the queue.

**Entries** (`webhooks.Partition*`), each sealed under `PurposeSeal` and bound to its partition and key, so one moved or forged does not open:

| Entry | Holds | Written |
|---|---|---|
| `webhooks#state#<shard>`, at the connection's identifier (16 shards by a hash of it) | the notice in flight (its body, frozen from its first attempt: identifier, type, made, `since`, `scheduled_at`, `late`; for new mail its match; calls made, the next call), the generation waiting behind it (a match of new mail, merged; a scheduled run), the lease (an enclave's random name and until when), the progress of new mail (the cursor, the late sweep's window), the schedule's last run, next one and coverage boundary, the last notice taken, since when calls fail and the last outcome; at most about 8 KiB with a full window | at the version read; made by `Subscribe`, made again by the repair |
| `webhooks#due#<minute>`, at the connection's identifier | that its entry is due in that minute | made when a notice is due or a run comes; expires 72 hours after its minute |
| `webhooks#mailbox#<mailbox>`, at the connection's identifier | that its webhook wants that mailbox's new mail | made by `Subscribe` and by the repair; dropped by the repair once the connection has no webhook or no longer reaches the mailbox |

**`Subscribe`** is called by the connections service when it makes a connection with a webhook, after its record and before its code is issued, and fails the approval when it fails (the pending connection is then fenced by the hourly pass, as any approval that failed half way is): so every webhook has its entry, and no hint is needed for that. It writes the state entry (`CreateNew`'s equivalent, version 0, the cursor at the connection's making and the coverage boundary its minute), the mailbox index and the first due minute (now for new mail, the first run for a schedule), and is idempotent.

**Workers.** Each enclave lists the due minutes up to now every five seconds (from the newest it finished, and ten minutes back after a start; the repair covers older ones), and for each connection named: reads its state entry; skips it while another enclave's lease is live; writes its own lease at the version read (refused: the other enclave has it); decides what is due (the notice in flight whose next call has come, the generation waiting once the slot is free and `MinInterval` has passed since the last notice taken, a new-mail evaluation, a run); for new mail rechecks the match ("New mail"); asks `Connections.Admit`, and uses its permit only when the basis that read found is the one the match was evaluated and rechecked under (else the match is discarded, `changed`, and evaluated again under the current basis, so a rule saved between the recheck and the admission, or while the worker was delayed, is never bypassed: re-review, N2); starts its permit; calls; writes the outcome and the next due minute at the version read; deletes the due entry. **An attempt begins at the read that admits it** (re-review, N2): from that read on it is an answer being prepared (ADR 0040), so a rule saved after it lets that attempt finish, written by its `WriteBy` or not at all, and applies to the next attempt, which is rechecked and admitted afresh; a notice is frozen when its first attempt begins and written down before its call starts. An enclave that crashes mid-call leaves a lease that ends in 30 seconds, after which the other one calls again with the same notice, identifier and body, which the vendor takes once.

**Fair shares** (design review, finding 8; re-review, N3). What is due goes to bounded queues, one per canonical vendor host (the `client_id`'s host, which is the webhook's), at most 64 notices each (`HostReadyMax`) and 1,024 together (`ReadyMax`); what is past them stays in the due index for a later pass, never dropped. Admission into them is fair too: each pass reads the minute now from its start, then goes on with a sweep of the due minutes from where the last pass stopped (at most 2,048 entries a pass, the sweep starting again at most ten minutes back), so a backlog in front of an entry delays its reading by a bounded number of passes, never for good; and when every place is taken, a host with nothing ready takes the newest place of the host holding the most, which stays due, or, when every host ready holds one place only, waits in a line (one notice a host, first come first, at most 4,096 hosts), whose first takes each place that frees before any pass can give it again (review of pull request 3, finding 2), so a host found due is never kept out by others' backlogs, however many hosts are due; past 1,024 hosts ready and 4,096 in line, a host found due stays due and enters the line at a later pass. A scheduler takes from the hosts in turn (round robin), and within a host from its accounts in turn, so one vendor's backlog or one account's burst never fills every slot. Each enclave makes at most 16 calls at once and starts 600 a minute (`InFlightMax`, `AttemptsPerMinute`), of which at most two at once and 60 a minute to one host (`HostInFlightMax`, `HostAttemptsPerMinute`): a receiver that takes its whole ten seconds for every call holds two slots, and its own notices wait behind each other. **The wait is bounded by the load, not by a constant** (re-review, N3): a host's notice found due is called within one pass (five seconds), plus the rest of the enclave's minute of attempts when other hosts spent it (at most a minute), plus its turn among the k hosts with notices ready or in line, a turn starting within one call's timeout as slots free: at most 5 s + 60 s + ⌈k/16⌉ × 10 s (95 seconds for 41 hosts, 715 seconds for 1,025 hosts each holding a place, both of which K45 measures). With the budget left and fewer hosts ready than slots, that is the next free slot, at most ten seconds. **Overload policy**: when the enclave's budgets are spent nothing is dropped and nothing jumps the turn; notices wait in the ready queues or the due index, each within its 72 hours of retries, and every host keeps its turn, so a hostile vendor's backlog delays every host by at most its share and its own notices by the rest. Evaluations of new mail have their own bounds: four at once in an enclave, one per account, 120 started a minute (`EvaluationsInFlightMax`, `AccountEvaluationsInFlightMax`, `EvaluationsPerMinute`), within the model's spend guard and the account's share. The sender keeps no connection once a call ends and reads at most 8 KiB of an answer's headers (`ResponseHeaderBytesMax`) and 4 KiB of its body, so a hostile receiver costs at most two slots, ten seconds each and 12 KiB of memory per call.

**Coalescing and frozen notices** (design review, finding 2). A connection has at most one notice in flight and one generation waiting behind it. A notice's identifier and body are frozen when its first attempt starts: every retry, also one after a crash whose outcome nobody knows, carries the same identifier and the same body, signed afresh. Whatever happens meanwhile never changes it: a match of new mail goes to the waiting generation (merged with one already there: the earliest `From`, the earliest messages, at most 16), and so does a scheduled run (the latest run, `late` when it stands for more than one). When the notice in flight is taken (2xx), given up, gone (410) or dropped (its connection no longer calls, its match no longer stands), the waiting generation becomes the next notice with an identifier of its own, sent once `MinInterval` has passed since the last notice taken, after its match is rechecked. So a message that arrives while the vendor is fetching for notice N, or after N's 2xx left the vendor but before the worker wrote it, is told by a distinct notice N+1, which a vendor that took N once still takes. The two kinds share the slot and are never merged into one notice: when both wait, the scheduled one goes first (its time is the person's), and the new-mail one a minute later. 100 messages in a minute make one notice, then at most one a minute after.

**Idempotency and order.** At least once: every retry of one notice carries its identifier and body, and a new notice is made only once the one before was taken, given up or dropped. Ordering is not promised across a connection's notices or between connections; a vendor that needs order fetches by `received`.

**The hourly repair** (`RepairEvery`, design review, finding 5) walks the webhooks the connections' strict records hold, not the queue: `Connections.Webhooks` lists, a page of 100 at a time (`RepairPage`), every connection whose record has a webhook, found by the connections service from its strict records (the records' listing and each record read strictly), never from the queue or an index the queue writes. For each, it reads the subscription, and makes again what is missing or wrong from it: the state entry (`Subscribe`'s, its cursor an hour and `ArrivalLag` before the repair, `RepairEvery` back, since the previous repair found it whole; its coverage boundary the connection's making, since the last run taken is not known, which over-covers rather than skips), the due entry, the mailbox index (one per mailbox the connection reaches, the others dropped), the schedule's next run; and evaluates every new-mail subscription (a lost hint). Then it walks the state entries in each enclave under a sealed lease that only shares the work (as the receipts' audit's, ADR 0037) and drops every entry, due entry and mailbox index of a connection the listing did not name (removed, ended for more than a day, replaced) once a strict read of that connection made then finds it no longer calls: the listing is no snapshot, so a webhook subscribed or turned on again after its last page was read, or one whose read fails, stays and is rebuilt (review of pull request 3, finding 1). So deleting a subscription's state, its due entry and its mailbox index together delays its notices until the next repair, at most an hour, and the mail of that hour is still told; a schedule-only subscription is rebuilt the same way. What a deletion can cost beyond that is a notice for mail that arrived more than an hour before the repair while a repair was failing too, which the vendor finds at its next fetch or schedule, and a notice repeated, which it takes as one more fetch. What the repair cannot rebuild is a connection whose own record is gone: that connection gives no mail either, and makes no notice (fail closed).

## Calling a webhook

**Egress: the vendor egress, vsock 8004, through a dialer of its own.** Options: (a) the existing vendor egress, with a second dialer in the enclave (`enclave.WebhookEgressDialer(8004)`, the tunnelled stream the sender frames its own HTTP/2 over), as the model has its own client; (b) a third listener on the host; (c) the allowlisted proxy (8000), which cannot reach a vendor's host. (b) changes nothing a dishonest host cannot already do and adds a flag, a port and a test; the host sees the same TLS either way. **Decision: (a).** What the egress was promised to carry widens: from a bodiless `GET` of a vendor's document to also a `POST` of at most 2 KiB of a signed notice, to the same host, which holds nothing of the mail (docs/threat-model.md, "Egress allowlist", changes with pull request 1).

**The call** (`webhook.Sender`, implemented by `internal/webhook/send`, pull request 1): `POST` of the URL exactly, `Content-Type: application/json`, the three headers, no cookie, no credential, no `Referer`, no compression asked; HTTPS checked against the roots in the image, TLS 1.2 or later; **HTTP/2 only** (ALPN `h2`), as vendors' documents and the model are (ADR 0030, the owner's decision 11: Go's HTTP/1 transport writes an idle peer's unsolicited bytes to the process's log), so a receiver that speaks only HTTP/1.1 cannot be called until a separately tested path that logs nothing exists (choice 4); **no redirect followed**, wherever it points; at most ten seconds from the dial to the status (`AttemptTimeout`); at most 8 KiB of the answer's header block (`ResponseHeaderBytesMax`, also sent as `SETTINGS_MAX_HEADER_LIST_SIZE`) and 4 KiB of its body read, then the connection closed: its status decides and its body never does; no connection kept for another call (`IdleConnectionsMax`, zero); nothing of what the vendor's server sends reaches a log.

**The notice leaves within five seconds of a read that allowed it, or not at all** (design review, finding 4). The read is `Connections.Admit`, which registers a permit (`webhooks.Permit`) under the connection before it returns, as a read of mail registers its lease (`connections/lease.go`); the dial, the handshake and the write run under the context `Permit.Start` gives, which ends when the permit is cut and at `WriteBy` (`ReadAt` plus `oauth.ConnectionFresh`, never after the connection's end). Every stop made through that enclave (a revocation of the connection or of its last device, the person turning the webhook off, a replacement, an account's deletion asked, a vendor's 410 recorded) cuts the connection's permits before its record is written, so it is acknowledged only once no call of it can start writing; a stop through the other enclave reaches it by `WriteBy`. **What "written" means** is the boundary at which the bound is measured (`webhook.Call`): the request's HEADERS frame and its one DATA frame with `END_STREAM`, at most 16 KiB together (`RequestWriteBytesMax`, a TLS record's plaintext), handed to the TLS connection in a single `Write`, so they leave in one TLS record, and that `Write` returned. The sender starts that `Write` only after the server's SETTINGS, only when the peer's stream and connection windows take the whole body (a receiver that advertises less is `refused` with nothing written, since a request split by flow control would leave its headers, which say a notice exists, without its body), and only while the permit lives and `WriteBy` has not passed (else `cut` or `too_late`, nothing written). A cut or `WriteBy` that comes while that `Write` blocks (the host not reading the vsock) closes the connection, so the record is never whole and the vendor can open nothing of it; once it returned, only the answer is waited for. Go's HTTP/2 transport promises neither (it may send the headers, then wait on the peer's window for the body), so pull request 1 writes the frames itself (`golang.org/x/net/http2`'s framer and `hpack`, already in the module) over a TLS connection it dials through the egress client's dialer. Only the vendor's answer may come later; and a host that holds a whole record it was given before `WriteBy` can deliver it later, as it can any byte (ADR 0030's bound is on what Permaily writes).

**Outcomes** (`webhook.Outcome`, fixed): `sent` (a 2xx), `gone` (410), `redirect` (a 3xx), `status` (any other), `no_answer` (not reached, reset, timed out), `refused` (TLS that does not verify, no HTTP/2, or a window too small for the request), `too_late` (not written by `WriteBy`), `cut` (the permit was cut: nothing written). Only these words are counted, kept and logged; a vendor is named in a log only when it is on the verified list, else `other`.

## Retries and giving up

A notice is called at once (once `MinInterval` has passed since the last one taken), then, after each failure, at one, five and fifteen minutes, one, three, six and twelve hours, and every twelve hours after (`webhook.Backoff`), until 72 hours from when it was made (`GiveUpAfter`): about a dozen calls. Then it is dropped (`given_up`); the generation waiting behind it, or the next match or run, makes a new notice with an identifier of its own and starts again. `410 Gone` is the vendor asking to stop: the connection's record says `stopped` (`Connections.Stop`, at the version read) and no notice is made again for it; only a new request of the client turns it on (choice 14). Nothing else turns a webhook off on its own: a dead receiver costs a dozen calls per notice, which the bounds keep small (choice 10).

**What the person sees** (`contracts.ConnectionWebhook` on the connection's page): the host, what it is told and when (the schedule in their zone), its state (`on`, `off`, `stopped`), the next run, when a notice was last taken, and, while calls fail, since when and why in fixed words (`no_answer`: "trip-app.com didn't answer", `error`, `redirect`, `refused`). A button turns it off, with words that say it is final for this connection and that the vendor can still fetch on demand (`connections.WebhookAPI.WebhookOff`, at the version shown, no fresh proof, since it only protects, and allowed while new terms are not agreed, as revoking is; choice 11). **What the vendor sees** (`contracts.VendorWebhook` in `GET /v1/connection`): its subscription, the URL, what it is told, and the state; never the person's time zone, never why.

## Revocation, the end and removal

A call starts only after `Connections.Admit` answered `Calls` from a read at most five seconds old in that enclave, with a permit: the connection active and before its end, at least one device active (nobody else could fetch), the account open or only not agreed to new terms (blocked, closing, gone: no), the webhook `on`, `delivery` `delivering`, and the account's rules readable. So a revocation (of the connection, or of its last device), a replacement, the person turning it off, a vendor's 410 or an account's deletion asked cuts every permit of the connection in the enclave where it happened before it is acknowledged, and stops every notice within five seconds in the other; an end, or the model or the rules going off, is found by the next `Admit`, and a notice admitted before it is written by its `WriteBy` or not at all. A notice found waiting for a connection that no longer calls is dropped (`ended`, `no_device`, `off`); one waiting while the model or the rules cannot be used is dropped too (`model_unavailable`, `rules_unavailable`) and, for new mail, its match evaluated again from its `From` when they are back. Removing an account removes its connections' records (ADR 0030, #152): the queue's entries then read `ErrNoWebhook` and are deleted at their next touch or by the repair within the hour; they hold nothing of the mail meanwhile (times, counters, a watermark of message identifiers, sealed).

## Receipts

A notice is not a disclosure of mail and writes no receipt; what the vendor fetches after it writes the receipts of every read, as any fetch does (choice 15). Two moments are recorded, as consent: the approval's `consent` receipt carries the webhook approved (its host, what it is told, the schedule and the zone), and turning a webhook off writes a `consent` receipt of that change. The evaluation of new mail writes none, since nothing leaves; the model's asks it makes are the account's, as the default rule's, under the consent the connection recorded (docs/legal.md, engineering item 2).

## The threat model

What docs/threat-model.md says from this design on (its section "Webhooks (F2 step 6, ADR 0039; designed, not built)" is added with this pull request, and brought to present tense by pull request 6):

- **A vendor** can have Permaily call only its own `client_id` host, only after the person approved it, with a signed notice of at most 2 KiB that holds no mail, at most once a minute per connection, about a dozen times per notice. It cannot choose what a notice says, make Permaily call anyone else, or learn from a notice that held mail arrived. It learns when mail it may read arrives (to the minute), which is what it asked for and the person approved.
- **The host** sees a POST to the vendor's host and when, and its size: so roughly when mail a connection may read arrived for someone connected to that vendor, or that a schedule ran. It can drop or delay a call (the queue retries) and cannot read, forge or redirect one. If dishonest, it can relay the call to a TLS service it reaches that authenticates the vendor's name (ADR 0030, finding 19), which receives a signed notice with nothing of the mail.
- **Whoever writes the table** can delete or put back the queue's entries: a notice dropped, delayed until the repair (which rebuilds the queue from the connections' strict records), or repeated. Never one to another URL, with other content, for a match the current rules hold, or after a revocation, an end, the person turning it off or an account's deletion: those are read from strict records before every call, and a match put back names its older basis.
- **A replay** of a captured notice makes a vendor fetch at most once more with its own token, within five minutes of its signature, within its 72 hours of life, and only at the URL and for the subscription it was meant for; a vendor that keeps identifiers for 72 hours and ten minutes, as it is told to, takes it as the notice it already took.
- **A rule the person saves** applies to every attempt of a notice that starts five seconds after it, through either enclave: a match found under older rules is checked again before every attempt and discarded when the rules or the connection changed, whatever the queue holds.
- **Logs**: kinds and outcomes only; never a URL, a subscription, a notice's identifier, a time zone or a vendor that is not verified. `TestMailEndToEnd` fails if any of them appears.

## The privacy page and data

The privacy page (pull request 6, before the release) says, in present tense once released: a connection may ask to tell its app when new mail it may read arrives, or at a time the person chose; the person decides when they allow it and can turn it off; Permaily then sends the app a signed message from the enclave that there is mail to fetch, with the minute it arrived and nothing of the mail; it asks the model about a new message as soon as it arrives when the default rule or the query needs it; the person's time zone is kept with the connection and never given to the app. docs/data.md gains the rows of "The queue" and the webhook in the connection's record (with this pull request, marked designed); docs/legal.md gains engineering item 16 for the privacy page.

## Cost

At today's prices in `us-east-2`: a notice is about six write units of DynamoDB (the due entry, the lease, the outcome, the next due entry, its deletion; the state entry is under 2 KiB), $7.50 a million, and a strict read of the connection when the enclave's is older than five seconds (an S3 listing and a read), about $5 a million; the call itself is a few KiB of egress. An evaluation of new mail is one query of the mailbox's messages and, for a message that needs it, the model's ask, about $0.13 per 1,000 such messages (ADR 0028), most of which the vendor's fetch reuses. The hourly repair reads every state entry once an hour in one enclave: about $8 a year at 10,000 webhooks, and an evaluation per new-mail subscription, about $20 a year at 10,000. `time/tzdata` adds about 450 KB to the image. The state entry grows to about 8 KiB when the late sweep's window is full (a burst), eight write units instead of two then; a recheck before every attempt is one more strict read when the enclave's is older than five seconds. Measured on staging by K60, its numbers recorded here.

## What changes in ADR 0019 and 0030

- **ADR 0019**, "Delivery, three ways": a webhook is asked in a pushed request, on the vendor's own host, accepted with the request; on new mail, a notice only for mail that would leave, the model asked at arrival; on a schedule, two fixed choices in the time zone the person chose on the consent screen, and the vendor (not Permaily) runs the query when called: the notice says nothing of the mail. "What is left to design": retries, signatures and a vendor that does not answer are settled here.
- **ADR 0030**: the pushed request takes a fourth parameter (`webhook`), read exactly as client assertions are (`internal/jws.Object`); `AuthorizationRequest`, `AuthorizationDecision`, `Connection` and `VendorConnection` gain optional fields; "Asking again" counts the webhook in the scope; the vendor egress also carries notices, written under a permit the connections service registers and cuts as it does a read's lease; the table of later steps' "the webhook's secret sealed in the connection's record" becomes the subscription and the URL sealed there, with no secret (signatures are the enclave's).

## Changed after the design review (2026-11-20)

The design review of this record at `d229c89` found four P1 and six P2 problems; the orchestrator accepted all ten and chose among the review's options (the one that fails closed and is simplest). Each is answered below by number with what the design now says, where, and what is committed in this pull request; what only an implementation can show is named with its test.

1. **P1, a queued match outliving a rule change: resolved in the design and the seam.** A match is bound to the basis it was evaluated under (`webhooks.Basis`: the connection's record version and the account's rules epoch and version, ADR 0040's `accountrules.Applying`) and names where its evaluation began (`Match.From`) and its earliest messages. Before every attempt, the first and each retry, after a restart and through either enclave, `Connections.Recheck` reads the basis again strictly and answers true only when it is unchanged and one of the messages is still in a mailbox of the connection and within its retention; otherwise, or when the rules cannot be read, the match is discarded (`changed`, `rules_unavailable`) and evaluated again from `From` under the current rules, so the discloser decides again. A queue state put back from before a change names the older basis and is refused the same way. Delivery learns the rules only through `accountrules.Source` and decides only through the discloser, as ADR 0040's "The seam with delivery" says. Committed: `Basis`, `Match`, `Recheck`, `ErrRulesUnavailable`, `SkipRules`, `SkipChanged`, the fake and the contract's "a match under its basis" (a rule saved, a mailbox taken out, the messages deleted, the rules unreadable, a restored match). Shown later: K31 and K32 with the real `Source` (pull request 2), K46 against the fake (3), K59b with the real rules, the real queue and two enclaves (6). ("New mail", "Under the rules in force when it is sent")
2. **P1, coalescing reusing a consumed identifier: resolved in the design.** A notice's identifier and body are frozen when its first attempt starts and every retry carries both; what happens meanwhile goes to one waiting generation, which becomes a new notice with an identifier of its own once the one in flight is taken, given up, gone or dropped. A scheduled run and a match share the slot as two notices, the scheduled one first, never merged. Committed: the contract's words (`WebhookNotice`), `webhooktest.Vendor.Taking` for the vendor that fetches while it takes. Shown later: K37 as the review asked (pull request 3). ("Coalescing and frozen notices")
3. **P1, the watermark's livelock: resolved in the seam.** Progress is a `(received, mailbox, message)` cursor (`webhooks.Cursor`, `Compare`) that always moves forward, and a separate late sweep within `ArrivalLag` whose bounded window (`Progress.Recent`, `LateFrom`, `webhooks.Window`, `RecentMax`) drops its oldest and moves `LateFrom` past them instead of re-selecting them; a truncated set is never the cursor. Committed: the seam, the fake, the contract's "a burst" (600 messages, ten to a millisecond, in two mailboxes, the last alone leaving, late arrivals: reached in at most 14 passes, each message evaluated once), and `TestTheLateSweepsWindowIsBounded`. Shown later: K44 on the queue (3). ("How an arrival is found")
4. **P1, no revocable permit: resolved in the seam; the transport boundary defined.** `Connections.Admit` registers a `webhooks.Permit` under the connection, as a read's lease is registered, whose context (`Permit.Start`) the dial, the handshake and the write run under; every stop through that enclave cuts it (`webhook.ErrCut`, `OutcomeCut`) before it is acknowledged, and the other enclave's stops reach it by `WriteBy`. "Written" means the HEADERS and the one DATA frame handed to TLS in a single `Write` (one record, at most 16 KiB) that returned before `WriteBy`, started only after the server's SETTINGS and when the peer's windows take the whole body; a cut while the `Write` blocks closes the connection, so the record is never whole. Committed: `Permit`, `Admit`, `webhook.Call`'s boundary, `ErrCut`, `OutcomeCut`, `RequestWriteBytesMax`, the fake's permits and the contract's cuts (each stop, before `Start`, past `WriteBy`, the vendor's stop). Shown later: K18 (a cut after the handshake, a small window, a host that stops reading, a revocation between `Admit` and the write) in pull request 1, which frames requests itself; K31 through two enclaves (2). ("Calling a webhook")
5. **P2, repair cannot find deleted state: resolved in the seam.** `Connections.Webhooks` lists every connection whose strict record holds a webhook, from the records themselves; the hourly repair rebuilds the state, the due entry and the mailbox index from each subscription, then drops what the listing did not name. Committed before the parallel pull requests: `Webhooks`, `RepairPage`, the fake and the contract's "every webhook is listed from the records". Stated limit: a connection whose own record is gone is not listed, and gives no mail either. Shown later: K32b (2), K46 with state, due entry and index deleted together, schedule-only too (3). ("The hourly repair")
6. **P2, a catch-up schedule's `since`: resolved in the design and the pure package.** The queue keeps a coverage boundary apart from the latest run: the scheduled time of the last scheduled notice taken, initially the connection's making on its UTC minute (`webhook.Coverage`), unchanged by runs missed, skipped or given up; every scheduled notice's `since` is it. Committed: `Coverage` and its test, the notice's words, `Due`'s. Shown later: K43 with a fetch covering every missed day (3). ("Schedules")
7. **P2, signatures: resolved.** Notices are signed as Standard Webhooks' `v2a` at the pinned commit `3d65b18` (`webhook.SpecRevision`), bound to the subscription's identifier, which `GET /v1/connection` gives the vendor (`VendorWebhook.Subscription`). `testdata/v2a.json` holds positive and negative vectors computed by the specification's JavaScript library (`testdata/v2a.gen.ts`, none of our code) and the specification's own example; `Sign` writes what the library writes and `Verify` agrees with its verdicts. The interoperability claim is narrowed to what these prove (that library at that revision); pull request 1 adds the Go library's verdicts (K19b). `/status` says `standard-webhooks-v2a` and the revision. ("Signatures")
8. **P2, fairness: resolved in the design and the bounds.** Per canonical vendor host, at most two calls at once and 60 a minute from an enclave; bounded ready queues (64 a host, 1,024 in all, the rest left due, never dropped); a round-robin scheduler across hosts, then accounts; evaluations bounded at four at once, one an account, 120 a minute; the sender keeps no connection and reads at most 8 KiB of headers and 4 KiB of body. Committed: the constants and `/status`'s host share. Shown later: K17's header cap and K45 with a hostile, replenished backlog and a healthy vendor called within ten seconds (1, 3). ("Fair shares")
9. **P2, deduplication's retention: resolved.** The vendor's documented duty is to keep identifiers `DedupKeepFor` (72 hours and ten minutes: the retries' life and the clocks' tolerance at both ends); `webhook.Open` refuses a notice made more than `NoticeLife` (72 hours and five minutes) ago, so nothing older needs keeping, and the queue never sends one. Committed: the constants, `Open`'s check, the played vendor's expiring store (`DedupFor`) and `TestAVendorTakesARetryOnceOnlyIfItKeepsIdentifiersLongEnough` (once with the duty, every retry again with five minutes), `TestANoticeIsOpenedOnlyWithinItsLife`. Shown later: K38 with two enclaves and a lost acknowledgement (3). ("Replay")
10. **P2, the strict parser: resolved.** `ParseRequest`, its nested schedule, `Open` and `ParseKeySet` read through `internal/jws.Object` (exact names, a name twice in any case refused, nothing after the object) with every member's type checked; `Open` also takes only a body `Body` would have written. The failing cases were committed first (`TestTheParsersReadOneObjectExactly`: a stray brace or bracket, case-folded and repeated members, values of other types, invalid UTF-8; for notices and key sets too), then the parsers. ("Where the webhook comes from", K3, K7, K8)

## Options considered

- **The webhook in the vendor's document** (CIMD): per client, not per connection, so a vendor cannot route a notice to its own user by the URL, and a meaning for a member `oauth.Document` ignores today. Kept as an alternative (choice 1).
- **The registrable domain instead of the exact host**: needs the public suffix list in the image (choice 2).
- **HMAC with a secret per connection** (Standard Webhooks' `v1`): a secret to hand through the token answer, keep sealed, lose and rotate per connection; nothing attestable (choice 3).
- **A JWS of the body**: what Standard Webhooks' libraries do not verify; no gain.
- **Standard Webhooks' `v1a`** (Ed25519 over the content itself), what this design first chose: deprecated by the specification on 2026-11-18 in favour of `v2a`, and meant for a key per endpoint, so a shared key had to rely on `aud` alone; its libraries no longer prefer it (design review, finding 7).
- **A watermark with a bounded set of identifiers seen**, what this design first chose: a burst larger than the set made it select the same dropped messages again and never reach later ones (design review, finding 3).
- **Retrying a notice with its body updated** (`since` moved back, a generation counted): a vendor that took the identifier once ignores what was added (design review, finding 2).
- **Sending a pending match as it was found**, trusting the queue: a rule saved since would not apply to it (design review, finding 1).
- **Repairing from the queue's own entries**: deleted entries cannot be found by walking what is left (design review, finding 5).
- **Only global bounds on calls**: one vendor with a backlog could fill every slot (design review, finding 8).
- **Go's HTTP/2 transport for the call**: it may write the headers and wait on the peer's window for the body, so a cut could leave a notice's headers without it (design review, finding 4).
- **Mail in the notice**: refused by ADR 0019.
- **A count of matches in the notice**: says how much mail arrived beyond what the vendor can fetch, held mail never included but the timing of a burst; refused.
- **Notify on exact conditions before the rules**: tells the vendor that held mail arrived; refused. **Notify on any new mail**: the same, for every message; refused. **Exact conditions only, no model at arrival**: misses a message that needs the model until the next fetch (choice 5).
- **Evaluate at a scheduled run and skip empty ones**: tells by silence, asks the model on the schedule (choice 6).
- **Cron expressions; several schedules a connection**: unreadable corners, every minute possible (choice 7).
- **An account-wide time zone in Settings**: one more setting and its screen; a zone per connection fixed at consent is what the person saw (choice 8).
- **Every missed run sent**: a burst after a downtime with nothing more to fetch (choice 9).
- **Turning a webhook off on its own after days of failures**: one more state for the person to undo (choice 10).
- **A third listener on the host for notices**: changes nothing a dishonest host could not do; one more port (choice 4).
- **HTTP/1.1 for receivers**: Go's transport logs an idle peer's bytes; HTTP/2 only, as for documents and the model (choice 4).
- **Strict anchored records for the queue**: a ten-year commit per notice for state whose going back costs a repeated notice ("The queue").
- **Memory only**: loses notices in a restart and cannot be shared by two enclaves.
- **A receipt per notice**: a chained write per notice for what is not a disclosure (choice 15).
- **Redirects followed to the same host**: a vendor would rather fix its URL; none followed is what documents do.
- **A notice for MCP clients** (a server-sent notification, MCP's own): not what ADR 0019 decided, and stateless MCP keeps no stream; left for when a client asks.

## Choices made for the owner (to confirm)

Made on 2026-11-20 without the owner, as asked: for each, the one that fails closed and is simplest to build and test. Each can be reversed by a later decision; what reversing costs is said.

1. **The webhook's URL comes from the pushed request only**, so only apps ask for one and MCP clients get none. Alternative: a member of the vendor's document (per client), or both. Reversing: a member read by `oauth.Document` and a rule for when both are given.
2. **On exactly the `client_id`'s host.** Alternative: any host under the registrable domain, with the public suffix list in the image. Reversing: the list, its updates and a test of lookalikes.
3. **Ed25519 under Standard Webhooks' `v2a` (the specification at commit `3d65b18`), one key from the root secret, published and attested, each signature bound to the subscription's identifier given in `GET /v1/connection`** (changed after the design review, finding 7, from `v1a`, which the specification deprecated). Alternative: HMAC (`v1`) with a secret per connection given in the token answer. Reversing: the secret's life and the token answer's shape.
4. **Through the existing vendor egress (8004), HTTP/2 only, no redirect followed.** Alternative: a separate listener; a tested HTTP/1.1 path that logs nothing. Reversing: a receiver that speaks only HTTP/1.1 cannot be called until then.
5. **A notice of new mail only for mail that would leave, the model asked at arrival.** Alternative: only what exact conditions settle (no model at arrival, some notices late until the next fetch). Reversing: cheaper in the model, later for mail that needs it.
6. **A scheduled notice at every run, whatever the mail; no evaluation then.** Alternative: only when something new would leave. Reversing: an evaluation and the model at each run, and a vendor that learns by silence.
7. **Two fixed schedules (every day; every week on one weekday), on quarter hours, one a connection.** Alternative: more choices (weekdays only, twice a day), several a connection, cron. Reversing: more sentences and tests; `CheckSchedule` and `Next` are where.
8. **The person's time zone per connection, from the browser on the consent screen, fixed until a new request.** Alternative: an account-wide zone in Settings that every schedule follows. Reversing: a setting, a notice of the change, and every connection's next run recomputed.
9. **Missed runs make one late notice**, whatever the downtime, whose `since` is the last run the vendor took, so it covers every missed day (the coverage boundary, after the design review's finding 6). Alternative: skip runs missed by more than a set time. Reversing: one bound in `Due`.
10. **Retried for 72 hours, then dropped; never turned off on its own.** Alternative: turned off after several days of failures, the person told. Reversing: a state and the person's notice (step 9).
11. **The person turns a webhook off, without a fresh proof, for good; only a new request turns it on.** Alternative: off and on again on the connection's page, on with a fresh proof. Reversing: a route and a check that turning on is what was consented to.
12. **While the model cannot be asked, or the account's rules cannot be read, no notice of either kind** (scheduled ones skipped too; the rules' half added after the design review, finding 1). Alternative: send scheduled notices anyway, the vendor then fetching nothing. Reversing: one reason fewer in `Calls`.
13. **The webhook is accepted or refused with the request; it cannot be removed on the screen.** Alternative: allow without the webhook (as "new mail only" is the one thing a person may change today). Reversing: a draft field, its snapshot and a test.
14. **A 410 stops the webhook for good.** Alternative: treat it as any failure. Reversing: one outcome's meaning.
15. **No receipt per notice**; the approval's and the turning off's consent receipts carry the webhook. Alternative: a `notice` receipt per notice taken, chained as receipts are. Reversing: a kind of receipt and its cost (a chained write per notice).
16. **At most one notice a minute per connection**, everything meanwhile coalesced into the one waiting generation behind the notice in flight. Alternative: a shorter or longer interval. Reversing: one constant.
17. **A pending match is discarded, not re-sent, when the rules or the connection changed or its messages are gone, and evaluated again from where it began** (design review, finding 1). Alternative: recheck each named message under the new rules and keep the match when one still leaves. Reversing: `Recheck` asking the discloser instead of comparing the basis; more work at every attempt.
18. **A late arrival is told only while the late sweep's window holds it** (64 messages within two minutes before the cursor); past that, it is not told and the vendor's next fetch finds it (design review, finding 3). Alternative: a larger window, at a larger state entry. Reversing: one constant, `RecentMax`.
19. **One vendor's host gets at most two calls at once and 60 a minute from an enclave** (design review, finding 8). Alternative: a share proportional to its connections. Reversing: two constants and the scheduler's weights.
20. **A vendor's duty is to keep identifiers 72 hours and ten minutes**, and Permaily's notices refuse to open after 72 hours and five minutes (design review, finding 9). Alternative: accept that a vendor may process a retry twice, and say so instead of "once". Reversing: the developers' page and `NoticeLife`.
21. **The connections' half waits for ADR 0040's rules service and disclosure** instead of building against a stand-in for them (design review's rulings). Alternative: build it against `accountrulestest.Fake` and run the real rules only in the image's pull request. Reversing: a later merge, and K31 and K32 with fakes until then.

## Open questions

1. **Who uses it first.** No client of the five at launch takes webhooks (MCP clients send no pushed request), and no app has asked yet. Building step 6 now, as docs/plan.md orders, or after a first app asks, is the owner's call; this design stands either way.
2. **The developers' page** (`https://permaily.com/developers/`) is where a vendor reads how to ask for a webhook and verify a notice; it does not exist yet (ADR 0030 named it in the server's metadata). Pull request 6 needs it, or this step ships without a public description.
3. **Zones' rules** follow the Go toolchain's `time/tzdata`; a country that changes its rules with little notice is served late until a release. Whether that needs its own release process (a toolchain update alone) is for docs/releasing.md.
4. **Step 9's notices by mail** may want to tell the person when a webhook stopped (410) or has failed for 72 hours; this design shows it on the connection's page only.
5. **The specification's packages.** `v2a` is two days old at this revision; whether npm, PyPI and the Go proxy carry a release with it by step 6's release decides whether the developers' page can point vendors at a package version or only at the repository's revision.

## What it rests on

The vendor's TLS, checked in the enclave with the image's roots; Ed25519 and the root secret's confidentiality (ADR 0013); the strict records of connections and the account's list (ADR 0016, 0030) for every authority; the default rule and the decisions kept in strict records (ADR 0031) for what a notice of new mail may say; the Go toolchain's `time/tzdata` for the zones' rules; and Standard Webhooks' `v2a` at the pinned revision, which its libraries verify; ADR 0040's `accountrules.Source` and the discloser for which messages a notice may be about.
