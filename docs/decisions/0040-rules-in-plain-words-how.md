## Context

ADR 0019 promised that a person's own rules hold a message back, or remove details from it, before any query, for every connection, every one but some, or only those picked; that the person writes a rule in plain words, a model turns it into a formula, and the person confirms it by the sentence the system writes; that removing details covers whole parts, data with a fixed shape that needs no model (addresses, phone numbers, account and card numbers, numeric codes, links), and meaning, sentence by sentence; and that when in doubt nothing leaves. ADR 0020 chose OpenAI's Responses API, with the answer bound to a schema and `store: false`, for words, and the Decisions API, asked sentence by sentence, for removing by meaning, so that what leaves is always the original text minus pieces.

Step 5 built the default rule and met step 7's prerequisite from ADR 0028's design review for every rule that protects: a rule's value that rests on the model is a decision begun in a strict anchored record of the mailbox's day before the model is asked, settled after, and a decision whose settlement was lost, or a record that cannot be read, holds the message (ADR 0031, "Holds that cannot be undone"). ADR 0031 left step 7 the person's rules as a strict anchored record beside the compiled default, which also says whether the default is off; rules for some connections as a field of each rule; removing details as a new effect and a new view of the text (`inbox.TextView`); and `Decisions` serving every rule that protects.

What this record must decide, each with the alternatives weighed: the rules' record and how a change applies to mail already decided and to answers in flight; scope, and connections made later; words to a formula through the model, what the person confirms, and the model unavailable; holding back against removing, and their order with the default rule and the queries; fixed shapes exactly; meaning sentence by sentence, with its cost bounds; rollback resistance for every model-dependent decision of a rule; and how rules show in previews, the consent screen, receipts, `/status`, `permaily-verify` and the web app, with the threat model, the privacy page and the costs.

## Decision in short

- **One record of rules per account**: `rules-<account>-<epoch>`, a strict anchored record (ADR 0016, 0026), sealed under a key from the root secret, made with `CreateNew` at the account's first change and written at the version read; at most 16 rules of at most 16 KiB each, 300 KiB of JSON, rolling to its next epoch at 400 versions, the earlier one removed, as the account's list of connections does (ADR 0030, C2), so a cold strict read never passes `store.MaxHistoryReads`. It says whether the default rule is off and which rules' notice the account agreed to. An account without one has the default on and nothing else: every account today, so nothing migrates. ("The rules' record")
- **Every change needs a fresh proof** (ADR 0022), names the record's version the screen read, and is recorded in a receipt of a new kind, `rules`, before the record is written (a receipt of a change that then lost says more than happened; a change is never made without one). A rule's version changes with its criteria, effect or removal, whose decisions are then made afresh; its name, scope and switch change no version. A change applies to every request that begins after it: at once through the enclave that saved it, within five seconds through the other, never undone by an earlier copy; an answer already being prepared finishes under the rules it began with, which its receipt names, and the confirmation says so. ("Changes, and what they apply to")
- **Scope by client**: all connections, all but those of the clients listed, or only theirs; a client is the app's `client_id`, matched exactly and shown by it (its host and document path, `contracts.ClientShown`), never by its domain alone, so a replacement, a device added or a later connection of the same app keeps the rule's scope; a connection of a new app, or of an app that moved its document, is covered by "all" and "all but", never by "only". ("Scope")
- **Words to a formula**: the person's words (at most 1,000 characters) and nothing of their mail or account go to OpenAI's Responses API with a prompt pinned in the image (`internal/rulewords`, its version in `/status`), `store: false`, the answer bound to a strict schema of criteria, effect and removal; the enclave checks it as it checks a vendor's formula, the system writes the sentence, and nothing applies until the person saves it, confirming that sentence with a fresh proof. With the model unavailable, words are refused (`model_unavailable`) and the editor builds the rule from conditions with no model. The words are kept nowhere. ("Plain words")
- **A rules' notice of its own**, per account, agreed before words are sent and before a rule a model decides any of is saved; a rule saved under another notice asks nothing and applies wherever it would need the model, until the person agrees and saves it again. The model's notice of connections is unchanged, so no connection stops. ("The rules' notice")
- **The order, on one view**: on the message as stored, every rule that holds (the default first), read for a rule; then every rule that removes and applies, each removal decided on **one fixed view of the message** (its subject, a line break and its text, whatever the connection reads and whatever other rules apply) as spans of that view, and all of them joined by union; then the connection's formulas on what is left, never on the original; then the parts the connection reads. A message held is never also partly given. ("The order")
- **Fixed shapes**, no model: six kinds, exactly specified over Unicode text (any script's digits, invisible characters transparent, NFKC for the at sign), over-removing on purpose (a digit group at or above a kind's least length is removed however long it is, so a number written next to another is never let out; dates, years and amounts are numbers; a domain under an ICANN suffix is a link), on the subject and the text only (the HTML never leaves); a rule that removes shapes or topics removes the attachments and the sender too, which it does not look into. Each piece becomes `[removed]`. ("Fixed shapes")
- **Meaning, sentence by sentence**: the message's one view, split by a fixed rule into at most 128 sentences within 32 KiB, is sent with numbered sentences, every fixed shape of every kind masked, and one statement per sentence and topic to the Decisions API (a new prompt version); a sentence is kept only when every topic answered below 0.30; what is past the bounds, refused, in doubt or unanswered is removed. The outcome is a decision of `inbox.Decisions`, one per message and rule whatever the connection, begun before anything is asked and settled with the sentences kept and the digest of the sentence map they are numbered in, so it can never be undone and never applied to other sentences; a decision that cannot be made or used, or whose map is not the message's, removes the whole subject and text. ("Removing by meaning")
- **Rollback resistance for every rule**: the default rule's mechanism, unchanged, for every rule's model-dependent applicability, and the same records, with one new field (the sentences kept with their map's digest, `inbox.MeaningKept`), for meaning. ("Decisions that cannot be undone")
- **Seen everywhere it acts**: the consent screen lists the rules that will apply to that app and its preview marks what they hold and remove; receipts record each rule's removal (never the content) and every change of rules; `/status` publishes the rules' design constants and prompt versions and `permaily-verify` checks them against the source; delivery (ADR 0039) learns rules only through `accountrules.Source` and decides whether to notify only through the discloser. ("Previews and consent", "Receipts", "/status and permaily-verify", "The seam with delivery")

## The rules' record

**Where.** One strict anchored record per account and epoch, `rules.RecordName(account, epoch)` (`rules-<account>-<epoch>`), in the composition's strict `store.Anchored` (as the connections' records are), sealed with `envelope.SealText` under a key derived from the root secret (`permaily rules v1`) and bound to its name, so whoever writes the table can neither read nor change it. Its document is `rules.Record` (committed with this design): the format, the account, the epoch, `next` once the next epoch holds the rules, whether the default is off and when it last changed, the rules' notice agreed (`version`, `at`), and the rules (`rules.Stored`: an identifier `rul_` and 26 base32 characters, random; the rule's version; the draft as saved, canonical; the draft's identifier; the notice it was saved under when it asks a model; when made and last changed).

**Made and written.** The first change of an account's rules makes epoch 0 with `CreateNew`; every later change reads the newest epoch strictly and now (`GetRecordAt`), applies the change, and writes it at the version read (`PutRecordAt`); `store.ErrChanged` is answered `rules_changed`, never retried with the person's change on another state, since the screen named the version it read (`RuleSave.Version`). An account without a record has the default on and no rule: every account of step 5, so nothing migrates.

**Bounds.** At most `contracts.MaxRules` (16) rules, each at most `MaxRuleBytes` (16 KiB of canonical JSON), at most `MaxRuleCriteria` (8) criteria, each a formula within the query language's limits, at most `MaxRuleModelConditions` (8) conditions a model decides in a rule and `MaxAccountModelConditions` (32) over the account's rules that are on (so every rule's statements about one message fit one call with the default's and a query's, `decide.MaxStatementsPerCall` being 64), at most `MaxRuleTopics` (3) topics in a removal and `MaxMeaningRules` (4) rules that remove by meaning. The record is at most `rules.RecordBytesMax` (300 KiB of JSON before sealing, within the 400 KiB item: 16 rules at their largest are 256 KiB). It rolls over as the account's list of connections does (ADR 0030, C2; `connections.ListEpochVersions`): the write that takes an epoch to `rules.RecordVersionsMax` (400 versions, `rules.Rotate`) writes it `next`, then the next is made with `CreateNew` holding everything, and the epoch before is removed (`RemoveRecord`) by the next epoch's first later write, its removal's commit staying as its fence. That removal is finished, never only begun (review of #190, finding 1): each prune also removes again the last epoch removed before (`RemoveRecord` deletes what is stored of a removed record and adds no commit), and an enclave stops pruning only once all of it succeeded, so a removal whose commit landed and whose deletes failed is finished by the next write, here or after a restart; each write of the record is bounded by `accountrules.WriteMax` (`store.WriteWithin`, 10 minutes, `connections.WriteMax`'s reasons), and the epochs removed are removed once more `accountrules.RemoveAgainAfter` (30 minutes) later by a write of the enclave that removed them and again by the next rollover's prune, for a value a write under way at their removal staged after it. So an epoch's history, the rollover's and the removal's commits included, stays below `store.MaxHistoryReads` (500 objects a cold strict read fetches), and a restarted enclave's first strict read of it never fails for its length. A crash between `next` and the next epoch leaves a `next` whose epoch a strict read finds never written, which the next writer makes; the newest epoch is found from the last this enclave saw, following `next`, as `connections.readList` does; past a removed epoch, the newest epoch ever written (every epoch from 0 to it has a commit, and none after it) is found by doubling the step and then halving it, about twice the logarithm of the epochs passed in strict reads, each of one epoch's history, so a restarted enclave reaches the current epoch however many rollovers came before it, not only within 400 (review of #190, finding 2). The storage's bound is not raised for this record.

**Read.** `accountrules.Source.Applying(ctx, account, client)` answers the rules a connection of `client` applies, in order (`rules.Record.For`, committed): the default first unless off, then the person's rules that are on and whose scope covers the client, those that hold before those that remove, each in the order made. A cache per account in each enclave, at most `accountrules.Fresh` (five seconds) old, read again strictly once older and at once after a change made through this enclave. A cached read's age counts from when that read **began**, not from when it answered, and a read that fails never makes a cached one younger: once the cached read is `Fresh` old it is not used, and `Applying` answers `ErrUnavailable` until a strict read succeeds. A record lost (`store.ErrLost`), forked (`store.ErrForked`), that does not open, or a rule in it that does not compile, is `accountrules.ErrUnavailable`: the account's connections are answered 503 with `Retry-After` and give nothing, the default alone included, counted on `/status` (`rules_unreadable`) and logged by kind. **An account has no record** (the default alone) only when the strict read says its epoch 0 was never written, which the anchors tell from a record lost or rolled back (ADR 0016, 0026); any error of the read, the open or the compile is `ErrUnavailable`, never taken for absence. Nothing is ever answered from a record that could not be read.

**Removed with the account.** Removing an account removes every epoch (`RemoveRecord`, walking from 0 by `next`, an epoch removed already and the fence after the last removed again so that a removal whose deletes failed is finished by the next pass) with its other records, and again in the account's last pass `connections.RemoveAgainAfter` later, for a value a write under way staged after it, in the removal's sweep (ADR 0030's `EndAll`); a later account starts with none (U30).

## Scope

A rule is for every connection (`all`), every one but those of the clients listed (`all_except`), or only theirs (`only`), at most 16 clients (`contracts.RuleScope`). **A client is the app's `client_id`** (its document's URL, ADR 0030), matched exactly, not a connection's identifier. **It is shown by what tells it from every other client**: `contracts.ClientShown`, the `client_id` without `https://` (its host and its document's path, so `vendor.example/client-a.json` and `vendor.example/client-b.json` are two apps on one screen), in the editor's picker, in `RuleCheck.For` and `RuleCheck.Clients` and on the consent screen, with the name the client's own document gives beside it labelled as the vendor's words ("calls itself Client A"), never in place of it. A client that moves its document is a **new app for scope**: an `only` rule naming the old `client_id` does not cover it, an `all_except` rule does, and its consent screen lists the rules that will apply to it before Allow. Clients are never collapsed by domain, which would widen an exception to apps the person did not pick. A client is named rather than a connection because a client has at most one active connection, which a new request replaces on fresh consent, and devices are added to it; naming the client keeps the rule's scope across a replacement and a device without rewriting the rules' record in the same commit as the connection's, which the store cannot do. **A connection made later** is covered by `all` and `all_except` (unless its client is listed) and never by `only`; its consent screen shows which rules will apply to it, by their sentences, before Allow. A scope this release cannot read covers every client (`rules.Scope.Covers`): a rule protects, so it errs toward applying.

The default rule is on or off for every connection; it has no scope and its criteria cannot be edited (`default_not_editable`).

## Plain words

**What is sent.** `POST /api/v1/rules/words` `{words, notice}`: 1 to 1,000 characters of the person's own words, and the rules' notice the screen showed (`rules_notice_needed` when the account has not agreed to the one in force). The enclave sends OpenAI's Responses API the pinned developer message, the JSON schema of the answer, and the words as the user's message: nothing of the person's mail, mailboxes, connections, account or address (a rule that names "my home address" is turned into a topic, "a home address", as the default rule's topics are asked with nothing of the account, ADR 0028's owner's decision 2). `store: false`, no tools, no previous response, `max_output_tokens` 2,000, structured output in strict mode. The words are kept nowhere: not in the record, a receipt, a log line or a cache.

**The prompt, pinned.** `internal/rulewords` (pure, depguard's `rulewords-is-pure`, as `internal/decide` is): the developer message, the answer's JSON schema generated from the contract's types (criteria as formulas of the query language with its variables and operators as enums, the effect, the removal's parts, the six shape kinds as an enum, topics as strings of at most 100 characters), the model's name, and `rulewords.PromptVersion` (`w1-<date>`), which names every byte of them: a change of a word is a new version, never an edit. `/status` shows the version and `permaily-verify` compares it with the source. The adapter (`internal/rulewords/openai`) makes one attempt a call through the model's egress, as `internal/decide/openai` does.

**What the person confirms.** The answer is checked as a vendor's formula is (`query.Parse` with the account's mailboxes, so a mailbox the model invents is refused), with the rule's bounds; a criteria that never matches, an unknown variable or operator, a shape kind that is not one of the six, a topic the language refuses, an effect with the wrong removal, or anything outside the schema, is `rule_words_unusable`, and nothing is proposed. A usable answer is answered as a `RuleProposal`: the draft made canonical, with `scope: all` and `on: true` (the model knows nothing of the account's connections), and the system's own sentence of its criteria (`query.RenderRule`), its effect and its scope in fixed words (`RuleCheck.Does`, `For`), with the parts a model decides marked. The model's own text is never shown. The person reads that, may change any of it in the editor, and saves: `POST /api/v1/rules` with the draft and its `draft_id`, which the server works out again and refuses (`rule_changed`) when it differs, so what is saved is exactly what the screen showed. Nothing applies before the save.

**The model unavailable.** No key, the provider refusing it, the month's or the provider's limit, the account's day share, a failure or a timeout: `model_unavailable` for words, nothing proposed, nothing saved. The editor then builds the rule from fields, operators and values with no model, and saving needs no model either: a rule with `about` or `mentions` saved while the model is off is held wherever it needs the model until the model answers (it protects). Each words call is reserved through the same guard before it is sent (`decisions.Service.Write`, at its upper bound: the developer message, the schema and the words at the provider's input price, plus 2,000 output tokens at its output price, compiled with the prompt), in the account's day share, and settled at what the answer reports; at most 30 words calls an account a day in each enclave besides the share (the rate a person writes; in memory, like ADR 0017's limits).

## The rules' notice

ADR 0028 requires each disclosure of mail to OpenAI to run on a consent the person gave, recorded with what was shown, its version and its time, and docs/legal.md's engineering item 2 says a rule of the person's records its own in step 7. The model's notice of connections (version 2026-11-16) says what is sent to decide a condition and that the Sign-in and security rule's decisions are kept; it does not say that a person's words are sent, nor that their own rules keep decisions, nor that sentences are sent for removing by meaning. Changing it would stop every connection until renewed (ADR 0030, finding 18). So the rules have **their own notice**, `domain.RulesNoticeVersion` and `web/src/lib/rules-notice.ts` (a test compares them, as the model's notice's does), whose words are in docs/web.md: what is sent for words, for a rule a model decides and for removing by meaning; that the words are not kept; what OpenAI keeps while zero data retention is not approved; how long a rule's decisions are kept. The account agrees once (`POST /api/v1/account/rules-notice {version}`), recorded in the rules' record with its time; each rule a model decides any of records the notice it was saved under. While the account has not agreed to the notice in force, or a rule was saved under another, that rule asks nothing (`rules.Rule.ModelOff`): its model atoms stay Unknown, so a rule that holds holds wherever its value needs the model, and a removal by meaning removes the whole text; the rules page says so and asks the person to agree and save it again. Nothing is ever sent for a rule without the consent in force.

## The order

For each message a connection's request considers, `inbox.Discloser` evaluates, in this order:

1. **Every rule that holds**, on the message as stored: the default first (unless off), then the person's, each read for a rule (True or Unknown applies), a model-dependent value decided through `Decisions` (ADR 0031). A message one of them applies to is held: not given, and the vendor told nothing (`not_found`), as today.
2. **Every rule that removes and applies**, each read for a rule on the message as stored, a model-dependent value decided through `Decisions` as a hold is: a rule whose applicability is Unknown applies.
3. **The removals, on one view.** Every removal is decided and applied on **the message's one view**, `redact.NewView(subject, text)`: the subject, one line break, and the text as `inbox.MessageText` gives it (at most 256 KiB, the HTML's text when there is no plain part), the same whatever parts the connection reads and whatever other rules apply. Each applying rule's removal is a set of byte spans of that view (`redact.Span`): a whole subject or text is its span (`View.Subject`, `View.Body`); a fixed shape's piece is found by `Redactor.Shapes` on the whole view; a sentence removed by meaning is its span in the view's `SentenceMap` (below). The spans of every applying rule are joined by `redact.Union`, so the result is the same in any order of the rules, and `redact.Join` makes the subject and the text that leave: each run of touching or overlapping spans, clipped to its part, becomes one `[removed]`, everything else kept byte for byte. The sender and the attachments are parts outside the view, removed whole by a rule that removes them, or that removes shapes or topics (choices 6 and 9).
4. **The connection's formulas** (consented scope, mailboxes, mail since, device, the vendor's search) on the message as it would leave: a `subject` or `body` condition reads the reduced subject and text, a condition on a part removed whole is Unknown (so a query does not match it), and a model's atom of a query is asked about the reduced text, under a view of its own (`inbox.TextView` named `r-` and the first 16 hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256 of what was removed: the union's spans, `redact.Version`, `redact.SplitVersion` and each meaning decision's map digest and sentences kept), so an answer about the original is never used for the reduced text and the reverse (ADR 0028's views). Evaluating a vendor's search on the original would let it probe what a rule removed ("does the text contain 555-1234?").
5. **The parts** the connection reads, as today, cut from what is left: a connection that does not read the subject gets no subject, whatever was removed from it; one that reads only the subject gets the subject as the union left it. Which parts a connection reads changes nothing of what was removed, nor of any decision.

Holding wins: a held message is never partly given. Rules apply to what leaves through connections and to the consent screen's preview; not to the person's own inbox and previews of a formula, as the default rule does not (ADR 0031).

## Fixed shapes

`internal/redact` (pure, `redact-is-pure`), at `redact.Version` 1, on the message's one view: its subject and the text a connection receives (`inbox.MessageText`, at most 256 KiB, decoded from base64 or quoted-printable, or taken from the HTML when there is no plain part). The HTML part never leaves a connection, so it is never redacted; should an HTML view, a raw header or an attachment ever leave by another route, it must be taken from the redacted view through the discloser, never redacted on its own. Attachments are not read: a rule that removes shapes or topics removes them whole, their names with them (choice 6). **The sender is not looked into either, so it is removed whole** by a rule that removes shapes or topics, as attachments are: a rule that says it removes email addresses must not let the sender's address leave, nor a phone number in its display name (choice 9, changed after the design review, finding 3). The rule's sentence says so ("Takes out email addresses, and the sender and attachments, which it does not read"), and so does the consent screen's list of rules.

**Reading the text.** Rune by rune, over valid UTF-8 (an invalid byte is a separator of its own, never part of a piece). A rune is a **digit** when Unicode says it is a decimal digit (`unicode.IsDigit`, any script: ASCII, fullwidth, Arabic-Indic, Devanagari…). Default-ignorable runes, as Unicode's `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point` property says (`Cf` such as U+200B to U+200D, U+2060, U+FEFF and U+00AD; `Other_Default_Ignorable_Code_Point` such as the combining grapheme joiner, the Hangul fillers U+115F, U+1160, U+3164 and U+FFA0 and the Khmer inherent vowels U+17B4 and U+17B5; the variation selectors; less white space, at the toolchain's Unicode tables), are **transparent**: inside a piece they belong to it, and they never end one (pull request 2's code review, finding 1). The at sign is any rune whose NFKC form is `@` (`@`, U+FF20, U+FE6B). Removal works on the original text's spans, never on a normalised copy.

| Kind | What is a piece |
|---|---|
| `email_address` | A run of runes that are not white space, controls or ``<>()[]",;:`` (an apostrophe and a backtick are, as in `o'connor@example.com`), of quoted local parts (a `"` to the `"` that closes it on its line, a backslash escaping the rune after it, followed by an at sign or a dot: `"private person"@example.com`) and, right after an at sign, of domain literals (a `[`, then runes that are not white space, `[`, `]` or a backslash, one of them a digit or `:`, and the `]` if there is one: `alice@[192.0.2.1]`, `bob@[IPv6:2001:db8::1]`), that holds an at sign with at least one rune before it and, after it, a domain literal or at least two labels joined by `.`; an at sign right after a `"` that opens no quoted local part takes the run back to the `"` before it on its line, or the line's start; the run's trailing `.`, `,`, `!`, `?`, `)` and `:` are not part of it (quoted local parts, domain literals and apostrophes: pull request 2's code review, finding 2) |
| `phone_number` | A **digit group** of 7 digits or more |
| `card_number` | A digit group of 12 digits or more (no Luhn check: a number that fails it is still removed) |
| `account_number` | A digit group of 8 digits or more; or an IBAN: two uppercase letters, two digits, then 11 to 30 uppercase letters and digits, in groups of up to four joined by single spaces |
| `numeric_code` | A digit group of 4 digits or more |
| `link` | A run of non-white-space runes that starts with a scheme (a letter, then letters, digits, `+`, `.`, `-`) and `://`, or with `mailto:` or `tel:`, or with `www.`; or two or more labels joined by `.` whose last is under an ICANN suffix of the public suffix list (`golang.org/x/net/publicsuffix`, compiled into the image), with whatever follows it to the run's end when that starts with `/`, `?`, `#` or `:` (a path, a query, a fragment or a port); the same trailing punctuation left out |

A **digit group** is a maximal sequence of digits in which any two consecutive digits are separated by at most two separator runes, a separator being a space of any width (`Zs`), a dash (`Pd`, `-`), `.`, `,`, `/`, `(` or `)`; with a `+` or `(` just before its first digit and a `)` just after its last included. Its count is its digits. **A kind has a least count and no most** (finding 2): a group at or above it is removed whole however long it is, so a code, a card or a phone number written next to another number (`Code 123456 2026-11-20` is one group of 14 digits; `4111 1111 1111 1111 2026` one of 20) is never let out because the two together are longer than the kind. The kinds of digits differ only in their least count, and the over-removal is said in the editor's words (below).

**Every piece becomes `[removed]`** (`contracts.RemovedMarker`), one marker for each run of pieces that touch or overlap, whatever their kinds and whichever rules removed them (`redact.Join`); a piece of several kinds is counted once, under the first in `contracts.ShapeKinds`. The marker holds no digit, at sign or dot, so redacting twice changes nothing (U9).

**False positives, on purpose.** Fail closed means over-removing: dates (`2026-11-20` is eight digits), years (`2026`), amounts (`1.299,00`), room and order numbers, version numbers, numbers written next to one another (a code and a date one space apart are one group), and words joined by a dot under a real suffix (`hello.world`) are removed by the kinds that match them, and the rule editor says so beside each kind ("every number of 4 digits or more, years, amounts and numbers written next to it included"; "every number of 7 digits or more, with whatever numbers are written next to it"). **False negatives, stated**: an address or a number written out in words ("five five five…", "ana at example dot com"), a domain with no dot or under a suffix not in the list, a number split by more than two separators, a number whose digits carry combining marks (a mark such as U+0301 after a digit is neither a digit, a separator nor transparent, so `5́5́5…` splits into groups below the kind's least; pull request 2's code review notes, kept as a false negative and tested by pull request 5), a lowercase IBAN's letters (its digits are still an account number), the words between an opening `"` with no closing `"` on its line and the address after them (the address is still removed; pull request 2's code review notes), anything inside an attachment or the sender (removed whole instead) or an image. A removal by meaning ("a phone number", "a home address") covers what a shape cannot, at the model's cost. The golden cases are `internal/redact/testdata/shapes.jsonl` (committed, U8); pull request 2 adds fuzz targets for every function.

**Bounds.** One pass over the text, each rune looked at a bounded number of times (the longest separator run is two, the IBAN's groups four): linear in the text, 256 KiB at most, measured by a benchmark and bounded by U9's adversarial inputs. So that redacting twice changes nothing (U9), `Fixed.Shapes` reads the text again with what it found standing as one marker each (at most four more passes, `rescansMax`): a removal can make a word readable (`abc.de5551234567` is a domain once its number is a marker, as it is once joined), and what it then finds is removed too.

## Removing by meaning

**On the one view** (finding 1). Meaning is decided on the message's one view (`redact.NewView`, "The order"), never on what a connection reads or what other rules left: so one rule's decision about one message numbers the same sentences for every connection, and next to any other rule, and changing, adding or deleting another rule never shifts its numbers.

**The split.** `Redactor.Sentences` (`redact.Fixed` in pull request 2, at `redact.SplitVersion` 1) gives the view's `redact.SentenceMap`: its sentences as spans of the view, in order, apart, never empty or white space alone. A sentence ends after `.`, `!`, `?`, `…`, `;` or `؟` followed by white space or the end; after `。`, `！`, `？` or `｡` whatever follows (the scripts that use them put no space after them, so waiting for one would make a paragraph one sentence); and at every line break (`\n`, `\r`, `\r\n`, `\v`, `\f`, U+0085, U+2028, U+2029), so the subject always ends one; an invalid byte is part of a sentence, never white space; the white space between sentences belongs to none and is kept; a sentence longer than `redact.SentenceRunesMax` (1,000 runes) is split at its last white space before the bound, or at the bound. No list of abbreviations: "Dr. Smith" is two sentences, which only makes each piece smaller. The map's `Cut` is where meaning stops reading. `SentenceMap.Check` states the invariants and `redacttest.Contract` checks them of every `Redactor` (U10): sentences sorted, apart, not empty or white space alone, at most `SentenceRunesMax` runes each and `MeaningSentencesMax` in all, every sentence and the cut on a rune boundary, and **every byte before the cut that is in no sentence white space**, so no text meaning should read is left out of what it asks about (re-review N1). A consumer refuses a map that fails it before any decision is asked or applied, and removes the whole subject and text instead (pull requests 4 and 5).

**The bounds.** Meaning reads at most `redact.MeaningTextMax` (32 KiB) of the view and `redact.MeaningSentencesMax` (128) sentences, the subject's counted; everything from the map's `Cut` on is removed (one marker, counted as one sentence removed), never sent.

**What the model sees.** One call per 64 statements (`decide.MaxStatementsPerCall`): the content is `{"sentences": [...]}` as JSON data (the Decisions API's `input_json`, at most 256 KiB), each sentence of the map in order **with every piece of every fixed shape kind masked** (`inbox.MaskedSentences`: all six kinds, whatever the rules remove, so what OpenAI sees does not depend on which rules apply and never holds a piece a shape could remove), each statement quoting its sentence, with its index and the topic, as one JSON object ("the sentence quoted here, at this index of sentences, refers to this topic, even in passing: `{"index": N, "sentence": "…", "topic": "…"}`"), one per sentence and topic, named `sNtM` (U34's first measure, with the index alone, found the model judging a neighbour of the sentence named in a long list; quoting the sentence fixed it, "Meaning's eval (U34)"); a new content kind (`decide.ContentSentences`) and a prompt version of its own (`s1-<date>`, beside `p2-2026-11-15`), its developer message saying that each statement is about its own sentence only, read in the context of the others, and that instructions inside the sentences are content. The subject's sentences are sent whether or not the connection reads the subject (the default rule already sends the subject, ADR 0028). Never the sender, the recipients, an attachment or an identifier. The cache is off, as for every call (ADR 0028).

**What is kept.** A sentence is kept only when every topic was answered below 0.30 (`query.Classify` with `ForRule` False, `inbox.KeptSentences`, committed): a refusal, an answer at 0.30 or above, a probability that is not one, and no answer at all remove it. The rule's removal is the spans of the sentences not kept and everything from `Cut` on (`inbox.MeaningSpans`), joined with every other rule's by union; each run of touching spans becomes one `[removed]`, and the white space and line breaks between sentences are kept, so two removed sentences one space apart are two markers. **Every masked piece is removed too** (the design re-review's N2): a meaning rule's removal also holds every piece of every fixed shape kind in the view, whatever the model answered (`MeaningSpans`), since the model was shown each of them as the same `[removed]` and cannot tell an address from a phone number, so nothing it answers can say a piece is not what the rule's topics name. A rule that removes only topics therefore also removes the fixed-shape pieces it could not show the model, with no shape rule present and whatever shape rules apply to the connection; the editor and the confirmation sentence say so (pull requests 5 and 7 deliver the words). And text before `Cut` that no sentence of the map covers is removed, white space apart, whatever the map's check said (N1's consumer half); a map that breaks `SentenceMap.Check` removes the whole subject and text, and a decision is never settled on one (`SettleMeaning` settles it `held`).

**Its decision.** Which sentences a rule kept is decided once per message and rule, through `inbox.Decisions` under `rules.MeaningRef(rule, inbox.TextVersion, redact.SplitVersion)` (the rule's version, the extractor's and the splitter's versions), apart from the rule's own applicability: begun before the model is asked, settled `cleared` with `Settlement.Meaning`, an `inbox.MeaningKept` (the map's digest, `SentenceMap.Digest`, over the view's bytes, its spans and its cut; and the sentences kept, at most 128 bits, `MeaningKept.Encode`), and the compact evidence (for each topic, the highest probability among the sentences kept: the closest call, with the model, prompt version and canary days), or `held`. **A stored decision is applied only to the map it numbered** (`MeaningKept.For`): a decision whose digest or count is not the message's view's now, a `held` one, one recovered `held`, a `cleared` one without its sentences, or none that can be made, removes the whole subject and text (`MeaningSpans` with nothing kept); a bitmap is never read against other sentences. The digest cannot differ for one message under one `TextVersion` and `SplitVersion` unless the extraction or the split is not what its version says, so a mismatch is a bug made safe, not a path. As `SettleAfterAsk` does for a hold (ADR 0031, R2), **an answer received is never forgotten**: an ask that got some answers and not others settles `cleared` with only the sentences answered low for every topic kept; one that got no answer, with positive evidence that the model answered nothing (the guard, every call failed before an answer), is abandoned, and this request removes the whole subject and text, undecided; one whose settlement is lost is recovered `held` after `PendingGrace`. The evidence fits `DecisionEvidenceMax` and `DecisionEvidenceBytesMax`, and a Pending of a meaning decision reserves `inbox.MeaningKeptBytesMax` more in its block, so a settlement always fits. The blocks keep it (pull request 4): a cleared meaning entry holds the map's digest and `MeaningKept.Encode` in two fields (`mm`, `mk`); a Pending of a meaning decision reserves `MeaningKeptBytesMax` and those fields' names beside `DecisionEvidenceBytesMax`, so a block of meaning Pending each settled at its largest stays within `DecisionsBytesMax`; a settlement whose sentences are past their bounds (no digest or one past 64 bytes, more than 128 sentences), or that gives sentences to a decision that is not a meaning one, changes nothing (the Pending stays, and is recovered `held`); `held` keeps no sentences; an entry whose two fields do not read back is answered `cleared` with none, which removes the whole subject and text. `inbox.SettleMeaning` is how the discloser settles one from what `AskSentences` answered, as `SettleAfterAsk` is for a hold.

**Cost bounds and the spend guard.** Every call is reserved before it is sent, through the same guard, in the account's day share (ADR 0028): its estimate is the guard's upper bound (1,000 tokens, 400 a statement, one a byte of content). A limit reached before the rule's statements are all asked leaves the rest unasked, so those sentences are removed (or, with nothing asked, the whole subject and text), never sent. See "Cost" for the numbers.

## Decisions that cannot be undone

Every model-dependent decision of a rule goes through `inbox.Decisions` and its one implementation, `inbox.BlockDecisions` (ADR 0031), unchanged but for the field above:

- a rule that holds: `(message, rule ref)`, exactly as the default rule;
- a rule that removes: whether it applies, the same way under its own reference; and which sentences its topics keep, under its `MeaningRef`, with the digest of the map they are numbered in;
- a rule with `ModelOff`: nothing begun; its model atoms are Unknown, so it holds, or removes the whole subject and text.

So whatever whoever writes the table does to kept answers, a message a rule held stays held, and a sentence a removal removed stays removed, for as long as the message exists, through every connection and whatever other rules come and go; a decision lost, or one that does not match its map, fails closed. A mailbox with `k` model-dependent rules uses up to `k` (plus one per meaning rule) decisions a message: a day's 28,800 decisions (`decisions_per_day`) then decide 28,800 / `k` messages before the rest is held, undecided, which with the bounds above is still at least 2,400 a mailbox a day. `/status` keeps saying `decisions: strict`.

## Changes, and what they apply to

- **A new rule, a rule turned on, a scope widened, the default turned on**: applies to every request that begins after the save, through the enclave that saved it at once and through the other within `accountrules.Fresh` (five seconds), to every message, old mail included.
- **A rule's criteria, effect or removal changed**: a new version; its decisions are made afresh (ADR 0031, H20), the old version's never used and dropped with their messages.
- **A rule turned off and on again, renamed, or its scope changed**: the same version, and its final decisions still stand: a decision depends on the message and the rule, not on who reads.
- **A rule deleted, or the default turned off**: from the next request, as above; mail that a rule held may then leave. Every such change needs a fresh proof, like every other change (choice 4), and is recorded in a `rules` receipt.
- **Mail already given** cannot be recalled; receipts say which rules and versions applied to each answer.
- **An answer in flight** (a page or a read already being prepared, at most a request's minute) finishes under the rules it began with; it is not cut as a revocation cuts one (choice 12). This is a window the design chooses, not immediate revocation, and every confirmation of a change says so in fixed words ("Applies to every request that begins after you save; an answer already being prepared can still finish").
- **Delivery's notices** (ADR 0039) carry no mail: the vendor fetches through the same checks, which apply the rules in force when it fetches.

## The seam with delivery

ADR 0039 decides whether to send a notice for a new message or a schedule; this record states what it must call so rules apply before its matching, without designing delivery:

1. The rules of the connection: `accountrules.Source.Applying(ctx, account, clientID)` (committed); an error sends no notice and is tried again later.
2. Whether the message would be given: `inbox.Discloser.Message` with the `inbox.Disclosure` the connections service builds for that connection (`connections.Service.Disclosure`, which pull request 5 adds, so delivery and a read build it in one place: the rules, the connection's formulas and parts, the account, the model's gate), notifying only on an `Outcome` with `Disclosed`. 3. Decisions it begins are the same records a later read meets (ADR 0031, H22).

`accountrulestest.Fake` and its `Contract` are what delivery's tests use for the first; `disclosetest` and the discloser's fakes for the second.

## Receipts

- A message given with details removed: `receipts.Delivered.Removed`, one entry per rule that removed something: the rule's reference and version, `contracts.RemovedSummary` (parts removed whole, pieces by shape kind, sentences removed of how many, whether the whole text was removed and why, attachments removed) and the evidence its decisions rest on. **Never the removed content**, and the senders and subject kept in the receipt are those that left (the reduced subject, never the original): a receipt keeps what left, not a copy of what did not.
- A message held by a person's rule: `Held` with that rule's reference, as the default's.
- A change of rules: a new kind, `rules` (receipts' version 3): which rule, its version before and after, what changed (made, criteria, effect or removal, scope, on, off, deleted, default on or off, notice agreed), the draft's identifier and its criteria's canonical identifiers, the notice, and the record's version the change was asked at; never the words. **Its order against the record's write** (the review's other constraints): the receipt is written first and asserts that the person **asked** for this change at that version, with a fresh proof; then the record is written at that version. A receipt that cannot be written makes nothing. A write that loses after its receipt (another change came first, or the enclave stopped) leaves a receipt of a change that was not made, which says more than happened, never less: the record's history at the version after says whether it was made, and the person's next read shows the rules as they are. A change made without its receipt cannot happen. This is the order a consent's renewal already has (ADR 0030, finding 6); nothing here is a transaction across the two records. U7 tests both interleavings.
- `Receipt.Rules` keeps naming each rule and version a request applied.

**The receipt's size** (finding 5). A page's receipt is built by `receipts.Page`, which counts the whole document as `Write` will store it and takes no message that would put it past `MaxDocumentBytes` (256 KiB): the page ends before that message and its cursor continues from the last one taken, so nothing is skipped and no evidence is dropped. `Delivered.Removed`, with every rule's summary and all its evidence, is part of what `Page.Add` counts, as everything else of a message is. With 32 model conditions over the account's rules and their answers kept per message, a page can hold far fewer than 50 messages; it ends early rather than drop evidence. A message that cannot fit even on an empty page would today end the page with no message and a cursor that never moves; pull request 5 makes that case a refusal (`unavailable`, recorded as the connections service records one), nothing given, never an empty page that loops (U24). One message's own entry is bounded (16 rules, each decision's evidence at most `DecisionEvidenceBytesMax`, 2 KiB, a summary of fixed fields), about 40 KiB at most, so this is a guard, not a path. Deduplicating evidence within a receipt was weighed and left out: it is a change of the receipts' format for a bound the page already keeps. The activity screen and the CSV (ADR 0041) show `Removed` and `rules` receipts; their words are that record's.

## Threat model

- **Whoever writes the table** cannot read, change or make a rule (sealed, bound to its name), cannot put back an earlier record (strict reads, refused) and cannot hide a change without every read of the account failing (a fork, a loss: nothing leaves). They can still delete a kept answer, which changes no decision once begun.
- **The host** sees ciphertext and the timing and size of writes; never the rules, the words or a sentence.
- **OpenAI** sees the person's words (under the rules' notice, `store: false`), and, for rules that remove by meaning, the sentences of the message's subject and text with every fixed shape masked, whichever parts a connection reads; never the sender, recipients or attachments. Under ordinary retention until zero data retention is approved (ADR 0020).
- **The model proposing a rule** cannot make anything apply: its answer is checked as a vendor's formula, written back by the system as a sentence, and saved only by the person with a fresh proof. A proposal that holds or removes less than meant is what the person confirms by its sentence; the stated risk is that the person does not read it.
- **The sender of a message** can write text to push the model toward keeping a sentence (ADR 0020's accepted risk, bounded to that message, and not removed by the schema or the 0.30 threshold); the fixed shapes, which need no model, cannot be pushed, and their removal joins meaning's whatever it answers. A sender's own address and name never leave under a rule that removes shapes or topics.
- **A vendor** cannot probe what was removed (its search reads the reduced message), is told nothing of holds, and sees `[removed]` where pieces were taken out. It cannot learn which rules exist beyond what the markers and absent parts show.
- **A stolen session** cannot weaken rules without a fresh proof; every change is in a chained receipt the person can check.

docs/threat-model.md has these since pull request 8 composed them ("A person's own rules").

## Cost

- **Words**: one Responses API call a proposal, at most 2,000 output tokens (reasoning included), priced at the provider's page as pull request 3 read it on 2026-11-20 and compiled with the prompt (`gpt-6-luna`: $0.10 input, $0.01 cached input and $0.50 output per million tokens; past 272K input tokens $0.20, $0.02 and $0.75). Each call is reserved at its bound, one token a byte of the developer message (4,781 bytes), the schema (1,492) and the words plus 500, and the 2,000 output tokens: about $0.0017; U33 measured $0.000205 a call. Bounded by 30 calls an account a day per enclave and the account's 0.50 USD share.
- **Holds**: a person's rule's atoms join the default's and the query's in the same call for a message (at most 32 of the rules' and 8 of a query's in 64), so a message costs about what it costs now: about $0.00013 (ADR 0028's measure), a little more per statement.
- **Meaning**: a typical message (3 KiB of text, 25 sentences, one topic) is one call of 25 statements: about 3,000 real tokens, $0.0003, reserved at its upper bound of about 17,000 ($0.0017: each statement quotes its sentence, one token a byte more). The worst (32 KiB, 128 sentences, 3 topics: 384 statements) is 6 calls, reserved at about 456,000 tokens ($0.046) and costing perhaps half. Measured (U34): 58 synthetic messages, 374 sentences, 63 calls, the 128-sentence message's six included, for $0.0185, about $0.0003 a message. A page of 50 typical messages with one meaning rule: about $0.015.
- **Storage**: one strict record per account and a commit per change (a handful a person); decisions within ADR 0031's blocks.
- **Spend guard**: unchanged: 50 USD a month, the alarm at 10 a day, 0.50 an account a day an enclave. A connection that reads past an account's share gets its meaning removals as whole texts removed, and its holds held, until the next day.

## What it guarantees, and what it does not

It guarantees: a rule saved applies to every request that begins five seconds later, through either enclave, and an earlier copy cannot undo it; a model-dependent decision of any rule, once begun, is never undone by the storage, is the same for every connection and next to any other rule, and is never applied to other sentences than those it numbered; a decision that cannot be made or used holds, or removes the whole subject and text; a vendor's search never reads what a rule removed; a rule that removes shapes or topics lets out no sender and no attachment; nothing of the account goes to OpenAI with the words, and no piece a fixed shape matches goes with a sentence; a rule never asks the model without the consent in force; every change of rules is in a chained receipt written before it, and every removal in the receipt of the answer it was part of. It does not guarantee: that a model's first answer is right (a sentence that mentions a topic in words the model misses leaves); that fixed shapes catch data written in words or inside attachments (which are removed whole instead); that the person reads the sentence before saving; that an answer already being prepared stops; that mail already given comes back.

## Words, as built (pull request 3, 2026-11-20)

**What is sent** (`rulewords.Request`, every byte named by `rulewords.PromptVersion` `w4-2026-11-20`, pinned by `TestU11ThePromptIsPinned`): `POST https://api.openai.com/v1/responses` with the model `gpt-6-luna`, the developer message `rulewords.Developer`, the words as the user message's one `input_text` part, `text.format` the strict JSON schema `rule` (`rulewords.Schema`, made from the contract's types), `reasoning.effort` `medium`, `max_output_tokens` 2,000, `store: false` and `prompt_cache_options.mode` `explicit` (the prompt cache off, as every call of ADR 0028); no tools, no previous response, no metadata. The answer's schema lists `possible` (false when the words describe no rule; nothing is then proposed), `name`, `effect`, `criteria` (each a formula's groups and conditions, its variables every one of the language's but `mailbox`, whose values the model never knows, and its operators all nine) and `removal` (null, or `parts`, `shapes` and `topics`); every object strict and every member required.

**The answer's check** (`rulewords.Read`): exactly the schema's JSON, a member twice or one not listed refused, and a null refused everywhere but a hold's removal and the value of `exists` (never read as its zero value); a name of 1 to 60 characters; 1 to 8 criteria, each canonical by `query.Canonicalise` and never "never", at most 8 conditions a model decides over them; hold with removal null, or remove with known parts and shapes each once and at most 3 topics the language accepts; no control or format character and no U+FFFD in a name, a value or a topic; no topic and no value but a date or a size that holds a disjunction joining alternatives (the words "or", "o", "ou", "oder", "или", or "或" or "または" anywhere), a question mark (`?`, `¿`, `؟`, `？`), JSON's punctuation (`{`, `}`, `[`, `]`, `"`, `\`, `:`, `,`) or a corner bracket, or letters of more than one script (Chinese, Japanese and Korean counted as one; letters of no one script ignored), so that an answer joining alternatives or broken inside the schema is unusable instead of protecting less (`single`, `TestU12AJoinedOrBrokenTopicIsUnusable`, built from U33's answers; the orchestrator's ruling of 2026-11-20: unusable is acceptable, protecting less is not); within `MaxRuleBytes`. Anything else, a refusal and an answer cut at `max_output_tokens` are `rule_words_unusable`. The draft is for every connection and on, its criteria and topics canonical; the account's own check (`accountrules.Rules.Check`, its mailboxes and bounds, the sentence) stays the route's (pull request 6).

**The call** (`decisions.Service.Write`, the `rulewords.Writer`; the client `internal/rulewords/openai`, built by `Config.NewWriter` with the service's credential): words that are not 1 to 1,000 characters of text are refused before anything is counted; the account's 30 calls of the day in this enclave are counted next (`too_many_requests` past them); then the key, one of the enclave's slots, and the reservation at its bound in the account's share through the same guard as `Ask`; one attempt, waited for at most 30 seconds, never tried again; settled once at what the answer reports (input, cached input and output tokens), at the bound when it reports nothing or failed after it may have been sent, at nothing when it was not sent. A refused key and the provider's limit stop the guard as `Ask`'s do; an answer naming another model is `model_unavailable`. Nothing of the words or the account is logged.

**U33, the words' eval** (`internal/rulewords/openai/eval_test.go`, build tag `wordseval`, on `internal/rulewords/testdata/words.jsonl`: 74 synthetic phrasings, 41 in English, 30 in Spanish, one in French, one in Portuguese and one mixed, 4 of them asking for no rule, two of them hostile, six of them naming alternatives, any one of which is enough). **The gate is met** with `w4-2026-11-20` and `Read`'s refusal of joined and broken topics and values, in both of two runs on 2026-11-20, each under a cap of 2 USD with the eval project's key. `w4` is `w2`'s developer message (its "Alternatives" paragraph and "no punctuation at its end", without `w3`'s list of joining words) with `w3`'s sentence that "only share" some parts removes every other part, the sender included (the developer message less that sentence is `w2`'s byte for byte). ## Disclosure, as built (pull request 5, 2026-11-20)

**The discloser** (`internal/inbox/removal.go` beside `discloser.go`). The first pass reads a message's sources while the formulas, a rule that holds, or a rule that removes still depend on them, and reads its text (the entry's, or the raw message's under the page's text reads, ending the page before it when they are spent) whenever a rule that removes from the subject or the text may apply; a message the formulas are False on is kept when a rule that removes may apply, since the formulas are read on what it leaves, never on the original (otherwise "the messages missing from `not contains 555`" would tell which hold 555). The second pass begins and settles every rule's applicability as before, the rules that hold before those that remove; a rule that holds wins, and nothing of a held message is removed or asked for meaning. Then, for each message no rule holds: every rule that removes and applies (True or Unknown without the model, `ModelOff`, the request's model off, or a decision that is not `Cleared`) gives spans of the one `View`: a part removed whole is its span; shapes are `Redactor.Shapes` on the whole view; topics are a meaning decision under `MeaningRef`, begun per rule and day before anything is asked, asked once per message and rule with `MaskedSentences`, settled by `SettleMeaning` (made even once the request ended), and applied only through `MeaningKept.For`, `MeaningSpans` adding every masked piece (N2) and any uncovered text before the cut (N1). They are joined by `redact.Union` and cut by `redact.Join`; a rule removing shapes or topics also removes the sender (`VendorMessage.From` empty) and the attachments (none listed, a download `not_found`).

**Whole text, and why.** The whole subject and text are removed, with `RemovedSummary.Whole` and `Why`, when a removal by meaning cannot use a decision: `undecided` for the model off for the rule or the request, a text that could not be read (no meaning decision is ever begun on a view without its text, which a later read would not share), a map that breaks `SentenceMap.Check` (refused before any decision is begun or asked: N1's consumer half, `TestN1AnInvalidSentenceMapRemovesTheWholeSubjectAndText`), blocks that cannot be read or written, a settlement lost, nothing answered (abandoned, decided by a later request), or a `Cleared` decision whose map is not the view's; `decided` for one settled `Held`; `recovered` for one recovered `Held` after `PendingGrace`. A read whose text a removal was to read and could not is `not_found`.

**The formulas on what is left.** The reduced subject and text replace the original's in the formulas' `query.Message`; a condition on a part removed whole (the subject, the text, the sender with its domain and the signatures' domains, the attachments) is Unknown and never read (`evalHiding`, which evaluates each group without those conditions and makes a group that held one Unknown where it would be False), and a model's atom on such a part is never asked. The formulas' other model atoms are asked of the reduced subject and text under `TextView{Name: "r-" + 16 hexadecimal digits}`, the SHA-256 of `redact.Version`, `SplitVersion`, the union's spans, the parts hidden and each meaning rule's map digest and sentences kept, so a kept answer about the original is never used for the reduced text nor the reverse (U21). ## Options considered

- **One record per rule**: changes of two rules never conflict, but reading an account's rules is one strict read per rule for every request; one record per account is one read, cached five seconds.
- **Rules inside the account's record**: one record fewer, but every sign-in and setting would write under the rules' version and the record's bound.
- **Scope by connection identifier**: exact, but a replacement or a re-connection makes a new identifier, and an `only` rule would silently stop covering the app; rewriting the rules' record in the same commit as the connection's is not something the store can do.
- **Applying only rules made before a connection**: simpler to explain for `only`, but `all` must cover connections made later, or every new app would leave every rule behind.
- **Words to a formula with the Decisions API**: it answers probabilities, not formulas.
- **Words without a model** (a keyword parser): no OpenAI, but "my home address except for Amazon" cannot be parsed reliably, and ADR 0019 promised words.
- **Showing the model's own explanation**: friendlier, but its words could claim what the formula does not do; the system's sentence is the only description.
- **Changing the model's notice instead of a rules' notice**: one notice, but every connection would get no mail until renewed (ADR 0030, finding 18), for consent that concerns rules only.
- **Evaluating the query on the original**: one view fewer, but a vendor's search would probe what a rule removed.
- **Meaning decided on the text a connection reads, after the other rules' shapes** (the design before its review): the model never saw a shape's piece, but one decision's sentence numbers then depended on the connection's parts and on other rules, so a bitmap could be read against other sentences (finding 1). One view with every shape masked keeps the first property without the second.
- **A meaning decision per view a connection reads**: each exact for its input, but a change of another rule would need a protocol to keep the earlier decisions' removed spans removed; one view needs none.
- **Sending the original sentences to the model, shapes unmasked**: simpler, but OpenAI would then see numbers and addresses a person's rules remove, which the design never allowed.
- **Removing before holding**: a hold's criteria would read a reduced message, and a rule meant to hold a message mentioning a number would miss it after another rule removed the number.
- **Regular expressions for shapes**: Go's are linear, but the digit group's separators, the transparent runes and Unicode digits are clearer and testable as one pass; the cases pin the behaviour either way.
- **Luhn for cards, a calendar for dates**: fewer false positives, at the cost of letting through a number that only looks invalid; fail closed is to remove.
- **A typed marker** (`[phone number removed]`): more useful to an agent, and it tells which kinds a person removes; one marker says only that something was removed.
- **Leaving attachments with a warning**: more useful, but a phone number inside a PDF would leave under a rule that says it removes phone numbers.
- **One call per sentence**: each sentence judged alone, which loses context ("my address is below:" then the address), and about forty times the cost of one call with the sentences numbered.
- **A model that rewrites** (ADR 0020 rejected it): it could add or change words; removing whole sentences cannot.
- **Kept answers for each sentence statement** (the `Asker`'s): the decision's record already keeps the outcome rollback-resistant; kept answers per sentence would add an entry per statement with nothing gained.
- **Cutting answers in flight on a change**, as a revocation does: a lease read again while an answer is sent, for a change that takes effect within seconds anyway; left out (choice 12).
- **A fresh proof only for changes that weaken**: less friction, but a classifier of what weakens (a scope narrowed, criteria changed, a rule replaced) is a place to be wrong; every change is simpler and fails closed.

## Choices made for the owner (to confirm)

Each is the option that fails closed and is the simplest to build and test; the alternative is what reversing it would mean.

1. **One strict record per account, in epochs of 400 versions (the account's list of connections' policy), 16 rules at most.** Alternative: a record per rule, or more rules.
2. **Scope names the app (`client_id`), not the connection, matched exactly and shown by its host and document path**, so a replacement or reconnection of the same app keeps "all but" and "only", and an app that moves its document is a new one. Alternative: name connections, and let a reconnection start without the person's exceptions; or match by domain, which widens an exception to every app on it.
3. **A connection made later is covered by "all" and "all but", not by "only"**, and its consent screen says which rules apply. Alternative: ask the person, on the consent screen, whether each `only` rule should cover the new app.
4. **Every change of rules needs a fresh proof**, adding a rule included. Alternative: only changes that weaken.
5. **The words are kept nowhere**, so a rule is edited by its conditions afterwards. Alternative: keep them, sealed, in the rule, to edit in words later.
6. **A rule that removes shapes or topics removes the attachments too.** Alternative: leave them, saying beside the rule that attachments are not read.
7. **One fixed marker, `[removed]`, for every piece and sentence removed; the vendor is told nothing else.** Alternative: remove silently, or name the kind.
8. **Shapes over-remove**: a digit group is removed at or above a kind's least count, however long; dates, years, amounts and numbers written next to one another are numbers; any domain under an ICANN suffix is a link; no Luhn. Alternative: exceptions for dates, amounts and invalid card numbers, or a most count per kind (which the design review showed lets a code out when a date is written beside it).
9. **Shapes and meaning read the subject and the text, not the `From` field; a rule that removes shapes or topics removes the sender whole**, as it removes attachments (changed after the design review, finding 3; before, the sender left unless removed as a part). Alternative: look into the sender's address and display name with the shapes, which keeps the sender for rules that do not match it at the cost of a parser of display names; or leave the sender and say so in the rule's sentence and the consent screen.
10. **Meaning, on one view**: decided once per message and rule on the subject and the text together, whatever the connection reads, every shape masked in what the model sees; 0.30 is the threshold (the rules' band); 32 KiB, 128 sentences, 3 topics a rule, 4 meaning rules an account; past them, removed. Alternative: larger bounds at a larger cost, or the query's 0.80; or a decision per view a connection reads, which needs a rule for keeping earlier decisions' removed spans when another rule changes.
11. **A rules' notice of its own**, so no connection stops; a rule under an older notice asks nothing and holds. Alternative: a new version of the model's notice, which stops every connection until renewed.
12. **A change applies to requests that begin after it (five seconds through the other enclave, counted from when a cached read began); an answer being prepared is not cut, and the confirmation says so.** This is weaker than a connection's revocation, on purpose. Alternative: read the rules' version again while an answer is sent, as a revocation's lease is.
13. **The default rule is on or off, for every connection, and not editable.** Alternative: scope it, or let the person edit a copy.
14. **Holds before removals, the query on the reduced message.** Alternative: none simpler that keeps a vendor from probing.
15. **The words' model proposes criteria, effect and removal, never scope; a proposal is for every connection and on**, which the person changes before saving. Alternative: send the names of the account's connected apps to the model.
16. **A `rules` receipt for every change, written before the record** (it records what was asked; one of a change that lost says more than happened), which makes receipts' version 3. Alternative: rely on the record's history alone, or write the receipt after the record, which a crash could leave without one.
17. **Rules do not apply to the person's own inbox and previews of a formula**, as the default does not. Alternative: show the inbox as a connection would see it.
18. **Turning a rule off and on keeps its version and its decisions.** Alternative: a new version, deciding again.
19. **30 words calls an account a day in an enclave**, besides the spend share. Alternative: the share alone.

## Changed after the design review (2026-11-20)

The design review of this record at `6ead6a3` found one P1 and six P2 and listed other constraints; the orchestrator accepted all seven and chose, where the review offered options, the one that fails closed and is simplest. Each is answered here by number, and the sections above say the design as it now is.

1. **P1, a meaning decision's bitmap reused for other sentences.** Resolved by the design. Meaning is decided on one fixed view of the message (`redact.NewView`: its subject, a line break and its text), the same for every connection and whatever other rules apply, split once into a `redact.SentenceMap` whose sentences are spans of that view; every rule's removals (whole parts, shapes, meanings) are spans of the same view joined by `redact.Union`, and only then are the connection's parts cut ("The order", "Removing by meaning"). A meaning decision is keyed by `MeaningRef` (the message, the rule's version, `TextVersion`, `SplitVersion`) and keeps its map's digest with the sentences kept (`inbox.MeaningKept`); `MeaningKept.For` applies it only to the same map, and any mismatch removes the whole subject and text, never an old bitmap. The review's reproduction no longer has two inputs: the body-only and the subject-only connection meet one decision on one map, and the address is removed for both (`TestU18AMeaningDecisionIsBoundToItsMap`); two meaning rules in either order remove the same text (`TestU18TwoMeaningRulesInEitherOrder`); another rule's change cannot shift a decision's numbers, since no other rule's removal enters the view. What the model sees no longer depends on other rules either: every sentence with every shape of every kind masked (`MaskedSentences`), which keeps the earlier promise that the model never sees a piece a shape could remove. The rest of U18, U20 and U21's cases (two enclaves, the strict-store fake, scoped shape sets, another rule edited or deleted) are pull requests 4 and 5's, listed in their entries.
2. **P2, maximal numeric groups defeating removal.** Resolved by the design: a digit kind has a least count and no most, so a group at or above it is removed whole however long it is ("Fixed shapes"; `contracts.ShapeKind`'s comments; choice 8); the linear scanner is unchanged. The review's cases are golden cases now (U8), the property is in U9, and the editor says the over-removal beside each kind.
3. **P2, the sender left by "remove email addresses".** Resolved by the design: a rule that removes shapes or topics removes the sender whole, as it removes attachments (`rules.Removal.Sender`, `contracts.RuleRemoval`, choice 9 changed); its sentence and the consent screen say so. U15 and U20 test it through the API and MCP.
4. **P2, scope sentences by domain alone.** Resolved by the design: clients are still matched by exact `client_id` and are shown by `contracts.ClientShown` (the `client_id` without `https://`: host and document path) in `RuleCheck.For`, the new `RuleCheck.Clients` (`contracts.RuleClient`, with the client's own name labelled as the vendor's), the picker and the consent screen; a moved document is a new app for scope ("Scope", choice 2). `web/src/lib/api.ts` mirrors it (`RuleClient`, `clientShown`). U1 tests two documents on one host; U3, U25 and U32 extend it.
5. **P2, the receipt-size claim.** Resolved by the design: the claim that 50 messages always fit is gone; a page's receipt is built by the existing `receipts.Page`, counting `Delivered.Removed` and all its evidence, ending the page before a message that would not fit and continuing from the last one recorded; a message that cannot fit an empty page is refused, never answered as an empty page that loops ("Receipts"). U24 now asks for maximal-evidence fixtures, a smaller page, nothing skipped and that refusal.
6. **P2, the epoch threshold.** Resolved by the design: the record rolls over at 400 versions (`rules.RecordVersionsMax`, `rules.Rotate`), the account's list of connections' policy, the earlier epoch removed after the next is committed, so an epoch's history with its rollover and removal stays below `store.MaxHistoryReads`; the storage's bound is not raised ("The rules' record"). **The other reviewed constraints.**

- **The rules receipt against the record's write**: the receipt is written first and asserts the change asked at a version; a receipt that cannot be written makes nothing, and a write that loses leaves a receipt of a change not made, never a change without one ("Receipts"; `accountrules.Rules`' documentation). U7 tests both interleavings, on the fake now (`RulesContract`).
- **The stale cache**: a cached read's age counts from when the read began, and a failed refresh never extends it; past `Fresh`, nothing leaves until a read succeeds (`accountrules.Fresh`'s documentation, "The rules' record", U4).
- **An absent record**: the default alone only when the strict read says epoch 0 was never written; any error is `ErrUnavailable` ("The rules' record", U2, U29 on real strict histories, forks and a rollback after a restart).
- **An answer in flight**: a chosen window, weaker than a revocation; every confirmation says an answer already being prepared can finish ("Changes, and what they apply to", choice 12, U32).
- **Decoded and HTML-derived text, Unicode domain punctuation, attachment downloads and effective parts** in U8 and U14; no route around the discloser for HTML, raw headers or attachments ("Fixed shapes").
- **Prompt injection and a wrong first answer** stay ADR 0020's accepted risks; U34 adds cross-sentence context, quoted instructions, multilingual text, the cuts at 32 KiB and 1,000 runes and partial multi-call failures, as a release gate, not a proof.
- **Words-call refusals, account-wide gating, per-attempt reservations with output tokens and partial meaning results** are kept as designed; `rulewords.Writer`'s documentation states the reservation and the errors.
- **TypeScript parity**: the Go U1 fixtures still assert Go's JSON; `clientShown` answers the Go cases now (`web/tests/lib/client-shown.test.ts`), and pull request 7 adds the web's decoding of the same fixtures (U32).
